### 头条：AI 帮忙找到 PHP 严重漏洞，攻击者抢在补丁前面

今天的头条，是一个跟大量网站都有关系的安全漏洞。

PHP，是一种做网站的编程语言，WordPress、Drupal 这些建站系统，都跑在它上面。周三，PHP 官方紧急发布了安全更新，修复了两个严重漏洞。攻击者不用登录，就可能在服务器上执行任意代码。官方的说法很直接，没有完整的缓解办法，唯一安全的选择，就是马上升级。GitHub 上的安全公告，给其中一个漏洞打了 9.2 分，满分是 10 分。

这次的漏洞被叫做 PHP2Shell。安全公司 Wiz 说，过去十年发布的每一个 PHP 版本，都至少受其中一个漏洞影响。

为什么说这是一条 AI 新闻？因为找到它的，是两支团队，各自独立，而且都用了 AI。

第一支，是安全公司 Calif，它参加了 OpenAI 发起的“修补地球”计划，用 AI 帮开源项目找漏洞。Calif 说，它 8 月 1 日找到第一个漏洞。OpenAI 说，Calif 用的是 GPT-5.6 Sol。第二支，是谷歌旗下的 Wiz，它说自己 8 月 19 日找到第一个漏洞，用的是自家工具，加上谷歌的 Gemini 4 Argon 和 OpenAI 的 GPT-5.6 Sol。

之后两个月，OpenAI 说，它牵头组织了维护者、研究人员和安全公司，一起准备修复。它还说，据它所知，是 AI 先于攻击者，找到了这些漏洞。

但问题来了。亚马逊的威胁情报团队说，它布置的诱饵服务器，在 10 月 17 日，就捕捉到一次利用这个漏洞的攻击尝试，比正式公开早了四天，攻击者怎么拿到细节的，目前不清楚。周三正式公开前大约 12 个小时，网上已经出现了攻击代码。亚马逊现在说，这个漏洞已经在被大范围利用，只是还不清楚，这些尝试有没有真的得手。还有至少一个欧洲国家的政府，为了保险，先把自己的网站下线了。

真正值得关注的，是两支团队，在同一个月，各自用 AI，找到了同一个藏了十年的漏洞。换句话说，找这种漏洞的门槛正在变低，对防守的人和攻击的人，都一样。OpenAI 自己也预计，接下来几个月，这类发现会大幅增加。

当然也要说清楚，验证漏洞、写攻击代码、写修复，大量工作还是人做的，说“AI 找到的”，是把过程简化了。到底有多少网站真的被攻破，现在也还不知道。

我的判断是，瓶颈已经变了。找漏洞越来越快，打补丁却没有跟上。PHP 8.1 及更老的版本，官方已经不再提供修复，还在用的，只能找服务商打补丁，或者升级。如果你的网站用 PHP，今天就升级。

### 第二条：微软披露，AI 智能体被用于大规模入侵

第二条，也是安全。周二我们讲过，有黑客用 AI 智能体，攻击了一千多个网站。周三，微软又公布了一起规模更大的。

微软说，一个它称为 Storm-0940 的组织，用一个叫 ARTEX 的开源渗透测试框架发动攻击。ARTEX 是一组 AI 智能体，可以接入任何模型，从侦察到窃取数据，一路自动跑下去。微软把这个组织描述为，与中国国家利益一致。这是微软的说法，我们没有看到中方的回应。

入口是一家软件供应商。攻击者控制了 ConnectWise 公司几个云应用的身份，其中包括它的云备份产品。客户之前已经给这些应用授过权，所以智能体登录客户的微软云账号，根本不需要用户本人登录。微软说，这些智能体尝试登录了 2250 多个租户，最快每分钟 2900 次。好几起案例里，从第一次接触到拿到管理员权限，不到三个小时。

微软也说，大多数尝试都失败了，问题也不在微软自己的系统。

我的判断是，AI 加快的，是进门之后的所有动作。但门本身，是个老问题，第三方应用手里的权限，给得太大了。

### 第三条：OpenAI 的模型，上了谷歌云

第三条，是个几年前很难想象的合作。

谷歌云宣布，OpenAI 的 GPT-6 Astra、GPT-6.1 Sol 和 GPT-6 Luna，开始以私密预览的形式，向部分企业客户开放。客户通过谷歌云的应用市场购买，统一开在谷歌的账单上，还能抵扣企业之前对谷歌云承诺的消费额度。谷歌说，客户的数据不会被拿去训练模型。目前没有公布价格，也没有正式上线日期，OpenAI 说，会在未来几周内全面开放，微软仍然是它的主要云合作伙伴。

谷歌说，自己现在是唯一一家，同时提供 Gemini、Claude 和 GPT 三大闭源模型的大型云厂商。这是谷歌自己的说法。

这件事真正重要的地方在于“抵扣额度”。大公司早就跟云厂商签了大额合同，现在这笔钱可以直接拿来用 GPT，换模型，就从搬家变成了改个设置。对微软来说，“主要合作伙伴”这几个字，分量没有以前重了。

### 第四条：Anthropic 推出会主动找你的智能体

周三，Anthropic 开放了 Claude Duet 的研究预览。它是一个独立的应用，Claude 在里面当一个主动型的智能体。每天早上给你发一份简报，每隔几个小时检查一遍你连接的应用，有需要你处理的事，它会主动来找你。它在自己的云端虚拟机上干活，你的电脑关了，它也能继续。

发消息、付款、签文件、删东西之前，它都会把具体操作给你看，等你确认。炒股、在账户之间转钱，就算你同意，它也不做。

目前只对美国的 Pro 和 Max 用户开放，要排队。不额外收费，但后台检查会消耗你的用量额度。Anthropic 自己也承认，它可能判断错哪些事情紧急。

我的判断是，这类产品，难的不是模型，而是它的打扰值不值得。这一点，Anthropic 自己也说还没做好。

### Main story — PHP2Shell: AI-assisted research finds two critical PHP flaws

On Wednesday the PHP project released four security updates: versions 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35. They fix two vulnerabilities that can let an attacker run code on a web server without logging in. PHP's own advisory is blunt. There are no complete mitigations, and updating is the only safe choice. The GitHub advisory rates the first one critical, nine point two out of ten.

Researchers are calling it PHP2Shell. Both bugs live in how PHP cleans up after errors, especially when a request runs out of memory. The security firm Wiz says every PHP version released in the last decade is affected by at least one of them, going back to version five point four. Proof-of-concept attacks worked against WordPress, Drupal and Joomla.

Here's what makes this an AI story. Two teams found these bugs separately, in the same month, and both used AI.

The first was Calif, a security firm OpenAI had invited into its Patch the Planet program. Calif's timeline says it found the first bug on August first. OpenAI says Calif found it using GPT-5.6 Sol, and that OpenAI then found more of the problem itself. The second team was Wiz, which is owned by Google. Wiz says it found its first bug on August nineteenth, using its own tooling together with Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-5.6 Sol. It reported to PHP on August twenty-fourth.

OpenAI says it then led a coalition of maintainers, researchers and security companies for two months to get the fix out in an orderly way. It says that, to its knowledge, frontier AI found these vulnerabilities before attackers did. It also published a step-by-step process it wants the industry to adopt for the next one, and a checker tool for server operators.

Now the catch. Amazon's threat intelligence team says its honeypots, decoy servers built to attract attackers, saw an attempt to exploit PHP2Shell on October seventeenth. That's four days before the public disclosure. Amazon doesn't know how the attacker got the details. On Wednesday, the first proof-of-concept exploits showed up around two in the morning UTC, about twelve hours before the embargo lifted at two in the afternoon. After the release, more exploits appeared within hours, from multiple actors. Amazon now says PHP2Shell is broadly exploited in the wild, though it's unclear whether those attempts succeeded on real systems. Palo Alto's Unit 42 says at least one European government took its website offline as a precaution.

So why does this matter? For months, labs have said their models are getting good at finding security bugs. This is the clearest case yet of that working on infrastructure almost everyone depends on. And it worked for defenders. The bug got found, fixed and coordinated.

But what most headlines are missing is the second team. If two groups with AI help find the same decade-old bug within three weeks, finding bugs like this is getting cheaper. That holds for everyone, including people who don't report what they find. OpenAI itself says it expects findings like this to increase sharply in the coming months.

The counterpoint: humans did a lot of the work here. They validated the bugs, built the exploits and wrote the fix, so "AI found it" compresses a lot. OpenAI's "before attackers did" claim is hard to check from outside. And we don't yet know how many sites were actually breached. Estimates of exposed servers vary by more than ten times, so I'm not going to quote one.

My read: the bottleneck has moved. Discovery is getting faster. Patching is not. PHP 8.1 and older don't get official fixes from the project, so anyone still on them needs a vendor patch or an upgrade. If you run PHP anywhere, update today. If you can't, take it offline or put it behind a firewall rule until you can.

What to watch next: confirmed breach reports, and whether other labs and maintainers sign on to OpenAI's coordination process, or argue for a different one.

### Microsoft: an AI agent framework in a state-aligned attack

On Tuesday we covered a different campaign, a hacker using AI agents against more than a thousand websites. On Wednesday, Microsoft published a bigger one.

Microsoft says a group it calls Storm-0940, which it describes as China-state aligned, used a tool called ARTEX. Microsoft calls it an open-source penetration testing framework that can use any AI model. It's a team of AI agents that runs an attack from reconnaissance to data theft. Microsoft says the group used it against hundreds of organizations since as early as September.

The way in was a software supplier. The group took control of the identities of several ConnectWise cloud apps, including its Cloud Backup product. Customers had already granted those apps access. So the agents could sign in to customer Microsoft 365 tenants without any user logging in. Microsoft says the agents tried to sign in to more than two thousand two hundred fifty tenants, at up to two thousand nine hundred attempts a minute. In several cases it took less than three hours to go from first targeting to admin-level access. In one example, a ConnectWise app gave itself the Global Administrator role.

Microsoft says most of the targeting failed, and that this wasn't a flaw in Microsoft's own systems. ConnectWise issued an advisory on October sixteenth. We didn't find a response from Chinese officials.

My read: the AI here speeds up everything after the door opens, like mapping the network, changing roles and planting persistence, at machine speed. The door itself is an old problem: third-party apps holding too much trust. If your response plan is measured in days, under three hours to admin is the number to think about.

### OpenAI's models come to Google Cloud

Next, a business story that would have been hard to imagine a couple of years ago. OpenAI's frontier models are now on Google Cloud.

Google says GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna are rolling out in private preview to select enterprise customers in its Model Garden. Customers buy them through Google Cloud Marketplace, on one Google invoice, and the spending counts against commitments they've already made to Google. Google's pitch is that it's now the only big cloud offering all three leading closed model families, Gemini, Claude and GPT. That's Google's claim, but it fits what we know.

This matters because of the existing commitments. Large companies have already pre-paid huge amounts to their cloud provider. Letting them spend that money on GPT makes switching models a setting, not a migration. For OpenAI, it's more distribution. For Microsoft, being the primary partner means less than it used to.

The catch: it's a private preview, with no prices yet, and some enterprise protections, like zero data retention, are still marked coming soon.

### Anthropic's Claude Duet: an agent that messages you first

So weigh this segment accordingly.

On Wednesday Anthropic opened a research preview of Claude Duet, a separate app where Claude works as what it calls a proactive agent. It sends a morning brief, checks your connected apps every few hours and messages you when something needs attention. It also keeps working in the background on its own virtual machine with a browser, so your laptop can be closed. It runs on Claude Opus 5.5 and connects to email, Slack, accounting and payment tools.

Before it sends a message, makes a payment, signs a document or deletes anything, it shows you the exact action and waits for approval. Some things are off-limits even with approval, like trading securities and moving money between accounts.

Access is limited. It's a waitlist for US Pro and Max subscribers, with no extra price, but background checks count against your usage limits. Anthropic says it may misjudge what's urgent, it's weaker at personal errands, and it can't show you its browser yet.

The comparison is Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots, which also come to you first. My read: the model isn't the hard part. Whether people keep a product like this depends on one thing, whether its interruptions are worth it. Anthropic admits that's the part it hasn't nailed yet.
