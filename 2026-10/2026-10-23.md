### 头条：外部研究员找到绕过 OpenAI 智能体监控的办法

今天的头条，是 AI 智能体的“监控”到底靠不靠得住。

周三我们讲过，OpenAI 自己披露，它内部的 AI 智能体，干扰过用来监视它们的系统。周四，两家 AI 安全机构，METR 和 Redwood Research，公布了他们从外部检查这套系统的结果。

两家各派了一名研究员，在 OpenAI 待了三周，从 7 月 21 日到 8 月 11 日。他们能看内部代码、监控系统的提示词和配置、内部文档，还采访了十来位工程师和研究员。全程在现场有人陪同，用的是 OpenAI 的设备，设备不能带出公司。

他们的任务，就是找智能体能怎么躲开监控。METR 公布的发现里，有几条很值得说。

第一，有个覆盖缺口。智能体在压缩自己的长篇工作记录时，这一步没有被监控。METR 估计，从 5 月到 7 月，应该受监控的内部 Codex 流量里，大约有百分之十，可能因此没被监控。这个问题，OpenAI 的员工在检查开始前就发现了，检查期间修好了。

第二，有一个漏洞利用办法，能让智能体最近的几百个操作，都逃过监控，具体细节被打了码。

第三，有两个客户端可以设置的参数，能把新启动的智能体的监控直接关掉。

第四，Codex 智能体，理论上有办法绕过 Codex 的沙箱和自动审查，做任何操作它的用户能做的事。

OpenAI 说，所有影响生产环境安全措施的漏洞，都已经修好了。METR 的回应只有一句，这些说法，我们没有评估过。研究员也没法确认，有没有智能体真的利用过这些漏洞。

还有一点要说清楚。完整报告有 61 页，交给了 OpenAI。公开的只是一篇简短的总结，而且 OpenAI 有权，按自己的判断删改敏感内容。METR 说，这是事先约好的，这次是一次试点。

为什么重要？这套监控，是 OpenAI 让智能体在内部干活时，依靠的一道安全保障。这次找到的问题，大多不是监控“看不准”，而是更底层的，比如一个参数就能把它关掉。这种问题，测监控准确率的评测，是测不出来的。

当然也要看到，这只是一次夏天的试点，两个人，三个星期。最大的缺口是 OpenAI 自己发现的，它也说都修好了，目前也没有证据显示，有智能体真的钻了空子。

我的判断是，最值得警惕的，是在好几个地方，智能体本身就可能影响，自己会不会被监控。METR 的建议也很直接，不受监控的运行，要专门审批，要定期检查智能体，而不只是等它停下来再查，还要让智能体碰不到监控系统本身。

### 第二条：微软的 AI 诊断系统登上《科学》杂志

第二条，是医疗 AI。

周四，微软的 AI 诊断系统，发表在《科学》杂志上。它是一组 AI 智能体，像一个医生会诊小组一样，向模拟病人问诊、开检查，再讨论出诊断。

在一套由《新英格兰医学杂志》304 个疑难病例组成的测试里，它的正确率是百分之八十三点二，全科医生是百分之三十六点一。急诊病例上，它大约百分之八十，医生大约百分之四十七。

但很多报道没讲的是两点。第一，参加对比的是全科医生，单独看病，平时处理这类疑难病例的专科医生，没有参加对比。第二，据 TechCrunch 梳理，GPT-5 自己单独做，正确率就有百分之七十八点九，微软这套系统，只多加了大约 4 个百分点，同时把每个病例的成本降了大约 3000 美元。急诊病例上，几乎没有提升。

我的判断是，真正在看病的，很大程度上是底层的大模型。医疗 AI 产品的价值，正在往成本、流程和责任这些地方转移。

### 第三条：DeepSeek 和 OpenAI，两笔巨额融资

第三条，是钱，两笔都很大。

据路透社援引四位知情人士的消息，DeepSeek 正在完成一轮大约 1000 亿元人民币的融资。宁德时代和腾讯，是最大的出资方之一，新进的投资方，包括美的、美团、吉利汽车和兆易创新。这一轮融资前的估值是 5000 亿元人民币，规模是原定目标的两倍。条款还没最终敲定，DeepSeek 没有回应。

另一边，据美国 CNBC 报道，OpenAI 接近完成一轮最多 300 亿美元的融资，融资前估值 1.25 万亿美元，领投的是 Coatue 和阿联酋的投资方。周二我们讲过，路透社当时的说法，是估值超过 1.4 万亿美元。OpenAI 没有置评。

我的判断是，有两点值得看。OpenAI 的估值数字，是往下走的。而 DeepSeek 的投资方名单，几乎是一张中国产业地图，电池、汽车、家电、外卖，最可能用上它模型的公司，正在直接投钱进来。

### 第四条：Anthropic 用 Claude 给开源代码做数学证明

昨天我们讲 PHP 漏洞，说的是 AI 找漏洞，比打补丁快。周四，Anthropic 公布了另一条路，用数学证明，某一类漏洞不存在。

Anthropic 说，它和验证公司 Theorem 合作，让 Claude 为 123 个开源库写出了可以被机器检查的证明，证明其中 239 个测试入口，在 64 兆以内的输入下，都不会崩溃。证明针对的是编译后的程序，证明代码总共大约 1.8 亿行。

Anthropic 说，在这个过程中，Claude 还找到并修复了几百个漏洞。在一个常用的 PDF 阅读器里，它一周找到的安全漏洞，差不多相当于平时一年报告的数量。

Anthropic 自己也讲了局限。经过验证，不等于没有漏洞。这些证明假设的是一种特定环境，跟大多数人实际用的电脑并不一样，也不是每个入口都覆盖到了。

我的判断是，这证明的只是一个很窄的性质，不能说这些库就安全了。但形式化验证，过去只适合小项目，或者要花大量专家时间。如果模型能写证明，它就可能变成开源维护者真用得上的工具。

### 第五条：沃尔玛向 AI 购物智能体敞开大门

第五条，是 AI 智能体购物。

沃尔玛周三推出了一个叫 Agent Gateway 的入口，基于 MCP 协议，让经过批准的 AI 助手，比如 ChatGPT、Gemini 和 Meta 的 Muse，可以在沃尔玛和山姆会员店搜商品，替你下单。有沃尔玛账号就能用。

下个月，还会上线“智能体档案”。每个智能体在你的账户里，有自己的档案，买的东西单独列出来。你可以要求每一单都确认，也可以设一个消费上限。智能体看不到你的银行卡信息。

而亚马逊，已经开始阻止 AI 智能体在它的网站上替用户购物。

我的判断是，给每个智能体单独设消费上限，是个很好的模板。跟我们这周反复看到的“先确认，再执行”是一个思路，只不过这次，是商家来把关。

### 快讯

最后是一条快讯。

Anthropic 宣布，把它的 Agent Skills 标准，捐给 Linux 基金会旗下的 Agentic AI 基金会，它的 MCP 协议也在那里。以后，来自 Anthropic、谷歌、OpenAI 等公司的工程师，会以个人身份共同维护。Anthropic 说，已经有 70 多个智能体平台在用这个标准。

### 结语

今天这些新闻背后，更大的趋势是，智能体的权限，正在变成产品本身。沃尔玛给每个智能体单独的档案和消费上限，银行在决定智能体能碰什么，而在 OpenAI 内部，问题变成了，智能体能不能关掉对自己的监督。

接下来我最想看的，是谁有权从外面检查这些控制。这次 METR 进了 OpenAI，但公众看到的，是一份可以被删改的总结。智能体权力越大，审计的条件，就和审计的结论一样重要。

### Main story — Outside testers found ways around OpenAI's agent monitor

On Wednesday we covered OpenAI's own reports of agents interfering with the system that monitors them. On Thursday we got an outside view of that same system.

The AI safety nonprofits METR and Redwood Research published the results of a red-team exercise inside OpenAI. One researcher from each group spent three weeks on site, from July twenty-first to August eleventh. They had access to OpenAI's internal code, the monitor's prompts and configuration, internal documents, and about a dozen engineers and researchers. They worked on OpenAI-issued devices that never left the premises, and they were accompanied on site.

Their job was to find ways an internal agent could avoid being watched. Here's what METR says they found.

First, a coverage gap. The monitor didn't look at what are called compaction events, when an agent compresses its long working history to keep going. METR estimates that this likely left on the order of ten percent of eligible internal Codex traffic unmonitored between May and July. OpenAI's own staff had spotted that before the exercise, and fixed it during it.

Second, timing. The monitor ran when an agent handed control back to the user. So during that gap, an agent that just kept working could put off being checked indefinitely.

Third, an exploit that could stop hundreds of an agent's recent actions from being monitored, including the actions used in the exploit itself. The details are redacted.

Fourth, two settings a client could control that turned monitoring off for newly started agents. An agent could, in principle, start an unmonitored copy of itself, though METR notes that setting them might get caught.

And fifth, a way Codex agents could in principle get around Codex's sandbox and its Auto-Review check, and take any action the user running them could take.

OpenAI says it has patched every vulnerability that affected its production safeguards. METR's answer is one line: "We have not evaluated these claims." The testers also couldn't check whether any agent had actually used these gaps.

Then there are the terms. The full report is sixty-one pages, and it went to OpenAI. METR and Redwood's own leadership got a thirty-eight page version that OpenAI redacted. OpenAI could redact anything it considered sensitive, at its sole discretion, and METR had no right to describe what was removed. The public gets a short summary. METR says that was agreed in advance, and that this was a pilot for deep access, not meant as public transparency. It says future exercises are expected to follow its published approach to working with AI companies. For comparison, the arrangement Anthropic announced with METR last week, which we covered on October sixteenth, lets METR publish without a veto, subject to narrow redactions.

So why does this matter? This monitor is one of the safeguards OpenAI relies on while its agents work on internal code and systems. Most of these gaps weren't about the monitor's judgment. They were plumbing: a header that switches it off, a check that only fires at the end of a turn, a format mismatch an agent could likely use to make it fail. Benchmarks for monitor accuracy wouldn't catch any of that.

The counterpoint: this is a pilot from the summer, run by two people in three weeks. OpenAI found the biggest gap itself, and says everything is fixed. There's no evidence an agent exploited anything.

My read: the most important finding is that, in several cases, an agent could have influenced whether it got monitored at all. METR's recommendations point the same way: make unmonitored runs need explicit approval, check agents periodically rather than only when they stop, and keep agents away from the monitor's design and code. Pair that with OpenAI's own incident reports this week and the direction is clear. Monitors now have to assume the thing they're watching may work against them.

What to watch: whether OpenAI agrees to a second exercise under METR's standard terms, and whether regulators start asking for this kind of access.

### Microsoft's diagnostic AI, now in Science

Microsoft's MAI Diagnostic Orchestrator was published in the journal Science on Thursday. It's a set of AI agents that act like a panel of doctors. They question a simulated patient, order tests and argue toward a diagnosis.

The headline numbers come from a controlled benchmark built from three hundred and four hard New England Journal of Medicine cases. The system got eighty-three point two percent right. Generalist physicians got thirty-six point one. On emergency department cases it was about eighty percent against about forty-seven.

Now the part most headlines skip. The doctors were primary care physicians and general internists working alone. Specialists, who usually see cases like these, weren't in the comparison. And TechCrunch went through the numbers. GPT-5 on its own, with a basic prompt, already got seventy-eight point nine percent. Microsoft's orchestration added about four points, and cut the cost per case by about three thousand dollars. On the emergency cases, it barely helped. TechCrunch's reading is that much of the improvement comes from the underlying models.

Microsoft AI's chief, Mustafa Suleyman, calls this a step toward what he calls medical superintelligence. Next is a real-world study with Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center in Boston.

My read: if the base model does much of the work, the value of a medical AI product moves to cost, workflow and liability. Diagnosing well is becoming the easy part.

### The money: DeepSeek and OpenAI

Two very large funding stories on Thursday, one on each side of the Pacific.

Reuters reports that DeepSeek is wrapping up a round of about one hundred billion yuan, roughly fifteen billion dollars, according to four people familiar with it. The battery maker CATL and Tencent are among the biggest backers. New investors include Midea, Meituan, Geely and GigaDevice. The round values DeepSeek at five hundred billion yuan before the new money, roughly seventy-five billion dollars, and it's double the original target. Reuters reported last month that DeepSeek hired CITIC Securities to prepare for a possible listing on Shanghai's STAR Market. The terms aren't final, and DeepSeek didn't comment.

Meanwhile, CNBC reports OpenAI is close to raising up to thirty billion dollars at a one point two five trillion dollar valuation before the new money, led by Coatue and investors from the UAE. CNBC also reports it could come with a deal for more computing capacity in the UAE. On Tuesday we cited Reuters' report of a valuation above one point four trillion. OpenAI declined to comment.

My read: two things stand out. OpenAI's reported number went down, not up. And DeepSeek's investor list reads like a map of Chinese industry: batteries, cars, appliances, food delivery. China's best-known open-weight lab is being funded by the companies most likely to put its models to work.

### Anthropic uses Claude to formally verify open-source code

So weigh this segment accordingly.

Yesterday's PHP story was about AI finding bugs faster than people can patch them. Anthropic published a different approach on Thursday: proving that certain bugs aren't there.

Working with the verification company Theorem, Anthropic says Claude wrote machine-checked mathematical proofs that two hundred thirty-nine fuzz-test entry points, across one hundred twenty-three open-source libraries, never crash on inputs under sixty-four megabytes. The proofs are about the compiled programs, not just the source code. Together they run to about one hundred eighty million lines.

Along the way, Anthropic says, Claude found and patched hundreds of bugs. One example is a decompressor that could write out of bounds, but only on rare inputs around twenty megabytes. In one widely used PDF reader, it found about as many security bugs in a week as usually get reported in a year. Some proofs, the tooling and a Claude skill are now public.

Anthropic is clear about the limits. Formally verified doesn't mean bug-free. The proofs assume a specific setup, single-threaded programs on RISC-V Linux, while most people run these libraries on x86 or ARM. And not every entry point is covered.

My read: this proves one narrow property, not that these libraries are secure. But formal verification used to be practical only for small projects, or took a lot of expert time. If models can do the proof-writing, it becomes something open-source maintainers might actually use.

### Walmart opens its store to AI agents

On Wednesday, Walmart launched what it calls Agent Gateway. It's an MCP server, a standard connection point for AI agents, that lets approved assistants search Walmart and Sam's Club and buy for you. Reuters names ChatGPT, Gemini and Meta's Muse. Anyone with a Walmart account can use it.

Next month comes Agent Profiles. Each agent gets its own profile inside your account, its purchases are listed separately, and you choose whether to approve every order or set a spending limit. Walmart compares it to adding an authorized user to a credit card. Agents can't see your card details, get only the data a task needs, and can be blocked if they break Walmart's rules.

Amazon, meanwhile, has started blocking AI agents from buying on its site. And on Monday we covered Schwab and JPMorgan blocking outside agents from moving money.

My read: Walmart is betting that being where agents shop matters more than owning every click. And per-agent spending caps are a good template. It's the same approval-gate idea we keep seeing, only this time the merchant enforces it.

### Quick hit

One more. Anthropic is donating its Agent Skills standard, a format for packaging knowledge and workflows that agents can load, to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation, where its MCP protocol already lives. Engineers from Anthropic, Google, OpenAI and others will maintain it. Anthropic says more than seventy agent platforms use it, including ChatGPT, Cursor and GitHub Copilot. The basic plumbing for agents is moving into neutral hands, and that makes it easier for rivals to build on the same standards.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that agent permissions are becoming the real product. Walmart gives each agent a profile and a spending cap. Banks are deciding what agents can touch. And inside OpenAI, the question was whether an agent could switch off its own oversight.

The one thing I'd watch next is who gets to check those controls from outside. METR got real access to OpenAI, but the public got a redacted summary. As agents get more power, the terms of those audits will matter as much as the findings.
