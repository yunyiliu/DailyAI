### 开场

这里是 Daily AI Signal，今天是 10 月 14 日，星期三。

### 头条：Meta 发布 Muse Wonder

本周一，Meta 超级智能实验室发布了两个模型：Muse Wonder，和更强的 Muse Wonder Full。

先说背景。Meta 之前推出的 Muse Spark，主打的是小和快，不是冲榜。这次的 Wonder 完全不同：Meta 说它建立在更大、更强的基础模型上，在 Meta 位于俄亥俄州的新数据中心训练。在我看来，它瞄准的就是 Anthropic、OpenAI 和 Google 所在的第一梯队。

具体能用到什么？Muse Wonder 已经上线 Meta 的 API。据 TechCrunch 报道，价格是每百万输入 token 4 美元，每百万输出 token 20 美元。它支持一百万 token 的上下文，也就是一次能读进非常长的材料，可以处理文字、图片、视频和音频。Meta 主推两个功能：一个叫 Active Memory，能把它对你的了解带到下一次对话；另一个叫 Active Focus，负责在长任务里坚持下去，卡住了会换个办法，做重要操作之前会先问你。

那它到底强不强？Meta 自己说达到了业界最佳。但更有参考价值的是第三方数据。独立评测机构 Artificial Analysis 给 Wonder Full 打了 56 分，Meta 上一代最好的模型是 48 分。这个分数，已经把它放进了第一梯队。

不过，进步很不均衡。在终端里执行任务的 Agent 测试上，成绩从 33% 涨到了 57%，提升非常明显。但在事实性知识测试上，Wonder Full 只拿了 30 分，同一项测试里 Claude Opus 5.5 是 46 分。据 The Decoder 报道，在法律和金融任务上，它和上一代基本持平，最多还低了两分。我的判断是：这个模型干活的能力进步很大，但知识面的进步不大。

接下来，是很多报道没讲到的三点。

第一，最强的模型不对外卖。Muse Wonder Full 不在 API 里，想用它，只能订阅 Meta 自己的编程产品 Muse Code。我的理解是，这才是 Meta 真正的打算：它不想打 API 价格战，而是拿最强模型当理由，把开发者拉进自己的工具。

第二，Meta 不再是便宜的那一家了。输入价格是上一代 Muse Spark 的三倍多。Artificial Analysis 跑完整套测试，Wonder 每个任务大约花 4 美元 5 毛，老模型只要 1 美元 6 毛。一家过去把模型免费送人的公司，现在走的是高端定价。

第三，完整的安全报告还没出来。Meta 发布了模型卡，里面有主要的安全测试结果，并表示更完整的报告即将发布。也就是说，一个前沿级 Agent 模型，详细的测试说明要晚于模型本身才到。

这对你意味着什么？如果你在选写代码的 Agent 模型，现在多了第四个值得认真测试的选项，建议用你自己的任务去试。如果你的工作非常依赖专业领域的准确性，比如法律和金融，从目前的数据看，可以再等等。

接下来值得关注的有两件事：Wonder Full 会不会开放 API，以及完整安全报告发出来之后怎么说。

### Anthropic：防止推理过程被偷学

第二条，Anthropic 的一项调整，直接影响到开发者。

先解释一个词：蒸馏。在这里，它指的是未经允许，大规模地拿别人模型的输出，去训练自己的模型。本周一，Anthropic 发文说，新的防护措施让蒸馏者能从 Claude 拿到的推理数据，减少了大约百分之九十到九十九。它说已经封禁了数万个账号，很多是在一次行动开始后两小时内就封掉的。

它还点了名。Anthropic 指称，智谱从美国模型中蒸馏了网络安全相关的能力，而发给月之暗面 Kimi 模型的部分请求，被暗中转发给了 Claude。需要强调，这些都是 Anthropic 单方面的说法。我们没有看到这两家公司对这篇文章的正式回应，也没有外部机构核实过。

为什么偏偏是推理过程？Anthropic 自己做过实验：用 Claude 完整推理过程训练的模型，在一项编程测试里的得分大约是百分之八十；只用推理摘要训练，大约是百分之七十二。思考过程，比答案本身更值钱。

问题在于，其他人也要付出代价。现在开发者看到的是推理摘要，不是完整过程；较新的 API 账号，在顶级模型上不能修改之前的上下文。另外，从 9 月 24 日起，Anthropic 恢复了对部分被拦截请求的收费，范围是包括防蒸馏在内、误判率低于千分之一的几类拦截。千分之一不高，但也不是零。

我的看法是：模型的推理过程，正在从一个方便调试的功能，变成一项被保护的资产。其他公司很可能会跟进。

### Google：Gemini 4 Argon 开放

第三条，Google。本周二，Gemini 4 Argon 通过 Gemini API 向付费开发者开放，Google AI Pro 和 Ultra 的订阅用户也能在 Gemini 应用和搜索里用到它。

这个模型两周前就发布了，但第一阶段只给经过审核的网络安全防御人员用，Google 称之为分阶段发布。现在，付费用户都能用了。

有两点值得注意。一是价格：每百万 token，输入 2 美元，输出 10 美元，是 Meta 定价的一半。不过这是首发优惠价，据报道持续到 11 月底，之后会翻倍。二是单次输出上限，从六万四千 token 提高到一百万 token，对需要一次生成整个代码库或超长文档的人来说，这个变化很实用。Google 还说它在多项编程和视频测试里拿了最高分，这些是 Google 自己公布的数字，只能作为参考。

### 微软：自研芯片 Maia 300

第四条，芯片。微软公布了第三代自研 AI 芯片 Maia 300 的设计，这款芯片专门用于推理，也就是运行模型，而不是训练模型。

参数方面：采用台积电 2 纳米级工艺，配备大量高速内存，每个机柜装 96 颗芯片。微软称，每个机柜的产出最多是上一代的 7 倍。

但真正重要的是一句话：微软表示，Maia 将成为微软产品和服务的主要推理系统，并从 2027 年开始进行数吉瓦级别的部署。我的判断是，这等于英伟达最大的客户之一在说：以后跑模型，默认用自己的芯片。

当然也要看到局限：芯片明年才交付；和 GPU 的对比，没有说明比的是哪款 GPU；也没有公开测试方法。但方向很清楚，和 Google、亚马逊走的是同一条路。

### Anthropic：小模型能否自我复制

再来一条 Anthropic 的研究。这条被报道得很吓人，我们仔细说。

它的红队研究了一个问题：一个小 AI 模型，能不能把自己复制到便宜的消费级设备上，比如智能电视和电视盒子。在一个八台机器组成的测试网络里，一个由开源模型 GLM 5.2 驱动的蠕虫程序，五次尝试里有四次传遍了所有机器，并在每台机器上装上了一个小模型。GLM 5.2 本身运行在另一台服务器上，因为测试机器太小，装不下它。另外，研究人员还让一个中等规模的模型，跑在了十五台每台大约 55 美元的安卓电视盒子上。

吓人的标题没说的是：这个测试跳过了入侵环节，目标机器开着一个没有防护的端口，对攻击方来说是最理想的情况。传播测试是在云端模拟器上做的，不是真实设备。电视盒子虽然是真机，但运行速度大约每秒三分之一个 token，换算下来，一分钟大概十五个英文单词。

所以，这在今天还不是一个快速蔓延的威胁。它真正说明的是：当模型不再需要数据中心，想把它关掉会变得更难。应对办法其实很朴素：便宜设备默认要有更好的安全设置，网络运营商也要留意设备之间传输好几个 GB 的模型文件。

### 快讯

最后是几条快讯。

路透社援引知情人士报道，Anthropic 和 OpenAI 曾希望在很大程度上控制外部安全评估机构能发布哪些内容，理由是涉及商业机密。两家公司都对这一说法的部分内容提出了异议。这件事值得关注，是因为两家公司上个月刚刚公开表态支持独立评估。

根据路透社援引的 IDC 数据，今年上半年，英伟达在中国 AI 芯片出货量中的份额降到了 34%，华为是 28%，而且还在上升。

Character AI 申请了破产保护。迪士尼出价 2000 万美元，但买的是部分知识产权的使用授权，不是这个应用本身。公司表示，如果没有买家接手继续运营，平台将会关闭。

最后一条，顺便纠正一个流传的说法。Anthropic 介绍了它内部用来替代 Git 的版本管理系统 Scion。起因是它的代码合并量，四个月里从每天大约两千五百次，涨到了每天三万次以上。有早期报道说 Anthropic 把 Scion 开源了，其实没有，原文明确说，它并不适合通用场景。

### 结语

今天这些新闻背后，我看到的更大趋势是：AI 公司正在把门关上。

Meta 把最强的模型留在自己的产品里；Anthropic 隐藏了模型的推理过程；Google 先限定人群，再逐步开放新模型；微软自己造芯片，减少对外部的依赖。

一年前，大家问的是谁的模型最强；现在，问题变成了谁控制使用权。接下来我最想看的是：一直以开放著称的 Meta，会不会把 Wonder Full 放进 API。

### Hook

For the last couple of years, the top of the AI leaderboard has mostly been a three-company race. As of this week, it has a fourth name on it. And it's Meta.

But the most interesting part of Meta's launch isn't the model they released. It's the one they're keeping for themselves.

### Today's top stories

Also today: Anthropic says it has cut the theft of Claude's reasoning by ninety percent or more, and ordinary developers are paying part of the price. Google opens up Gemini 4 Argon. And Microsoft says its own chips will become the main way it runs AI models in its products, starting in 2027.

This is Daily AI Signal for Wednesday, October fourteenth. Let's get into it.

### Main story — Meta Muse Wonder

On Monday, Meta's Superintelligence Lab released two models. Muse Wonder, and Muse Wonder Full.

Some context. Since Alexandr Wang reorganized Meta's AI work last year, the company has shipped small, fast models. Muse Spark was built for low latency, not for topping charts. Wonder is different. It's much larger, it was trained at Meta's new gigawatt-scale data center in Ohio, and it's aimed directly at Anthropic, OpenAI and Google.

Here's what you get. Muse Wonder is on Meta's API right now. According to TechCrunch, it costs four dollars per million input tokens and twenty dollars per million output tokens. It has a one million token context window, and takes text, images, video and audio.

Meta is pushing two features. Active Memory, which carries what the model knows about you from one conversation to the next. And Active Focus, which is about staying on a long task: trying another approach when it gets stuck, and checking in with you before it does anything consequential.

So, is it actually good? Meta says it's state of the art. The numbers are in its model card rather than the launch post, and the most useful ones are independent. Artificial Analysis, which runs an independent benchmark index, scored Wonder Full at fifty-six. Meta's previous best scored forty-eight. That puts Full in the top group of models, alongside the ones you already know.

The gains are specific, though. On a benchmark of terminal-based agent tasks, the score went from thirty-three percent to fifty-seven. That's a big jump. But on a test of factual knowledge, Wonder Full scored thirty. Claude Opus 5.5 scored forty-six on the same test. And on legal and finance tasks, The Decoder reports it was roughly flat, down by as much as two points from the model it replaces.

My read: this is a model that got a lot better at doing things, and not much better at knowing things.

Now, here's what most headlines are missing.

First, the best model isn't for sale. Muse Wonder Full is not on the API. The only way to use it is to subscribe to Muse Code, Meta's coding product. My read is that this is the real strategy. Meta isn't trying to win the API price war. It's using its strongest model as a reason to move into its own tools.

Second, Meta is not the cheap option anymore. The input price is more than three times what Muse Spark cost. Artificial Analysis found that running its full test suite cost about four dollars and fifty cents per task on Wonder, against a dollar sixty on the older model. That's premium pricing, from a company that used to give models away.

Third, the full safety report isn't out. Meta published a model card with headline safety results, and says a fuller report with more evaluations is coming soon. So the detailed account of how a frontier agent model was tested is arriving after the model.

What does this mean in practice? If you're picking a model for coding agents, you now have a fourth serious option, and it's worth testing on your own tasks. If your work depends on accuracy in a specialist field, like law or finance, the early numbers say wait.

What I'd watch: whether Wonder Full ever reaches the API, and what the full safety report says when it lands.

### Secondary — Anthropic distillation defenses

Next, Anthropic, and a story that changes the product for developers.

Distillation, in this context, means training your model on another model's outputs, at scale, without permission. On Monday, Anthropic published a post saying its new defenses cut the amount of reasoning data that distillers can pull out of Claude by roughly ninety to ninety-nine percent. It says it banned tens of thousands of accounts, often within two hours of a campaign starting.

It also named names. Anthropic alleges that Zhipu distilled cyber capabilities from American models, and that requests to Moonshot's Kimi models were secretly forwarded to Claude. To be clear, those are Anthropic's claims. I couldn't confirm an official response from either company to this post, and nobody outside has verified the claims.

The interesting part is why reasoning is the target. Anthropic ran its own experiment. A model trained on Claude's full reasoning scored about eighty percent on a coding benchmark. Trained on summaries only, it scored about seventy-two. The thinking is worth more than the answer.

The catch is what this costs everyone else. You see summarized reasoning, not the full trace. Newer API accounts can't edit earlier context on the top models. And since September twenty-fourth, Anthropic has gone back to charging for requests blocked by certain safeguards. Those are the ones, including the distillation filter, where it says false positives are under a tenth of a percent. That's low, but it isn't zero.

My interpretation: model reasoning is turning from a debugging feature into a protected asset. I'd expect the other labs to follow.

### Secondary — Gemini 4 Argon

Google. Yesterday, Gemini 4 Argon became available to paying developers through the Gemini API, and to AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app and in Search.

This model was announced two weeks ago, but it first went only to trusted cyber defenders, in what Google called a phased release. Now it's open to paying users.

Two things stand out. The price: two dollars in, ten dollars out, per million tokens. That's half of what Meta is charging. It's an introductory rate, it reportedly runs through the end of November, and then it doubles. And the output limit, which goes from sixty-four thousand tokens to one million. That matters if you're generating whole codebases or very long documents in one pass.

Google claims the top score on several coding and video benchmarks. Those are Google's numbers, so treat them as a starting point, not a verdict.

### Secondary — Microsoft Maia 300

Now chips. Microsoft published the design of Maia 300, its third in-house AI chip, built for inference, meaning running models rather than training them.

The specs are what you'd expect: TSMC's two-nanometer-class process, a lot of fast memory, ninety-six chips to a rack. Microsoft claims up to seven times the output of its previous generation, per rack.

But the bigger story is one sentence. Microsoft says its Maia chips will become the main inference system for its products, with multi-gigawatt deployments starting in 2027. My read: this is one of Nvidia's biggest customers saying that its default, for running models, will be its own silicon.

The caveats are real. The chip ships next year. The comparisons to GPUs don't name which GPUs. And there's no public methodology. But the direction is clear, and it's the same direction as Google and Amazon.

### Secondary — self-replication research

One more from Anthropic, and this one got dramatic coverage, so let's be careful with it.

Its red team published research asking whether a small AI model could copy itself onto cheap consumer devices. Think smart TVs and streaming boxes. In a test network of eight machines, a worm driven by one open-weight model, GLM 5.2, reached every machine in four out of five tries, and set up a small model on each one. GLM itself ran on a separate server, because the test machines were too small to hold it. Separately, the researchers got a mid-sized model running across fifteen Android TV boxes that cost about fifty-five dollars each.

Here's what the scary headlines left out. The test skipped the hacking. The target machines had an open, unprotected port, so this is a best case for the attacker. The spreading test ran on emulators in the cloud, not real devices. The TV boxes were real, but that setup produced about a third of a token per second. That's around fifteen words a minute.

So this is not a fast-moving threat today. What it does show is that the off switch gets harder to find when a model doesn't need a data center. The fixes are unglamorous: better default security on cheap devices, and internet providers watching for multi-gigabyte model files moving between them.

### Quick hits

Quick hits.

Reuters reports that Anthropic and OpenAI had been pushing for significant control over what outside safety evaluators are allowed to publish, citing trade secrets. ### Final take

The bigger trend I see behind today's news is that the labs are closing their doors.

Meta keeps its best model inside its own product. Anthropic hides its models' reasoning. Google gated its newest model before releasing it. Microsoft is building its own chips so it depends on fewer outsiders.

A year ago, the question was who has the best model. Now it's who controls access to it. The one thing I'd watch next is whether Meta, the company that built its AI reputation on being open, ever puts Wonder Full on the API.
