### 头条：一个人，一套 AI 智能体，攻破上百家公司

今天的头条，是一起由 AI 智能体完成的黑客攻击。

周一，安全公司 NorForge 的研究员斯诺勒·法格兰发布报告，路透社同一天做了独家报道。有一个开源工具叫 Cairn，给它一个目标，它就会调度一批 AI 智能体，去找漏洞、尝试入侵。有些人把 Cairn 的控制面板直接暴露在网上，法格兰找到了 20 多个他判断为恶意使用的面板，能看到里面在做什么。

其中最活跃的一个操作者，用中文下指令，指令不到 100 个汉字。据路透社报道，从 9 月下旬开始，这些智能体攻击美国、加拿大、澳大利亚和日本的网店，到上周末，已经尝试入侵了 1200 多个网站。

路透社报道，被攻破的公司超过 100 家，点名了迪尔、尚科宁家和康明斯。迪尔说，已经补救，没有迹象显示敏感信息被访问。尚科宁家说，受影响的是一个独立的售后网站，已经控制住。康明斯说，正在调查一个外部供应商运营的网站。有多少数据被拿走，路透社没能查清。

成本方面，法格兰估算，整个行动大约花了 5000 美元的 token 费用，平均每成功入侵一次，大约 50 美元。他也说，这些数字可能有不小的误差。成功率在百分之十左右，成功一次，平均不到一天。

用的是谁家的 AI？报告里有一个智能体进程，名字同时带着 Claude Code 和 DeepSeek V4.1 Flash，上周日我们刚讲过这个 DeepSeek 模型的新版本。NorForge 说，这可能意味着用 Anthropic 的 Claude Code，调用 DeepSeek 的模型。但这只是一个名字，不是确认的结论，我们也没有看到两家公司的回应。

这件事真正值得注意的，是分工变了。人只负责定目标，找入口、找漏洞、动手尝试，都是智能体在做。被攻击的，也不是有大安全团队的银行，而是普通网店。起作用的，不是什么顶级模型，而是一套免费的调度工具，加上一个便宜的模型。如果进程名字属实，工具和模型来自不同公司，任何一家 AI 公司，都很难单独发现这种滥用。

当然，这些都是一位研究员的估算，成功率也不高。但每次尝试只花几美元，百分之十已经足够了。

我的判断是，大范围黑客攻击的成本，已经降到一个人就付得起。小网站要有准备，以后来试探你的，可能是不知疲倦的智能体。

### 第二条：美国要求联邦民用机构清除 DeepSeek

第二条，还是 DeepSeek。

10 月 20 日，美国《联邦公报》刊登通知，国土安全部部长依据 2018 年的一部供应链安全法律发布命令，禁止联邦民用机构采购 DeepSeek 的产品，已经在用的，要清除出系统。

关键是范围。要清除的，还包括用 DeepSeek 开发的第三方产品和服务。也就是说，哪家供应商在 DeepSeek 基础上做了产品，卖给联邦民用机构，也在范围之内。

据美国政府科技媒体 MeriTalk 报道，命令不适用于国防部、情报机构和国家安全系统，理由包括用户数据存储在中国，用户的提问会发送到中国的服务器，以及取证分析发现的安全漏洞。命令哪天签的、整改期限多长，各方说法不一，我们先不说。我们没有看到 DeepSeek 的回应。

还有一个问题没说清楚，承包商把 DeepSeek 的开源权重，部署在自己的服务器上，算不算“用 DeepSeek 开发”？通知里没有写。

我的判断是，用哪个模型，正在变成供应链合规问题。以后给美国政府卖软件，得说清楚产品里用的是哪家的模型。

### 第三条：微软把 AI 智能体当成“内部人员”来管

第三条，从外部攻击者，说到公司内部的智能体。

周一，微软发布安全博客，标题是“智能体是你最新的内部人员”，意思是，要像防范可能出问题的员工一样，管理每一个 AI 智能体。

微软说，它的工具现在能在员工电脑上识别 60 种智能体，包括 Claude Code、OpenAI 的 Codex 和 GitHub Copilot。每个智能体有自己的身份，和一个持续更新的风险评分。权限按任务给，任务结束就收回，高风险操作，需要经过验证的真人批准。

不过，微软没有给出上线时间和价格，所以这还是一个方向。

我更关注那份识别清单。开发者自己装的编程智能体，以后也会被公司 IT 登记、打分。昨天我们讲了银行不让外部智能体动钱，再加上今天的黑客新闻，管住智能体，正在变成一个单独的产品方向。

### 第四条：OpenAI 赶在 Anthropic 上市前融资

第四条，说钱。

据路透社报道，OpenAI 正在加紧锁定投资人，想赶在 Anthropic 预计 11 月中旬上市之前，完成一轮大约 300 亿美元的融资，估值可能超过 1.4 万亿美元。今年 3 月，OpenAI 的估值是 8520 亿美元。

路透社还报道，Anthropic 第三季度收入 168 亿美元，第二季度是 116 亿美元，但有投资人认为，它的估值应该在 1.5 万亿美元左右，低于据报道它想要的 2 万亿美元。OpenAI 指责 Anthropic 收入算多了，Anthropic 表示，它遵循的是公认的会计做法。

我的判断是，OpenAI 想在公开市场有参照之前，先把自己的价格定下来。这些数字目前都没有最终确定。

### 第五条：MiniMax 发布 M3.1 Flash

第五条，MiniMax 周一发布了 M3.1 Flash，已经可以通过 API 使用，每百万输入 token 0.15 美元，每百万输出 token 0.6 美元。

MiniMax 说，推理速度提升了将近 3 倍，跑智能体任务的成本，比上一代降低了百分之七十。这些都是 MiniMax 自己公布的，还没有第三方验证。权重会晚些开源，没有给日期。

智能体特别耗 token，又便宜又能干的模型，会让用智能体的成本降下来。只是看过今天的头条，我们也要记得，用的人里，也包括攻击者。

### 快讯

最后是三条快讯。

Meta 公开了它在美国俄亥俄州的 AI 超级计算集群 Prometheus。Meta 说，Muse 系列模型就是在这里训练的，现在有几十万块 GPU，计划扩展到大约 100 万块。

昨天我们讲了 Nscale 上市遇阻。彭博社现在报道，Nscale 和 Anthropic 正在谈第二笔大约 450 亿美元的交易，是西弗吉尼亚州园区的另一栋设施。谈判还在进行，细节可能会变。

在加拿大，Postmedia、Torstar、《环球邮报》和加拿大通讯社，在安大略省法院起诉 Anthropic，指控它未经授权，用它们的新闻内容开发 Claude。这些都是指控，还没有经过法庭认定，报道发出时，Anthropic 没有回应。

### 结语

今天这些新闻背后，更大的趋势是，AI 智能体正在被当成“人”来对待。攻击者把它当廉价劳动力，微软给它发身份、打风险分，政府开始追问，产品里的模型到底来自哪里。

接下来我最想看的，是名字出现在 Cairn 面板里的 AI 公司，会怎么回应。模型公司能不能发现这种用法，还是说，防守只能靠网站自己。

### Hook

One person typed short instructions into an open-source tool, and AI agents did the rest. According to Reuters, more than a hundred companies were broken into, for a token bill of a few thousand dollars.

### Today's top stories

Also today: the US government orders DeepSeek out of federal civilian systems, Microsoft starts treating AI agents like employees who might go rogue, and OpenAI races to raise money before Anthropic goes public.

This is Daily AI Signal for Tuesday, October twentieth. Let's get into it.

### Main story — a hacking campaign run by AI agents

The research comes from Snorre Fagerland, a threat researcher at a security firm called NorForge. He published it on Monday, and Reuters ran an exclusive the same day.

Here's what he found. There's an open-source tool called Cairn. You give it a target and a goal, and it coordinates a set of AI agents that look for weaknesses and try to get in. Some people running Cairn left its control panels open on the internet. Fagerland found more than twenty panels he judged to be malicious, and he could read what they were doing.

The busiest operator wrote in Chinese. According to Reuters, from late September this person aimed the agents at online stores in the United States, Canada, Australia and Japan. The instructions were short, under a hundred Chinese characters. By the end of last week, Reuters says, the agents had tried to break into more than twelve hundred websites.

Reuters reports that more than a hundred companies were breached, and it named three: Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins. Deere says it took remediation steps and sees no sign that sensitive information was accessed. SharkNinja says a standalone product support site was hit and the problem is contained. Cummins says it's investigating a possible attack on a website run by an outside vendor. Reuters could not determine how much data, if any, was taken.

Now the cost. Fagerland told Reuters he estimates the campaign used about five thousand dollars in tokens. That's roughly fifty dollars for each successful break-in. His own report is careful here. He says the numbers could be off by a clear margin. He puts the success rate at around ten percent, and successful attacks took a little under a day on average.

Which AI was doing the work? One of the agent workers is labeled with the names of Claude Code and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash model. On Sunday we covered a new version of that DeepSeek model. NorForge says the label could mean Anthropic's Claude Code running DeepSeek's model. That's a label, not a confirmed finding. We haven't seen a response from Anthropic or DeepSeek.

So why does this matter? Look at the division of labor. The operator mostly set targets and goals. The agents did the scouting, found the weak spots and tried the exploits, over about a day per success. The targets weren't banks with big security teams. They were online stores.

What most headlines are missing is where the leverage sits. In this case it isn't a secret frontier model. It's a free harness that coordinates agents, plus a model that's cheap to run. If the label is accurate, the harness comes from one company and the model from another. That makes abuse harder for any single AI provider to spot.

The counterpoint is real. These are one researcher's estimates. The success rate is low. And we don't know what data was taken. But a ten percent hit rate is plenty when each attempt costs a few dollars.

My read: the cost of a broad hacking campaign has dropped to something a single person can pay for. Defenders should assume that small sites will be probed by tireless agents, not by bored humans.

What to watch next: whether the AI providers whose names appear in these panels say anything about detection, and whether more victims come forward.

### DHS orders DeepSeek out of federal civilian systems

Next, DeepSeek and Washington. On Tuesday, the Federal Register published notice of an order from the Secretary of Homeland Security. It uses a twenty eighteen supply-chain security law to bar DeepSeek products from federal civilian agency purchases. It also requires agencies to remove DeepSeek products already in their systems.

The detail that matters is the scope. The removal covers third-party products and services "developed using DeepSeek implementations." So it isn't only DeepSeek's own app. It reaches any vendor that built something on top of DeepSeek and sold it to a civilian agency. The cybersecurity agency, CISA, handles implementation.

According to MeriTalk, the order was signed by Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin. MeriTalk says it doesn't apply to the Defense Department, the intelligence community or national security systems. The reasons it lists include user data stored in China and prompts sent to servers in China. They also include security weaknesses found in forensic analysis. Reports disagree on when the order was signed and how long agencies have to comply, so we're leaving those details out. We haven't seen a response from DeepSeek.

The open question is DeepSeek's open-weight models. If an agency's contractor runs those weights on its own servers, does that count as a DeepSeek implementation? The notice doesn't say.

My read: this turns model choice into a supply-chain question. If you sell software to the US government, you now need to know which models are inside your product, the way you already track open-source libraries.

### Microsoft treats agents as insiders

From attackers to the agents inside your own company. On Monday, Microsoft published a security post titled "Agents are your newest insiders." It treats each AI agent the way security teams treat an employee who might go rogue.

Here's what's new. Microsoft says its tools can now find sixty types of agents running on staff computers, up from twenty-five. That includes Claude Code, OpenAI's Codex and GitHub Copilot. Each agent can get its own identity. It gets a risk score that updates continuously. And it gets permissions that are scoped to one task and expire when the task ends. For high-impact actions, a verified human has to approve. As an agent's risk rises, Microsoft describes graduated responses. There are also sandboxes that put an enforcement boundary around an agent, and "guardian agents" that do security work within policies people set.

Microsoft's deputy chief information security officer, Terrell Cox, said in a Microsoft post quoted by The Next Web: "At Microsoft, we are already operating in a world of hundreds of thousands of agents."

The catch: Microsoft didn't give availability dates or prices in the post. So this is a direction, not something you can switch on today.

The interesting part is the discovery list. Coding agents that developers install themselves are now something IT will inventory and score. Yesterday we covered banks blocking outside agents from moving money. Put that next to today's hacking story, and controlling agents is turning into its own product category.

### OpenAI races to raise before Anthropic's IPO

Now the money. Reuters reports that OpenAI is trying to lock in investor commitments before Anthropic's expected public listing in mid-November. According to its sources, the round could be about thirty billion dollars, at a valuation above one point four trillion. SoftBank and Abu Dhabi's MGX are among the existing backers in talks. For comparison, OpenAI was valued at eight hundred fifty-two billion in March.

One source told Reuters that OpenAI told investors it has reached fifty billion dollars in annualized revenue and expects seventy billion by year end. On Anthropic's side, Reuters reports third-quarter revenue of sixteen point eight billion, up from eleven point six billion. But some investors are arguing for a valuation near one and a half trillion, below the two trillion it reportedly wants. OpenAI has accused Anthropic of counting revenue too generously from deals sold through cloud providers. Anthropic says it follows established accounting practices. OpenAI declined to comment, and Anthropic didn't immediately respond.

Why the rush? According to Reuters, OpenAI wants to finish before Anthropic's financial disclosures give investors a fuller picture to compare against. My read: OpenAI wants its price set before public-market comparisons exist. And again, this show is made with Claude, so weigh my take on the Anthropic side accordingly. None of these numbers are final.

### MiniMax M3.1 Flash

One more launch. Chinese lab MiniMax released M3.1 Flash on Monday. It's available now through MiniMax's API and its coding product. The price is fifteen cents per million input tokens and sixty cents per million output tokens.

MiniMax claims inference is nearly three times faster than before, and that running agent tasks costs seventy percent less than on its previous model. It also says an anonymous model called Space Bunny, which MiniMax says processed more than two hundred trillion tokens, was an early version of M3.1 Flash. The benchmark numbers on its page are MiniMax's own, and nobody has checked them independently yet. MiniMax says the weights will be released later, with no date.

This matters because agents burn through tokens, so a cheap, capable model makes agents cheaper to run for everyone. After today's main story, it's worth remembering that "everyone" includes attackers.

### Quick hits

Meta published a look inside Prometheus, its AI supercluster in New Albany, Ohio. Meta says it trained its Muse models there, it has hundreds of thousands of GPUs today, and it plans to grow to about a million. One detail engineers will like: Meta says it cut training interruptions by ninety-nine percent from their peak in mid twenty twenty-five.

Yesterday we covered Nscale, the data center company whose IPO is meeting resistance. Now Bloomberg reports that Nscale and Anthropic are in talks on a second deal worth about forty-five billion dollars, for another building at Nscale's campus in West Virginia. Talks are ongoing and details could change. Neither company immediately responded to Reuters.

And in Canada, four news publishers have sued Anthropic in an Ontario court. They are Postmedia, Torstar, The Globe and Mail and The Canadian Press. They allege Anthropic used their journalism to build Claude without a license, and that it keeps scraping their sites when users ask questions. These are allegations, not findings. Anthropic hadn't responded to a request for comment when the story was published. The same publishers sued OpenAI in twenty twenty-four, and that case is still open.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that agents are being treated like people. Attackers use them as cheap labor. Microsoft gives them identities and risk scores, like staff. And governments are starting to ask where the model inside a product comes from.

The one thing I would watch next is the response from the AI providers named in the Cairn panels. If cheap harnesses plus cheap models are this effective, the question is whether providers can spot that kind of use, or whether defense has to happen on the websites themselves.
