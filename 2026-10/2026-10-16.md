### 头条：Meta 开放 Muse Spark Open 权重

今天的头条来自 Meta。周四，Meta 公开了一个叫 Muse Spark Open 的模型权重，任何人都可以下载。Meta 说，这是迄今为止美国最强的开放权重模型。

先说背景。Muse Spark 今年 4 月发布的时候，是 Meta 第一个不开放权重的模型。这次放出来的，是它的开放版本，Meta 额外做了安全训练，让它脱离 Meta 自己的防护系统，也能单独使用。

具体参数是这样的。它是混合专家模型，总参数 8160 亿，每次激活 420 亿，上下文最长 100 万 token。能输入文字、图片、视频和音频，输出文字，主要面向写代码和长时间运行的 Agent。

下载渠道有 Hugging Face，需要先填申请表，还有亚马逊、谷歌云和微软的模型平台。Groq、Fireworks、Together 等 7 家服务商，第一天就提供了 API。Meta 还说，两台顶配的苹果 Mac Studio 连起来，就能跑这个模型。

真正值得看的，是 Meta 自己公布的成绩表。在 Artificial Analysis 的综合智能指数上，Meta 列出的分数是 39，同一张表里，智谱的 GLM 5.3 是 45，月之暗面的 Kimi K3 是 44。在命令行编程测试 Terminal-Bench 上，它是 76.4，Kimi K3 是 85。它领先的是工具调用，在 MCP-Atlas 测试上拿了 89.2，是这张表里的最高分。

换句话说，美国最强开放模型，和全球最强开放模型，是两回事。按 Meta 自己的表，综合排名靠前的，还是中国模型。

再说许可证。它叫 Muse Model License，不是开源协议。大多数开发者可以免费用，但如果公司年收入超过 2000 万美元，想把它做成托管服务来卖，就要先通知 Meta，另签协议。用它做的产品，必须标注 Built with Muse Spark Open。使用政策禁止用于武器、军事和关键基础设施，也不面向 18 岁以下的用户。另外，完整的安全评估报告，Meta 说很快发布，也就是说现在还没有。

我的判断是，Meta 在把产品线一分为二。最新的 Muse Wonder 系列，我们周三讲过，仍然闭源。上一代模型，拿出来开放。对需要私有化部署的海外企业来说，这是一个来自美国公司的新选项。接下来要看独立评测的结果，和 Meta 承诺的安全报告。

### DeepMind：AI 写了大部分代码，研究却没变快

第二条，一个对所有技术团队都有参考价值的数字。

谷歌 DeepMind 为 Gemini 4 Argon 发布了一份安全报告，其中一部分专门回答一个问题，AI 有没有在加速 DeepMind 自己的 AI 研究。这是安全研究者最担心的情况之一，AI 越来越快地改进 AI。

据 Decrypt 的报道，答案是，还没有。DeepMind 超过百分之七十五的生产代码，已经由 AI 写成。但大约百分之八十八的代码合并请求，仍然需要人反复引导，或者由人来写。完全交给 AI 的合并请求，从 4 月的百分之七点五，升到 9 月的百分之十一点九。而 DeepMind 内部的能力指数，一直在稳定上升，4 月以后，没有出现可以测量到的加速。

我们直接查看了 DeepMind 的模型卡，上面确认，Argon 没有达到自动化 AI 研究的风险门槛。

我的判断是，百分之七十五和百分之八十八放在一起，最有启发。代码大部分交给了 AI，研究并没有因此变快，瓶颈转到了定方向和做检查。如果你的团队用代码行数来衡量 AI 工具，可能量错了东西。

需要提醒的是，这是 DeepMind 给自己打分。同一份模型卡也说，不能排除 Argon 达到了网络攻击和生物武器方面的风险门槛，所以加了防护措施。

### 微软：2.8 万亿参数的基座模型

第三条，微软。

周四，微软 AI 宣布，完成了 MAI-Base-2 的预训练。这是一个混合专家模型，总参数 2.8 万亿，激活 800 亿，用 1.6 万块英伟达 GB200 训练了 38 天，训练数据 50 万亿 token，原生支持图像。现在正在做后训练，也就是把基座模型调成能用的产品。

微软最主要的说法是效率。它说，自己的预训练效率，是 DeepSeek 最强开放基座模型的 4 到 12 倍。这是微软用曲线拟合做的估算，不是正面对比测试。

更值得关注的，是微软打算怎么用它。它说，会通过 Foundry 平台，把模型权重授权给受信任的合作伙伴。微软没有宣布公开发布。

把这两天放在一起看。Meta 把权重给所有人，微软授权给受信任的合作伙伴，OpenAI 和谷歌，最强模型的权重留在自己手里。我的判断是，开放还是封闭，已经不是二选一了。

### Anthropic 让外部评估人员进驻

第四条，是一条后续。周三我们讲过，据路透社报道，Anthropic 和 OpenAI 都曾希望在很大程度上控制，外部评估机构能发表什么。周四，Anthropic 公布了它的安排。

Anthropic 说，评估机构 METR 的 6 名员工已经进驻公司，拿到了公司设备、办公室，以及和内部风险团队差不多的系统权限。Anthropic 说，它不能否决 METR 发表内容，但可以要求删去几类预先划定的信息，比如客户数据，受法律保护的信息，和模型架构。METR 可以公开说，它认为这些删减合不合理。

路透社提到，有专家担心，评估机构是公司自己挑的，独立性要打问号。

真正的检验，是 METR 的第一份报告，看看删了多少，METR 自己怎么说。

### 台积电：没有看到放缓

第五条，也是后续。昨天我们讲了 CoreWeave 的债券承压，和 ASML 说供不应求。周四，台积电发布了第三季度财报。

营收 467 亿美元，按美元算，同比增长百分之四十一。按新台币算，净利润增长百分之六十九。台积电预计，下个季度营收 523 亿到 543 亿美元，今年全年按美元算，增长百分之四十四左右。最新的 2 纳米工艺，已经占到晶圆收入的百分之八。

董事长魏哲家说，没有看到任何放缓的迹象，需求仍然高于供给。

可是这一周，芯片股并不好过。据雅虎财经报道，原因包括一份高于预期的通胀数据，让市场担心利率继续走高，还有对债务上升、AI 基建可能受阻的担忧。

我的判断是，产业链最底层的公司都在说订单很足，市场担心的，是谁来付钱，利息多高。这是两个不同的问题。

### 快讯

最后是几条快讯。

英伟达发布了开放模型 Nemotron 3.5 Super VL，总参数 1200 亿，激活 120 亿，能看文字、图片和短视频。模型卡写明，许可证允许商用。

据彭博社报道，苹果计划在 12 月，随 iOS 27.3 在中国大陆推出 Apple Intelligence。苹果还没有确认。报道说，这个版本功能有限，不包括新版 Siri，模型和阿里巴巴合作，后续模型更新需要经过监管审批。

另外，在苹果起诉 OpenAI 等被告盗用商业机密的案子里，据彭博社报道，法官周三表示，很可能批准苹果申请的初步禁令。随后 OpenAI 同意，在案件审理期间，不使用苹果所说被带走的信息，并对 14 名员工的设备做取证备份。OpenAI 还在一份新的动议里指控，一名回到苹果的前员工带走了 100 多份机密文件。双方的指控都还没有被证实。

### 结语

今天这些新闻背后，我看到的更大趋势是，AI 公司开始公开自己的成绩单，但都是按自己的方式。Meta 公布了一张自己不是第一的成绩表。DeepMind 公布数据，说 AI 没有加速自己的研究。Anthropic 公布了外部评估的规则。问题在于，这些都是自己报告的。

接下来我最想看的，是第一次外部检验，不管是 Muse Spark Open 的独立评测，还是 METR 对 Anthropic 的第一份报告。到那时候，才知道这些自我报告值多少。

### Main story — Meta releases Muse Spark Open

On Thursday, Meta released the weights of a model called Muse Spark Open. Some context first. Muse Spark launched in April as Meta's first closed model. Meta had been the American lab most associated with open weights, and then it put its best model behind an API. This is an open-weight version of that model, with extra safety training so it can run without Meta's own guardrails around it.

Here are the key facts. It's a mixture-of-experts model with eight hundred and sixteen billion total parameters, and forty-two billion active for each token. The context window is up to one million tokens. It takes text, images, video and audio in, and writes text out. It's built for coding and long-running agent work.

You can get it on Hugging Face, after filling in an access form, and in the model catalogs of Amazon, Google Cloud and Microsoft. Seven hosting companies, including Groq, Fireworks and Together, serve it through an API from day one. And Meta says it can run on two of Apple's top-end Mac Studios linked together. On the data-center side, the model card lists at least seven hundred gigabytes of GPU memory for the compressed version.

Meta calls it the most capable US open-weight model to date. Now look at Meta's own benchmark table, because this is the part most headlines are missing. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, Meta lists Muse Spark Open at thirty-nine. In the same table, Zhipu's GLM 5.3 scores forty-five, and Moonshot's Kimi K3 scores forty-four. On Terminal-Bench, a test of coding in a command line, it's seventy-six point four against Kimi's eighty-five. Where it leads is tool use. On MCP-Atlas, which tests choosing and chaining calls across real tools, it scores eighty-nine point two, the top score in Meta's table.

So "most capable US open model" may be true by Meta's numbers, but it's a narrower claim than "best open model". On the overall index, the leaders in Meta's own chart are still Chinese.

Then the license. It's called the Muse Model License, and it is not open source. Most developers can use it for free. But if your company makes more than twenty million dollars a year and you want to sell the model as a hosted service, you have to notify Meta and sign a separate agreement. Every product built on it has to display "Built with Muse Spark Open". The usage policy bans things like weapons, military uses and critical infrastructure, and says the model isn't intended for anyone under eighteen. And Meta says a full safety evaluation report is coming "soon", which means it isn't out yet.

Why does this matter? For a lot of companies, especially in regulated industries, the choice has been: send your code to a closed API, or run an open model from a Chinese lab. This gives them a third option, from a US company, on the clouds they already use.

My read is that Meta is now splitting its line. Its newest models, the Muse Wonder family we covered on Wednesday, stay closed. The previous generation goes open. That's a sensible business pattern, but it means "open Meta" now means "last season's Meta".

Who should care: teams building coding agents, anyone who has to self-host for compliance, and the inference providers. That twenty-million-dollar clause is aimed squarely at them.

What to watch: independent benchmark results in the next couple of weeks, and the safety report Meta has promised.

### DeepMind: AI writes the code, but research isn't faster

Next, a number that every engineering manager should hear.

Google DeepMind published a safety report for its Gemini 4 Argon model, and one section asks a big question. Is AI speeding up AI research inside DeepMind itself? This is one of the scenarios safety people worry about most, where AI starts improving AI faster and faster.

According to Decrypt's reporting on the report, the answer is no, not yet. More than seventy-five percent of DeepMind's production code is now written by AI. But about eighty-eight percent of pull requests still need several rounds of human steering, or code written by a person. Fully handed-off pull requests rose from seven and a half percent in April to about twelve percent in September. And on DeepMind's internal capability index, progress has been steady, about a tenth of a point a month, with no measurable speed-up since April.

Haydn Belfield, a DeepMind researcher who worked on the assessment, put it this way on X: "There is currently no significant end-to-end acceleration of ML R&D, AI-driven or otherwise." DeepMind's model card, which we read directly, confirms that Argon stayed below the lab's own danger threshold for automating AI research.

The interesting part for everyone else is that gap between seventy-five and eighty-eight. Writing most of the code didn't make the research go faster. The bottleneck moved to deciding what to build, and checking it. If you're measuring AI coding tools by lines written, you may be measuring the wrong thing.

Two caveats. This is one lab grading itself, and the same model card says DeepMind can't rule out that Argon crosses its thresholds for cyberattack and bioweapon information. That's why it says it has added safeguards. And notice the timing. On Wednesday, the evaluation nonprofit METR published a proposal for how an outside assessor could check exactly this kind of claim.

### Microsoft finishes MAI-Base-2

Which brings us to Microsoft.

On Thursday, Microsoft AI said it has finished pre-training MAI-Base-2. It's a mixture-of-experts model with two point eight trillion total parameters and eighty billion active. It was trained for thirty-eight days, on sixteen thousand of Nvidia's GB200 chips, on fifty trillion tokens, and it understands images natively. It's now in post-training, the stage that turns a base model into something you can use.

Microsoft's headline claim is efficiency. It says its recipe is four to twelve times more compute-efficient in pre-training than DeepSeek's strongest open base model. That's Microsoft's own estimate, from fitting scaling curves, not a head-to-head test.

The bigger story is what Microsoft plans to do with it. It says it will license the weights to trusted partners through its Foundry platform. It hasn't announced a public release.

So look at the last two days together. Meta gives its weights to everyone. Microsoft will license them to trusted partners. OpenAI and Google keep their frontier weights. My read: "open or closed" isn't a binary any more. For enterprise buyers, the vetted-partner option may be the one that matters most.

### Anthropic lets METR inside

Now a follow-up. On Wednesday we covered a Reuters report that Anthropic and OpenAI had sought significant control over what outside evaluators could publish about their models. On Thursday, Anthropic published its terms.

Anthropic says it has brought six people from METR inside the company, with company hardware, office space and access to internal systems roughly like its own risk teams have. The key line from Anthropic's post: "Anthropic can't veto publication, but we can require them to redact information." Those redactions are limited to narrow, predefined categories, and Anthropic's examples are customer data, legally privileged material, and model architecture. METR can say publicly whether it thinks a redaction was justified.

Anthropic describes three tiers of access, with METR at the top. In the lowest tier, Anthropic still approves what's published. Others, like the biosecurity group SecureBio, are starting with narrower projects and more limited access.

Reuters notes that experts have raised concerns about independence when companies choose their own evaluators. And "model architecture" could cover a lot.

So the real test is METR's first report. METR says it "will openly describe the scope, access, and other limitations" of its work. Watch how much gets blacked out, and what METR says about it.
