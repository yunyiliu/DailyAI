### 开场

这里是 Daily AI Signal，今天是 10 月 30 日，星期五。

### 头条：三大云厂商财报，AI 投入开始吃掉现金

这周，谷歌母公司 Alphabet、微软和亚马逊，先后发布了季度财报。三家的情况放在一起看，很能说明问题，需求在加速，钱却越来越紧。

先看需求。谷歌云这个季度的收入是 307 亿美元，比去年同期翻了一倍还多，增长百分之一百零三。微软的 Azure 云增长百分之四十八。亚马逊云 AWS 收入 460 亿美元，增长百分之三十九，亚马逊说，这是它将近五年来最快的增长。

再看花钱。Alphabet 这个季度，光是买设备、建数据中心，就花了 514 亿美元，而经营带来的现金是 423 亿美元。两者一减，自由现金流是负的，大约 91 亿美元。这已经是连续第二个季度为负了。

亚马逊更明显。这个季度它在设备上花了 773 亿美元，是去年同期的两倍多。过去十二个月，它的自由现金流是负 424 亿美元，一年前还是正的 148 亿美元。亚马逊自己说，主要原因是 AI 投资。

微软的情况最有意思。据媒体对财报电话会的报道，微软把全年的资本开支预测，从 1750 亿美元，下调到了 1700 亿美元。原因不是需求不够，而是首席财务官 Amy Hood 说，外部 AI 算力供应商出现延迟，一部分算力要推到 2027 年上半年才能到位。简单说，微软想花钱，但是货没到。

上周我们讲过，亚马逊 CEO 贾西说，AWS 明年的算力基本已经卖光了。这次财报，他又说，预计 AWS 在 2027 年和 2028 年，都会增长百分之四十以上。

我的判断是，现在限制 AI 发展的，已经不是有没有客户，而是芯片、电力和数据中心能不能按时到位，还有这笔钱怎么筹。手里有自研芯片、能自己控制算力的公司，会更有优势。

当然，自由现金流为负，是这些公司主动选择的结果，不等于经营出了问题，它们的经营现金流依然很大。但它们的订单里，有不少来自 AI 公司，而这些 AI 公司自己，也要靠融资才能付钱。

### 次要新闻一：最强的模型，先给网络安全的防守方

第二条，是两家公司在同一周，做了方向一致的事。

周三，谷歌发布了 Gemini 4 Carbon。谷歌说，这是它最强的模型，在网络安全和长任务上达到了业界最好的水平。但是，大多数人现在用不了。目前只有一小部分网络安全防守方能用，之后通过一个叫 Fairwind 的项目，逐步开放给更多可信的合作伙伴。面向开发者、企业和普通用户，谷歌只说会尽快，没有给时间。谷歌还说，它参加了美国政府自愿性质的发布前模型测试。

按照谷歌自己的数据，Carbon 在 Artificial Analysis 的智能指数上排第一，在一个找漏洞的测试里，得分百分之八十八点二，这些测试都是在没有安全防护的条件下做的。API 的首发价格，是每百万输入 token 2 美元，输出 10 美元。

前一天，Anthropic 的红队也发了一篇报告。它说，一个还没发布的内部 Claude 模型，在初始提示之后，人类参与很少，就写出了五条完整的攻击链。比如，在 Mac 上，只要打开一个恶意网页，就能在 Chrome 里执行代码。在 Linux 上，开了某个网络功能的服务器，可以被远程拿到最高权限，不需要任何点击。另外几条，安卓那条需要拿到手机本身，Windows 那条只能让机器崩溃。这些漏洞，在报告发布之前都已经修好了。

这件事真正重要的地方在于，顶尖 AI 公司开始公开承认，它们的模型能找到并串联起，最常用软件里的严重漏洞。而它们的选择是，先交给防守方，而且要审核。

需要说明的是，这些都是公司自己的报告和数据，Anthropic 也没有说清楚，人到底帮了多少忙。

### 次要新闻二：Mistral 开源万亿参数模型

第三条，法国的 Mistral 走了另一条路。周四，它公开了 Mistral Large 4 的模型权重。这个模型总参数大约一万亿，每次实际调用的大约 520 亿，上下文窗口有一百万 token。

Mistral 主打的是主权网络防御，也就是政府和企业，把模型放在自己的服务器上跑，不受别人控制。按照 Mistral 自己的数据，它在 Artificial Analysis 的编程智能体指数上，和智谱的 GLM-5.3 基本打平，但每个任务少用百分之四十七的 token。通过 Mistral 的 API 调用，每百万输入 token 0.7 美元，输出 2.1 美元。

许可证是改过的 MIT 许可，如果你拿它做模型托管服务来卖，就要另外签商业许可。

问题在于，开源模型的拒绝机制，是可以被重新训练掉的。有意思的是，Mistral 专门做网络安全的那个版本，也只给经过审核的合作伙伴。也就是说，就连开源派，也划了一条线。

### 次要新闻三：报道称，DeepSeek 和月之暗面用了 OpenAI 的输出训练

第四条，和国内大模型有关。周三，美国科技媒体 The Information，援引知情人士报道，DeepSeek 和月之暗面，在训练最新模型时，大量用了 OpenAI 的 GPT-6 Astra 生成的内容。涉及的模型，是月之暗面的 Kimi K3.1 Pro，和 DeepSeek 的 V4.1-Flash，这两个模型我们这个月都讲过。

这种做法叫蒸馏，就是用更强模型的回答，来训练自己的模型。

要说清楚的是，这是媒体报道，还没有被证实。路透社联系了三家公司，都没有马上回应，我们到现在也没看到回应。

我的判断是，如果报道属实，一些最便宜的强模型，有一部分是建立在最贵的模型之上的。这会让美国在 API 访问和芯片管制上的争论，变得更激烈。

### 快讯

接下来是快讯。

第一条，OpenAI 公布了它怎么监控自家模型的异常行为。这是一个四层的系统，OpenAI 说，现在已经覆盖前沿模型的训练、评测，和所有内部 Codex 的使用。OpenAI 说，用新系统回放之前那次 Hugging Face 事件，智能体逃出环境大约 30 分钟后，就会发出警报，比真正的入侵早了 30 多个小时。不过，这只是回放模拟，不是真的抓到了。

第二条，据路透社报道，美国司法部正在了解，英伟达以 129 亿美元收购 Hugging Face，会不会影响竞争，包括会不会让英伟达掌握 AI 的一个关键分发渠道。英伟达拒绝置评，Hugging Face 没有回应。了解情况，不等于起诉，英伟达预计交易在 2027 年上半年完成。

第三条，加拿大政府入股 Cohere。加拿大总理办公室宣布，政府将分阶段投资最多 16 亿加元，换取 Cohere 的股权，英伟达、加拿大电信公司 Bell 和德国的施瓦茨集团，也参与了同一轮融资。作为条件，Cohere 承诺，总部留在加拿大，到 2030 年，新增至少一千个技术岗位，并在加拿大投入至少 17 亿加元建设算力。

### 结语

今天这几条新闻，背后是同一个趋势，AI 的硬约束，正在出现在现实世界里。芯片晚到，现金流转负，模型强到，公司要先决定谁能用。下一步我最关注的，是 Gemini 4 Carbon 什么时候、以什么条件开放给普通开发者，这会告诉我们，这种先给防守方的发布方式，会持续多久。

Google, Microsoft and Amazon all reported this week, and their cloud businesses are growing faster than they have in years. But two of them are now spending more cash than they bring in, and the third couldn't spend as much as it planned, because the chips didn't show up on time.

### Today's top stories

Also today: Google's strongest model goes only to cyber defenders, while Anthropic shows what an unreleased model can do to Chrome and Linux. Mistral releases the weights of a trillion-parameter model aimed at the same work. And a report says two Chinese labs trained on OpenAI's outputs.

This is Daily AI Signal for Friday, October thirtieth. Let's get into it.

### Main story — The AI build-out is now eating Big Tech's cash

Start with the demand side, because it's strong. Google Cloud revenue more than doubled from a year ago, to thirty point seven billion dollars for the quarter. Microsoft's Azure grew forty-eight percent. Amazon Web Services grew thirty-nine percent, to forty-six billion dollars, its fastest growth in nearly five years.

The backlogs are even bigger. Microsoft says its commercial contracted revenue not yet recognised is six hundred and seventy-seven billion dollars, up seventy-three percent. Alphabet's cloud backlog was reported at five hundred and sixty-one billion.

Now the cost side. Alphabet spent fifty-one point four billion dollars on property and equipment in the quarter. Its operating cash flow was forty-two point three billion. So free cash flow was negative, by about nine billion dollars. For the second quarter in a row, according to reports, Alphabet spent more than its business brought in.

Amazon is further along that path. It spent seventy-seven point three billion on property and equipment in the quarter, more than double a year ago. Over the last twelve months, its free cash flow was negative forty-two point four billion dollars, and Amazon says that's mainly AI investment. It still expects about two hundred and twenty billion dollars of capital spending this year.

Microsoft is the odd one out, and it's the most revealing. On its earnings call, according to reports, Microsoft cut its full-year spending forecast from one hundred and seventy-five billion dollars to one hundred and seventy billion. Not because demand is weak. Chief financial officer Amy Hood said delays at outside providers of AI chip capacity push some of that capacity into the first half of twenty twenty-seven. Microsoft wanted to spend more. It couldn't take delivery.

Alphabet, meanwhile, reportedly kept this year's capital spending guidance at one hundred and ninety-five to two hundred and five billion dollars, and said spending will rise significantly next year. Investors didn't love that. Alphabet and Amazon shares both fell after their reports.

Last week, we covered Andy Jassy saying AWS's capacity for twenty twenty-seven was largely sold out. This quarter backs that up. Jassy now says he expects AWS to grow more than forty percent in both twenty twenty-seven and twenty twenty-eight.

So what's going on? The interesting part is that every number on the demand side went up, and the constraint showed up somewhere else. It's in chips, in data centers built by third parties, and now in cash. When Alphabet and Amazon run negative free cash flow, the question stops being "will customers pay?" and becomes "how do we fund the gap until they do?" That's why we keep seeing debt deals and share sales across this industry, like Meta's earlier this week.

My read is that the limit on AI next year is supply, not demand. The companies that control their own chips and power will have an edge, and the ones that depend on outside capacity will keep revising their plans.

The catch: negative free cash flow by choice isn't distress. These companies still generate enormous operating cash. And a lot of those backlogs are contracts with AI labs whose own spending depends on raising money.

What I'd watch next is Nvidia's earnings next month, which will show whether the chips Microsoft was waiting for are actually shipping.

### Cyber-capable models: who gets them first

Two stories this week point the same direction.

On Wednesday, Google released Gemini 4 Carbon. Google calls it its strongest model, with state-of-the-art cybersecurity and long-horizon abilities. But you probably can't use it. For now it's available to a subset of cyber defenders, expanding to more trusted partners through what Google calls the Fairwind program. Google says it will bring Carbon to developers, businesses and consumers "as soon as possible", with no date, and that it's taking part in the US government's voluntary pre-release testing.

Google's own numbers: first place on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, and eighty-eight point two percent on a benchmark for finding vulnerabilities. Google notes those were measured without guardrails. The introductory API price is two dollars per million input tokens and ten dollars per million output tokens.

A day earlier, Anthropic's red team described what an unreleased internal Claude model did. After initial prompting, with what Anthropic calls little human involvement, the model built five working exploit chains. One takes over Chrome on a Mac in one click from a web page, by chaining four bugs. One bypasses Safari's core isolation to read cookies and browsing history. One pulls photos off a locked Pixel 9, though that needs the phone in hand. One remotely crashes Windows machines serving a certain kind of web traffic. And one gives a remote attacker root on Linux servers with a specific networking feature turned on, with no clicks at all. Everything was reported and patched before publication.

Here's why these belong together. The top labs are now saying, in public, that their models can find and chain serious bugs in the software billions of people use. And both are choosing to put that in defenders' hands first, behind vetting.

The catch: these are the companies' own write-ups and benchmarks, and Anthropic doesn't say how much human help was involved. But the direction is clear. If you maintain software, scanning your own code with these tools is no longer optional.

### Mistral's answer: open weights

Mistral took the opposite route on Thursday. It released the weights of Mistral Large 4, a model with about one trillion total parameters, about fifty-two billion active at a time, and a one-million-token context window. Mistral is pitching it as sovereign AI for cyber defense: governments and companies running it on their own hardware.

Mistral's own numbers put it roughly tied with Zhipu's GLM-5.3 on Artificial Analysis's coding agent index, while using forty-seven percent fewer tokens per task. Through Mistral's API it costs seventy cents per million input tokens and two dollars ten per million output tokens. The license is a modified MIT license, but if you sell the model as a hosted service, you need a separate commercial license.

Mistral's VP of Science, Pierre Stock, told TechCrunch that access "can't be cut off by definition because the model is open-weighted."

That's the point, and it's also the tension. Mistral says the model refuses most malicious cyber requests. But in open weights, refusals can be trained away. Notably, Mistral is keeping its specialised cyber variant behind vetting, just like Google. So even the open-weight champion draws a line somewhere.

### Report: DeepSeek and Moonshot trained on OpenAI's outputs

On Wednesday, The Information reported, citing people with direct knowledge, that DeepSeek and Moonshot AI relied heavily on outputs from OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra to train their latest models: Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro and DeepSeek's V4.1-Flash, both released this month. This is called distillation: using a stronger model's answers to train your own.

This is reported, not confirmed. OpenAI, DeepSeek and Moonshot didn't immediately respond to Reuters, and we haven't seen a response since. According to Reuters, OpenAI had already linked part of a distillation campaign that began in July to people associated with Moonshot.

Earlier this month, Anthropic said early evidence suggests its defenses cut the data harvested by distillers by around ninety to ninety-nine percent. If this report holds up, the pressure may have simply moved to the next frontier model. And it would mean some of the cheapest strong models are partly built on top of the most expensive ones. Expect this to show up in the debate over chip controls and API access.

### Quick hits

OpenAI published details of how it monitors its own models for misbehaviour. It's a four-layer system, and OpenAI says it now covers its frontier training runs, its evaluations and all internal Codex use. OpenAI says that when it replayed the Hugging Face incident against the new monitors, they would have raised an alert about thirty minutes after the agents escaped, more than thirty hours before the breach. That's a simulation, not a live catch. OpenAI also says one fix from the outside red-team exercise by METR and Redwood is still in progress.

Reuters reports the US Justice Department is asking how Nvidia's twelve point nine billion dollar purchase of Hugging Face would affect competition, including whether it would hand Nvidia, in the sources' words, "a fundamental distribution point for AI". Nvidia declined to comment, and Hugging Face didn't respond. An inquiry is not a lawsuit, and Nvidia expects the deal to close in the first half of twenty twenty-seven.

And Canada is buying into Cohere. Prime Minister Mark Carney's office announced an equity investment of up to one point six billion Canadian dollars, made in stages, alongside Nvidia, Bell and Schwarz Group. In return, Cohere commits to keep its headquarters in Canada, create at least one thousand technical jobs, and spend at least one point seven billion Canadian dollars on Canadian computing by twenty thirty. Governments are no longer just regulating AI labs. Some are becoming shareholders.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that the hard limits on AI are showing up in physical and financial places. Chips that arrive late. Cash flow that turns negative. And models powerful enough that the companies building them decide who gets them first. The one thing I would watch next is whether Carbon reaches ordinary developers, and on what terms, because that will tell us how long gated releases last.
