### 头条：Nscale 上市遇阻，微软退出英国项目

今天的头条，是 AI 数据中心公司 Nscale。

Nscale 是一家英国公司，英伟达是它的投资方。它的生意是建数据中心，把算力租给客户，和上周我们讲过的 CoreWeave 是同一类公司，业内叫“新型云”，英文是 neocloud。9 月，它申请在纽约上市。

周日，彭博社援引知情人士报道，Nscale 的上市估值遇到了阻力。公司的目标大约是 300 亿美元，但有一批投资人建议，定在 160 亿到 190 亿美元之间，差不多砍掉一半。投资人提到的原因之一，是澳大利亚 AI 数据中心公司 Firmus 的上市计划泡汤了。

同一天，英国《每日电讯报》报道，微软退出了 Nscale 在英国埃塞克斯郡劳顿的数据中心项目。微软是这个项目唯一公开的客户，原本要在那里租用大约 2.3 万颗英伟达 AI 芯片。据报道，电网方面告诉 Nscale，这个地方可能要到 2030 年代，才能接上电网。微软没有公开回应。据报道，Nscale 表示，会继续推进这个项目，正在寻找其他电力来源。

为什么说这件事，不只是一个园区的问题？看 Nscale 的上市文件。它列出的合同总额，大约 1030 亿美元，其中大约百分之八十五，来自两个客户。微软大约 438 亿美元，期限到 2033 年。Anthropic 在 8 月签了一份 446 亿美元的协议，地点在美国西弗吉尼亚州。但截至 8 月底，真正开始执行的合同，只有大约 26 亿美元。今年上半年，Nscale 收入 1.4 亿美元，亏损超过 10 亿美元。而且提交文件的时候，给 Anthropic 建算力所需要的融资，还没有落实。

这个周末，两个原本分开的风险，同时出现了。一个是电力。以前卡脖子的是芯片，现在一个园区有了客户，也可能好几年接不上电。另一个是价格。公开市场的投资人在说，合同金额再大，如果大部分还没建、也还没钱建，就不能按面值算。

我的判断是，市场开始按“能不能建成”来给这类公司定价，而不是按合同有多少。上周我们讲过，CoreWeave 的债券收益率接近百分之十五，股价一周跌了大约四分之一。Nscale 面对的是同一个问题，只不过换成了上市的形式。

当然也要看到，这是在谈价格，上市并没有取消。Nscale 和微软在挪威、葡萄牙和美国的项目，还在继续合作。一个园区接不上电，是当地电网的问题，不能说明需求没了。

接下来要看，Nscale 最后定的发行价区间是多少，以及微软和 Anthropic 这两个大客户的合同，会不会有变化。

### 银行拦下外部 AI 智能体

第二条，和每个人的钱包有关。

上周五，美国券商嘉信理财的首席执行官里克·伍斯特，接受彭博电视采访时说，嘉信会拦截那些想动客户资金的、未经授权的 AI 智能体，其中就包括 Meta 的智能体 Muse。他的理由很直接，我们控制不了它，客户也控制不了它，一旦出了问题，谁来负责？他说，他每周都会收到一份拦截报告。

嘉信并不是完全拒绝 AI。它计划搭建 MCP 服务器。MCP，就是让 AI 模型连接外部系统的一种标准接口。通过它，客户自己选的 AI，可以查余额、做研究，但不能转钱。要做更多的事，嘉信希望客户用它自己的助手。

摩根大通也是同样的态度。上周二的财报电话会上，杰米·戴蒙提醒要警惕“失控的智能体”。摩根大通表示，外部智能体不能替零售客户操作，但余额这类只读数据，还可以通过 Plaid 这样的服务查看。9 月，亚马逊也拦截过 Muse，理由是这个智能体没有表明自己的身份。

我的判断是，在法律说清楚“智能体出错谁来赔”之前，AI 智能体能看你的账户，但动钱的事，还是留在银行自己的助手手里。反过来说，很多这类智能体，是直接用客户的密码登录的，拦下它们，本来就是正常的安全措施。银行也有自己的商业考虑，希望你留在它的 App 里。

### Anthropic 拿出百分之五的股份

第三条，是 Anthropic。

周日，Anthropic 宣布，要把公司百分之五的股份，投入一个新的公益基金，这还需要股东批准。公司估值在某个基准之上每涨百分之五十，董事会还可以再追加两个百分点。这个基准是多少，Anthropic 没有公布。

这个基金的形式，是 Anthropic 全资拥有的有限责任公司，不是慈善机构。Anthropic 的说法是，这样在怎么捐、捐给谁上，更灵活。它举的用途包括，向非营利机构大额捐款，直接向特定人群发钱，以及应对 AI 对就业的冲击。基金持有的股份，不能用于游说，也不能用于政治捐款。Anthropic 说，科技公司通常会给高管不断加码的薪酬奖励，而它把这部分股份，投向了公益。加上联合创始人此前承诺捐出百分之八十的个人股份，以及员工捐赠配捐，大约百分之二十的股份，会用于公益。

有意思的是它的设计，公司越值钱，拿出来的比例就越高。不能用于政治的规定，也提前回应了外界最容易提出的质疑。但限制也很明显，股东还没批准，谁来管、怎么管，Anthropic 说会在未来几个月公布，追加的部分，由董事会决定。而且这个时间点，正好在它据报道准备上市之前。

### Meta 删掉“训练”这个词

第四条，和内容创作者、媒体有关。

上周，美国媒体 Nieman Lab 报道，有法律专家认为，Meta 新的商业条款，可能允许它抓取和平台上商家有关的网站，包括新闻网站，再拿这些内容训练 AI。Meta 否认这些条款是为了抓取新闻网站。新条款 10 月 30 日生效。

这几天，Meta 改了条款。据土耳其阿纳多卢通讯社报道，Meta 删掉了用这些内容“训练”自家产品的说法，改成了开发广告和商业产品。Meta 发言人说，这次修改不会改变它使用网络爬虫的方式，网站可以继续用 robots.txt 文件，表明是否允许抓取。至于更大范围的抓取条款还剩多少，各家报道说法不一，我们等最终文本出来再看。

这件事说明，“训练”这个词，现在一写进条款就会引发争议。媒体只用了几天，就让 Meta 让了步。

### 快讯

最后是两条快讯。

OpenAI 的山姆·奥特曼，在一档播客里被问到，OpenAI 会不会造人形机器人，他的回答是，会。他说，机器人的大脑比身体更重要。他此前说过，预计 2027 年会有一个很酷的演示，但机器人真正走上街头，还要再等几年。

另外，被控在 OpenAI 高管会议室安装录音设备的那名工程师，今天在联邦法院出席羁押听证会，结果出来后我们再告诉大家。

### 结语

今天这些新闻背后，更大的趋势是，AI 的扩张，正在碰上真正承担风险的那些人。电网保证不了供电，上市的投资人不愿意为还没建成的合同付全价，银行在弄清谁负责之前，不让智能体动钱。

接下来我最想看的，是 Nscale 的发行价区间。CoreWeave 刚经历了糟糕的一周，这个价格，是对公开市场怎么看新型云公司的第一次检验。

### Today's top stories

### Main story — Nscale's IPO meets resistance

Nscale is a British company backed by Nvidia. It builds data centers and rents out the computing power, the same kind of business as CoreWeave. People in this industry call these companies neoclouds. Nscale filed to go public in New York in September.

On Sunday, Bloomberg reported that Nscale is meeting resistance on price. According to people familiar with the matter, the company has been aiming for a valuation of about thirty billion dollars. A group of investors has urged a range of sixteen to nineteen billion. That's roughly half. One reason they gave was the collapse of the IPO plan of Firmus, an Australian AI data center company.

The same day, the Telegraph reported that Microsoft has pulled out of Nscale's planned data center in Loughton, in Essex. Microsoft was the only customer announced for that site, and it had agreed to rent about twenty-three thousand Nvidia AI chips there. According to reports, Nscale was told the site might not get a grid connection until the twenty-thirties. Microsoft hasn't commented publicly. Nscale has said, according to reports, that it's still committed to the site and is looking at other power sources.

Here's why that matters more than one building in Essex. Nscale's IPO filing lists about a hundred and three billion dollars in contracts. About eighty-five percent of that comes from two customers. Microsoft accounts for about forty-four billion through twenty thirty-three. Anthropic, which makes Claude, signed an agreement in August worth forty-four point six billion, at a site in West Virginia. At the end of August, only about two point six billion of the hundred and three billion was active. In the first half of this year, Nscale had a hundred and forty million dollars in revenue and lost just over a billion. And when it filed, it had not yet secured the financing it needs to build the Anthropic capacity.

So what changed this weekend? Two things that were separate risks have now arrived together. One is power. Chips used to be the bottleneck, and now a site can have a customer and still have no grid connection for years. The other is price. Public investors are telling a neocloud that a large contract backlog isn't worth face value if most of it is still unbuilt and unfinanced.

My read is that the market is starting to price these companies on their execution risk, not their backlog. Last week we covered CoreWeave's bond yields near fifteen percent, and then a week in which its stock fell about a quarter. Nscale is the same question in IPO form. Will the long contracts from a few big AI buyers actually get built, financed and paid for?

The catch is that this is a negotiation, not a cancelled IPO. Nscale and Microsoft still work together on other projects, including in Norway, Portugal and the U.S. One site without power is a local grid problem, not proof that demand is gone.

What to watch: whether Nscale sets a price range, and where. Also watch for any change to the Anthropic and Microsoft contracts, since those two customers hold up most of the company's value.

### Banks block outside AI agents

Next, the agents that are supposed to do things for you are hitting a wall at the bank.

On Friday, Charles Schwab's chief executive, Rick Wurster, told Bloomberg Television that Schwab blocks unauthorized AI agents that try to move client money, and he named Meta's Muse agent among them. His explanation was simple: "We can't control it, the client can't control it, something could go wrong and who's liable?" He said he gets a weekly report on how often agents are blocked, and it's in the tens and twenties each week.

Schwab isn't shutting AI out completely. It plans to set up MCP servers. MCP, or Model Context Protocol, is a standard way for AI models to connect to outside systems. Through those servers, a client's chosen AI could check balances and do research, but not move money. For anything more, Schwab wants you to use its own assistant, called Charley.

JPMorgan is in the same place. On the bank's earnings call last Tuesday, Jamie Dimon warned about "rogue agents," and the bank says outside agents can't act for its retail customers. They can still see read-only data like balances through services such as Plaid. In September, Amazon blocked Meta's Muse from its store too, saying the agent didn't identify itself.

This matters because the big promise of consumer agents is that they act: they book, buy and pay. But the accounts that matter most are run by companies that carry the risk, and their answer right now is to let agents read and not act. My read is that until the law says who pays when an agent makes a mistake, consumer agents get to read your financial data, and moving money stays with the bank's own assistant. The counterpoint is that some of this is ordinary security, since many of these agents log in with the customer's password. And banks do have a business reason to keep you inside their own app.

### Anthropic's public-benefit fund

Now Anthropic. On Sunday it said it will commit five percent of its shares to a new Anthropic Public Benefit Fund. That needs approval from its stockholders. The board can also add two more percentage points for every fifty percent rise in the company's value above a starting point. Anthropic hasn't said what that starting point is.

The fund is set up as a company Anthropic wholly owns, a limited liability company, not a charity. Anthropic says that gives it room to choose between different kinds of giving. The examples it lists are large donations to nonprofits, direct payments to specific groups of people, and work on the effects of AI on jobs, where it says "bolder approaches may be needed." None of the fund's equity can be used for lobbying or political giving, in the US or abroad. Anthropic points out that tech companies commonly give their executives escalating pay awards, and says it's directing that equity to the public instead. Together with its co-founders' earlier pledge to give away eighty percent of their own shares and an employee donation match, it says roughly twenty percent of its shares will be dedicated to the public good.

The interesting part is the design. The amount set aside grows as the company's value grows, so the giving is tied to how well the company does. And the no-politics rule addresses the obvious criticism before anyone makes it. The limits are real, though. Stockholders haven't approved it yet. Anthropic hasn't said who will run it or how it will be governed, and it says those details come in the coming months. The extra points are up to the board. And it comes just before an IPO the company is reportedly preparing, which invites the reading that part of this is about reputation.

### Meta changes its crawling terms

One more for publishers. Last week, Nieman Lab reported that legal experts said Meta's new business terms could let it scrape websites linked to businesses on its platforms, including news sites, and use that content to train AI. Meta denied that the terms were about scraping news sites. The terms take effect on October thirtieth.

In the past few days, Meta changed the text. According to Anadolu, Meta removed the language about using that content to train its products. It now talks about developing advertising and business products. A Meta spokesperson said the change doesn't alter how it uses web crawlers, and that site owners can still use robots dot text to set their preferences. Reports differ on how much of the wider crawling clause remains, so I'll leave that open until the final text is out.

The reason it matters is that "train" is now the word that sets off a fight in terms of service, and publishers got Meta to back down on it in a few days.

The one thing I'd watch next is Nscale's price range. It's the first real test of how public investors value a neocloud after CoreWeave's bad week.
