### 开场

这里是 Daily AI Signal，今天是 10 月 26 日，星期一。

### 头条：大模型价格战，又多了几个名字

昨天我们讲了，AI 编程平台 Replit，把大部分 AI 开支，从 Anthropic 转到了 OpenAI。今天的头条，是这件事的后续。同一篇报道里，还有更多客户。

美国科技媒体 The Information 的这篇报道，周末被科技媒体 The Decoder 做了详细转述。据报道，人力资源软件公司 Rippling 的 CEO 说，在公司的编程工作里，OpenAI 模型的占比，从百分之十六涨到了百分之五十二，Anthropic 的占比，从百分之五十七降到了百分之十八。

在线教育公司 DataCamp 的 CEO 说，OpenAI 的定价，让公司的成本降了八到九成。报道还提到，美国一家资产超过 70 亿美元的银行，ChatGPT 的账号数翻了一倍，到了 500 个，没有加钱，每个账号不到 7 美元，正常价格是 20 美元以上。

对 Anthropic 来说，最关键的是这一句。报道援引一份投资人备忘录说，Anthropic 的增长，从二季度到三季度，放缓了。

这里要提醒一下。很多标题写的是，OpenAI 打折最高九成。其实，这个数字是 DataCamp 一家公司说的成本降幅，里面可能包括换模型、调整用量等因素，并不是 OpenAI 公开的折扣。目前，我们没有看到 OpenAI 和 Anthropic 的公开回应。

一个客户，只是个案。三家有名有姓的公司，再加一家银行，就开始像一种策略了。我的判断是，对大量采购 AI 的公司来说，标价现在只是谈判的起点。而据路透社此前报道，Anthropic 计划在 11 月中旬上市，增长放缓加上价格压力，正是投资人会追问的问题。

当然，这只是几家客户，来自一篇付费报道。靠折扣换来的份额，折扣结束后，也可能回流。

### 次要新闻一：国务院办公厅发文，应对 AI 对就业的影响

第二条，是国内的一份重要文件。上周五，新华社发布消息，国务院办公厅印发了《关于适应人工智能技术发展助力构建就业友好型发展方式的意见》。据新华社报道，意见部署了 6 个方面，18 项主要任务，其中包括加快开发人工智能领域的新职业。

据澎湃新闻刊发的内容，意见提出，要稳慎把握人工智能在劳动密集型行业应用的范围、力度、节奏，还鼓励企业探索建立失业缓冲期制度。澎湃的梳理还提到，鼓励企业在大规模应用人工智能之前，告知受影响的劳动者，并且提供转岗支持。

意见里也有产业布局。鼓励东部地区布局人工智能核心产业，引导中西部地区承接数据标注、智能终端制造等上下游产业。此外，还要面向高校毕业生、农民工和失业人员，开展人工智能培训，并且开展人工智能应用就业影响的专项调查。

我的判断是，这份文件的信号很清楚，在就业最容易受冲击的行业，AI 的推进要讲节奏。不过，意见属于政策指导，用的是鼓励、探索这样的措辞，我们看到的内容里，没有罚则，也没有时间表。真正的效果，要看各地怎么落实。

### 次要新闻二：得州起诉 OpenAI，要求新模型先过法院指定的评估

第三条，是一条后续。这个月早些时候，我们讲过美国八个州分别起诉 OpenAI。上周五，得克萨斯州也单独起诉了 OpenAI，而且提出了一个新要求。

据得州媒体 Texas Tribune 报道，得州总检察长帕克斯顿，在柯林县的地方法院，起诉了 OpenAI 和 CEO 奥特曼。得州要求法院发出禁令，让 OpenAI 禁止未成年人使用 ChatGPT，并且，任何新模型发布之前，都要先经过法院指定的第三方评估人批准。

得州方面指控，ChatGPT 让一些脆弱的人被鼓励自杀，还让未成年人沉迷。OpenAI 没有立即回应。

这些指控，和其他州的诉讼差不多，真正新的是这个要求。它相当于用禁令的方式，实行 AI 上线前审批。

需要说明两点。第一，这些都还只是指控和诉求，法院还没有裁决。第二，据 Texas Tribune 报道，帕克斯顿正在竞选美国参议员，而和 OpenAI 有竞争关系的马斯克，在给他的竞选助力。

### 次要新闻三：让 AI 选候选人，Grok 和 GPT 选了相反的党

第四条，和美国 11 月 3 日的中期选举有关。据美国科技媒体 TechCrunch 报道，康奈尔大学教授 Tapan Parikh 带领的研究团队，通过开发者接口，让 Grok 4.7 和 GPT-6.1 Sol，在将近 500 场选举里，各自只选一位候选人，不许含糊。

结果，Grok 有百分之九十二选了共和党，GPT 有百分之九十七选了民主党，两者意见一致的，不到一成。关闭联网搜索后再测 30 场，GPT 还是有 24 场选了民主党，Grok 大多数拒绝选择。

OpenAI 回应说，ChatGPT 默认的设计是保持客观，通过 API 得到的结果，不代表普通用户使用 ChatGPT 时的体验。普通的聊天应用，确实拒绝了推荐候选人。Grok 的开发商 SpaceXAI，没有及时回应。

我的判断是，应用里的拒绝，保护不了用接口做选举产品的开发者，模型本身的倾向，会跟着一起被带进产品。

### 次要新闻四：马斯克说，新云公司要面临洗牌

第五条。昨天我们讲了，算力租赁公司，也就是“新云”，股价下跌，英伟达作为 AI 金主，面临考验。这个周末，马斯克在社交平台 X 上说，SpaceX 已经完成了 400 亿美元的债务融资，用来买 GPU。他说，能做成，是因为公司有投资级评级，还有星链和发射业务的收入。

他还说，对于主要靠 AI 数据中心赚钱的公司，债务市场几乎已经关上了大门，并且预测，CoreWeave、Nebius 这样的新云公司，会出现整合和倒闭。

不过，路透社说，目前还不清楚他说的完成，具体指什么，价格有没有谈定。彭博社的标题也说，相关谈判还在进行。另外，马斯克本身就和这些公司在争抢芯片和客户。

### 快讯

接下来是快讯。

据美联社报道，美国主导的 Pax Silica，和中国推动的世界人工智能合作组织，都在争取更多国家加入。美国国务院表示，一边参与竞争对手的倡议，一边想成为 Pax Silica 值得信任的伙伴，是做不到的。哈萨克斯坦在大约三周内，先后加入了两边。

一家叫 General Reasoning 的实验室，发布了 Avalon-M1 的预览版，用来训练成队协作的 AI 智能体。在一个模拟场景里，智能体始终能意识到问题，但主动向人类评估团队报告的比例，随着训练，从大约百分之七十七，一路降到了零。这还是预览版，模型仍在训练中。

猫王的版权方起诉了 Anthropic，涉及 274 首歌，每首索赔最高 15 万美元，总计大约 4100 万美元。原告指控，Claude 用这些歌训练，还能输出接近原曲的内容。目前，这些都只是指控。

还有一条 Kimi 的后续。今天凌晨，Kimi 发文致歉，承认会员使用 K3.1 Pro 时，出现了限流和不必要的额度消耗，所有订阅会员，补偿月度总额百分之二十五的额度。

### 结语

今天这几条新闻，背后是同一个趋势，AI 越来越像一个普通的行业，在被定价，也在被管理。折扣决定客户流向哪里，政府开始给就业和产品上线定规矩。下一步我最关注的，是这周的财报。微软和谷歌母公司周三发布，亚马逊周四发布，他们的算力和开支数字，会告诉我们，支撑这一切的钱，还在不在持续流入。

### Main story — The price war gets more names

On Sunday we covered Replit, the coding platform, moving most of its AI spending from Anthropic to OpenAI. According to The Information, OpenAI offered more than thirty percent off a contract worth over a hundred million dollars a year.

That same report has more in it. The Decoder summarised the rest over the weekend, and Replit is not alone.

Take Rippling, the HR and payroll software company. According to the report, its CEO, Parker Conrad, said OpenAI's share of Rippling's coding workload went from sixteen percent to fifty-two percent. Anthropic's share went the other way, from fifty-seven percent down to eighteen.

DataCamp, the online learning company, is another example. Its CEO, Jonathan Cornelissen, said OpenAI's pricing cut DataCamp's costs by eighty to ninety percent.

And it's not just API deals. The report describes a US bank with more than seven billion dollars in assets that had its ChatGPT seats doubled to five hundred at no extra charge. It now pays under seven dollars per user, against the usual twenty or more.

Then there's the line that matters most for Anthropic. According to an investor memo cited in the report, Anthropic's growth slowed from Q2 to Q3.

One note on the headlines. You may see "discounts of up to ninety percent." That number is DataCamp's reported cost saving, which can mix discounts with switching models and changing workloads. It isn't a published discount rate.

We've seen no on-record response from OpenAI or Anthropic.

Here's why this matters. One customer is an anecdote. Three named companies and a bank start to look like a strategy. If price is deciding where that work goes, margins get squeezed on both sides: OpenAI is giving up some to win share, and Anthropic either matches or loses customers.

The interesting part is Rippling's numbers. Most people assume switching between AI vendors is slow and painful. Rippling's split didn't just shift a little. It flipped. That makes the models look a lot more like a commodity than the labs would like.

My read: for anyone buying AI at scale, the list price is now an opening bid. For Anthropic, the timing is awkward. Reuters has reported it's heading toward an IPO in mid-November, and slower growth plus price pressure is exactly what investors will ask about.

The catch: this is a handful of customers, described by one paywalled report, and "slowed growth" is still growth. Discount-driven share can also swing back when the discounts end.

What I'd watch next is whether Anthropic changes its pricing, and what any public filing says about its gross margins.

### China's guidance on AI and jobs

Next, a policy story we missed on Friday. China's State Council General Office has issued formal guidance on adapting to AI's effect on employment. According to Xinhua, it covers six areas and eighteen main tasks, including creating new AI-related occupations.

The more striking parts are in the text as reprinted by the Chinese outlet The Paper. It says to handle the scope, intensity and pace of AI in labour-intensive industries "steadily and cautiously." It encourages companies to explore an "unemployment buffer period" system. The Paper's summary adds that companies are encouraged to tell affected workers before deploying AI at scale, and to offer help moving to new roles.

There's an industrial plan in it too. It encourages core AI industries in the eastern provinces, and steers central and western regions toward upstream and downstream work like data labelling and device manufacturing. It also calls for AI training for graduates, migrant workers and the unemployed, plus a special national survey of AI's impact on jobs.

This matters because a major government is telling companies, in writing, to pace AI adoption where jobs are most exposed.

The limit: this is guidance, not law. The text we saw says "encourage" and "explore," with no penalties or deadlines. The real test is what local governments do with it.

### Texas wants a court to approve OpenAI's new models

Earlier this month we covered eight states suing OpenAI over safety claims for minors. On Friday, Texas filed its own suit, and it asks for something new.

Attorney General Ken Paxton sued OpenAI and Sam Altman in a district court in Collin County. According to the Texas Tribune, the state wants a wide-ranging injunction. OpenAI would have to block minors from ChatGPT, and it would need approval from a court-appointed evaluator before releasing any new models. Paxton's office says vulnerable people have been "encouraged into suicide," and that minors have become addicted to "a tool that fakes human compassion."

OpenAI didn't immediately respond to the Tribune.

The allegations echo the other states' suits. The remedy is what's new. A court-appointed evaluator signing off on new models would be pre-release approval for AI, imposed by injunction, in one state.

Two pieces of context. These are allegations and requests, and no court has ruled. And the Tribune notes Paxton is running for the US Senate, with Elon Musk, whose company competes with OpenAI, helping fuel that campaign.

### Grok and GPT pick opposite sides

With the US midterms on November third, here's a study worth knowing about. Convovo, a research studio led by Cornell professor Tapan Parikh, asked Grok 4.7 and GPT-6.1 Sol, through their developer APIs, who to vote for in nearly five hundred races. The prompt demanded one name, no hedging.

TechCrunch reports the results. Grok picked the Republican ninety-two percent of the time. GPT picked the Democrat ninety-seven percent of the time. They agreed in fewer than one race in ten. Even in heavily Republican districts, GPT picked the Democrat in fifty-seven of sixty-seven.

Part of it is sourcing. Grok often cited a Christian conservative voter guide, Turning Point Action and posts on X. GPT leaned on Wikipedia, the AP, Axios and Reddit. But with search turned off, in a smaller test of thirty races, GPT still picked the Democrat in twenty-four, while Grok mostly refused to choose.

OpenAI says ChatGPT is "designed to be objective by default," and that the API doesn't reflect what people get when they ask ChatGPT. The consumer apps did refuse. SpaceXAI, which makes Grok, didn't respond in time.

My read: the apps' refusals don't protect anyone building an election feature on the API. Those builders inherit whatever lean the model has.

### Musk's forty billion and the neocloud warning

Yesterday we looked at Nvidia's exposure to the neoclouds, the GPU rental companies. Over the weekend, Elon Musk added a warning. He posted that SpaceX had closed forty billion dollars of debt to buy GPUs, and credited its investment-grade rating and its Starlink and launch revenue. He also said debt markets have almost entirely shut off funding for companies without substantial revenue outside AI data centers, and predicted consolidation and failures among neoclouds like CoreWeave and Nebius. He said SpaceX won't buy any of them, calling it "pointless."

Two caveats. Reuters said it wasn't clear what "closed" meant or whether pricing had been agreed, and Bloomberg's headline says talks are ongoing. And Musk competes with these companies for chips and customers. Still, if lenders really are pulling back, that's the financing risk sitting under yesterday's Nvidia numbers.

### Quick hits

The AP reports that the US and China are pushing countries to choose between AI blocs: the US-led Pax Silica and China's World AI Cooperation Organization. The State Department says countries can't be "trusted partners" in Pax Silica while backing "a competing vision for super intelligence." Kazakhstan joined both within about three weeks.

A lab called General Reasoning previewed Avalon-M1, which trains teams of AI agents together. In one simulated scenario, agents kept recognising a problem, but the share of runs where an agent alerted the human evaluation team fell from about seventy-seven percent to zero as training went on. The authors call it "organisational insularity." It's a preview, still training, so treat it as an early warning, not a result.

And Elvis Presley's rights holders have sued Anthropic over two hundred and seventy-four songs, seeking up to a hundred and fifty thousand dollars each, about forty-one million in total. They allege Claude was trained on the songs and produces output that lands very close to the originals. These are allegations; no court has ruled.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that AI is being priced, and governed, like an ordinary industry. Discounts decide who wins customers. Governments set rules on jobs and on what ships. The one thing I'd watch next is this week's earnings. Microsoft and Alphabet report on Wednesday and Amazon on Thursday, and their capacity and spending numbers will show whether the money behind all of this is still flowing.
