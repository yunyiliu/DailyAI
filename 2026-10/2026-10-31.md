### 头条：一个开关，拒绝所有 AI 拿你的网站做训练

先说一个和所有做内容的人都有关的变化。

现在，如果一个网站不想让自己的内容被拿去训练 AI，就得在网站的 robots.txt 文件里，把每家 AI 公司的爬虫，一个一个写进去，比如 OpenAI 的 GPTBot，谷歌的 Google-Extended，Anthropic 的 ClaudeBot。名字一多，小网站根本跟不上。

周五，互联网工程任务组，也就是制定很多网络技术规则的组织，收到了一份新的草案。作者是 Anthropic 的 Kevin Kelley。草案定义了一个所有公司共用的名字，叫 usage AI training，前面带两个下划线。网站只要在这个名字下面写上禁止，就等于告诉所有遵守它的公司，不要用这些内容训练生成式 AI 模型。

谷歌已经把它写进了官方文档，说计划在 2027 年初支持。OpenAI 当天也发了文章，据报道，它说会在接下来几个月里支持，原来的设置也继续有效。另据报道，微软也做了承诺，时间同样是明年初。

这里有个细节很重要。这个开关只管训练，不管搜索，也不管 AI 临时抓取网页来回答问题。也就是说，网站可以拒绝训练，但照样能被搜到。

同一天，英国竞争和市场管理局，正式对 Meta 展开调查。它担心，Meta 新的商业条款，可能让 Meta 从出版商的网站上收集内容，用在自己的产品里，包括 AI 功能，而企业没有选择的权利。上周我们讲过，Meta 把这些条款推迟到了 11 月 20 日。英国监管机构说，Meta 现在已经暂停推行，先征求行业意见。调查的第一步，是看 Meta 在英国市场有没有支配地位，监管机构也强调，目前还没有任何结论。Meta 对路透社说，这个调查没有依据，它已经承诺，不会在没征求出版商意见的情况下修改条款。

我的判断是，AI 公司想用一个自愿的标准，先把训练数据这件事定下来，免得监管替它们定。而英国对 Meta 的调查，正好说明了反过来会发生什么。

但问题在于，robots.txt 只是一个请求，不是一把锁，不配合的爬虫可以直接无视。它也收不回已经被收集的内容。草案现在还不是正式标准，参与讨论的人也说，还有一些分歧没有解决。而出版商最关心的，AI 回答把流量拿走这件事，这个开关根本管不到。

### 次要新闻一：苹果 AI 正式进入中国，接入阿里千问

第二条，和国内 iPhone 用户直接相关。

这个月早些时候，我们讲过彭博社的报道，说苹果 AI 会在 12 月进入中国。现在，苹果中国官网正式发了新闻稿。

时间表是这样的，11 月 4 日起，随最新的开发者测试版开放测试，12 月 iOS 27.3 正式发布时，面向中国用户推出。

首批功能包括，信息、FaceTime 和电话里的实时翻译，写作工具，视觉智能，照片的消除工具，智绘表情，还有邮件和通知的摘要。

真正值得注意的是阿里巴巴。苹果说，Siri 在处理特定请求时，可以接入千问，比如针对照片，或者 PDF 和演示文稿提问。写作工具里的创作功能，也可以用千问从零写出新内容。视觉智能也会用到千问。

设备方面，需要 iPhone 15 Pro 及以后的机型，而且 Siri 和系统语言，都要设成英文或者简体中文。

不过，最重要的一块还没来。苹果明确说，这次不包括 Siri AI，以及今年开发者大会上宣布的其他下一代功能。Mac、iPad 等其他设备，要等审批之后再推出。

我的判断是，这是苹果在中国补课，还谈不上领先。对阿里来说，千问进入了 iPhone 的系统层，这是一个很重要的入口。

### 次要新闻二：Cursor 里出现了被篡改的 Claude Code 插件

第三条，是一起针对开发者的攻击。

周四，在 AI 编程工具 Cursor 的插件市场里，出现了被篡改的 Claude Code 插件，挂了大约 11 个小时。Cursor 说，这些版本不是 Anthropic 开发的，最多影响了 5000 名用户，只涉及 Windows 和 Mac。

按照 Cursor 的安全公告，这段恶意代码会收集电脑的信息，包括用户名、Git 邮箱和公网 IP，发给攻击者。然后，它会尝试加入攻击者的 SSH 密钥，打开远程登录，建一条通往攻击者的隧道，还会每分钟重试一次。

好消息是，Cursor 说，没有管理员权限的话，这一步通常会失败，攻击者的服务器也已经下线。坏消息是，Cursor 提醒，只卸载插件可能不够。如果你用 Cursor，又在 10 月 29 日更新过 Claude Code，建议按照 Cursor 的公告检查电脑，清理残留，并更换各种密钥和密码。

现在还不清楚的是，攻击者是怎么用 Anthropic 的名义，把恶意版本发到 Cursor 插件市场的。Cursor 没有解释，我们也还没看到 Anthropic 的说法。

我的判断是，这次出问题的不是 AI 模型，而是分发渠道。编程智能体拥有你电脑的全部权限，每多一个自己运营插件市场的编辑器，就多一个可以被掉包的地方。

### 次要新闻三：市场开始对 AI 投入说不

第四条，接着昨天的财报。

昨天我们讲到，大厂的 AI 投入，已经把自由现金流打成了负数。周五，市场有了反应。

英特尔股价下跌大约百分之二十九。原因是它预计第四季度收入只有 150 亿到 160 亿美元，低于分析师预期的大约 170 亿美元。CEO 陈立武说，客户因为内存价格和供应问题，开始发出电脑需求放缓的信号。简单说，AI 数据中心抢走了大量内存，电脑市场受了伤。当天纳斯达克指数下跌大约百分之二点九，英伟达跌了大约百分之四，高通跌了大约百分之十一。

钱的方面也有变化。据英国《金融时报》报道，OpenAI 把这一轮融资目标，从大约 300 亿美元，下调到了大约 200 亿美元，估值大约 1.25 万亿美元。《金融时报》还报道，在一笔 630 亿美元的 Anthropic 芯片融资里担任主要投资方的黑石，现在预计 Anthropic 上市后的信用评级是双 B，也就是垃圾级里最高的一档，而不是黑石之前预期的投资级。Anthropic 和黑石都对 TechCrunch 拒绝置评，我们也没看到 OpenAI 的回应。

我的判断是，钱还在，但条件变紧了，最依赖外部融资买算力的 AI 公司，会最先感受到。当然，200 亿美元依然是一笔非常大的融资，一天的大跌，也不等于趋势。

### Hook

If you run a website and you don't want your work used to train AI, you currently have to block each AI company's crawler by name. On Friday, the biggest model makers moved toward a single switch that covers all of them.

### Today's top stories

Also today: Apple Intelligence is finally coming to iPhones in China, with Alibaba's Qwen built in. A fake Claude Code extension sat in Cursor's marketplace for about eleven hours. And Friday's market drop reached OpenAI's fundraising.

This is Daily AI Signal for Saturday, October thirty-first. Let's get into it.

### Main story — One switch to opt out of AI training

Here's what changed. On Friday, a new draft was filed at the Internet Engineering Task Force, the body that writes many of the web's technical rules. Its author is Kevin Kelley, who works at Anthropic. It defines one shared name you can put in your site's robots dot txt file. The name is "usage AI training", written with two underscores in front. If you add a block under that name and disallow your pages, you're telling every company that honors it: don't use this content to train generative AI models.

Google has already put it in its documentation. Google says it intends to support the shared signal in early twenty twenty-seven. OpenAI published a post the same day, and according to reports, it says it aims to support the signal in the coming months, while keeping its existing controls. Microsoft has reportedly committed too, also targeting early next year.

A few details matter. The draft covers training only. It says nothing about search, or about AI tools that fetch your page to answer a question. That means you can refuse training without disappearing from search. According to Google's John Mueller, if you use the new signal and don't mention Google's own training control by name, Google will treat it as a no for training.

Now, why does this matter? Until now, saying no to AI training meant keeping a list of crawler names, GPTBot, Google-Extended, ClaudeBot, and more every few months. Small publishers didn't keep up. One shared switch makes the default much easier to set.

And it landed on the same day a regulator went after a company moving the other way. Britain's Competition and Markets Authority opened a formal investigation into Meta's new commercial terms. The concern, in the CMA's words, is that the terms could let Meta collect content from publishers' websites and use it for its own products, including AI features, without businesses getting a choice. Last week we covered Meta delaying those terms to November twentieth. The CMA says Meta has since paused them while it consults. The first question is whether Meta is dominant in a UK market at all, and the CMA stresses no finding has been made. Meta told Reuters there are "no grounds" for an investigation, and that it already committed not to change its terms without consulting publishers.

My read is that the industry is trying to settle the training question with a voluntary standard, before regulators settle it for them. The CMA case shows what happens to a company that doesn't.

The catch: robots dot txt is a request, not a lock. A scraper that doesn't cooperate can ignore it. It doesn't pull back anything already collected. The draft isn't an official standard yet, and one participant in the discussions says some disputes remain unresolved. And the fight publishers care most about, traffic from AI answers, isn't covered by this switch at all.

What I'd watch next is whether Meta signs on. I haven't seen it announce support.

### Apple Intelligence comes to China, with Qwen

Earlier this month we covered a Bloomberg report that Apple Intelligence would reach China in December. Now Apple has confirmed it, in a release on its China newsroom.

The first features open for testing with a developer beta on November fourth, and reach users with iOS twenty-seven point three in December. The list includes live translation in Messages, FaceTime and Phone, writing tools, visual intelligence, the photo clean-up tool, Genmoji, and summaries for mail and notifications.

The interesting part is Alibaba. Apple says Siri can tap Alibaba's Qwen model for certain requests, including questions about photos and documents. Qwen also powers a feature in writing tools that drafts new text from scratch, and helps with visual intelligence.

It needs an iPhone fifteen Pro or newer, with Siri and the device language set to English or simplified Chinese. And the biggest piece is missing: Apple says the next-generation Siri and the other features it announced at this year's developer conference are not part of this release. Other devices, like the Mac and iPad, come after approvals.

This matters because China is one of Apple's largest markets, and iPhones there have gone without Apple's AI since it launched in the US, about two years ago. For Alibaba, it's a spot inside the iPhone's operating system. My read: this is Apple catching up in China, not leaping ahead. The test is whether next-gen Siri gets approved next.

### A fake Claude Code extension in Cursor

On Thursday, a tampered version of the Claude Code extension was available in the extension marketplace of Cursor, the AI code editor, for about eleven hours. Cursor says these versions were not developed by Anthropic, and that at most five thousand users were affected. Only Windows and Mac were hit.

According to Cursor's security notice, the code collected details about the machine, including the username, the Git email address and the public IP, and sent them to the attacker. It then tried to add the attacker's SSH key, turn on remote login, and open a tunnel back to the attacker, and set itself up to retry every minute.

The good news: Cursor says that step usually fails without administrator rights, and the attacker's server is now offline. The bad news: Cursor warns that uninstalling the extension may not be enough. If you use Cursor and updated Claude Code on October twenty-ninth, check Cursor's notice for the SSH key name, remove anything left behind, and rotate your credentials.

What's still unknown is how someone published a malicious build under Anthropic's name in Cursor's marketplace. Cursor hasn't said, and I haven't seen a statement from Anthropic.

The bigger point is that the AI model wasn't the weak spot. Distribution was. Coding agents run with your full permissions, and every editor that runs its own marketplace is another place a trusted extension can be swapped.

### Markets push back on the AI build-out

Yesterday we covered Big Tech's free cash flow turning negative. On Friday, the market reacted.

Intel fell about twenty-nine percent, after forecasting fourth-quarter revenue of fifteen to sixteen billion dollars, below the roughly seventeen billion analysts expected. Its chief executive, Lip-Bu Tan, said customers began signaling a PC slowdown because of memory prices and availability. In other words, AI data centers are soaking up memory, and that's hurting PCs. The Nasdaq fell about two point nine percent. Nvidia lost about four percent, Qualcomm about eleven.

The money side moved too. The Financial Times reports that OpenAI trimmed its funding round to about twenty billion dollars, from about thirty, at a valuation of about one point two five trillion. The FT also reports that Blackstone, which anchors a sixty-three billion dollar financing deal for Anthropic's chips, now expects Anthropic to get a double-B credit rating after its IPO. That's the top tier of junk, not the investment grade Blackstone expected before. Anthropic and Blackstone declined to comment to TechCrunch; I haven't seen an OpenAI comment.

My read: the money is still there, but on tighter terms, and the labs that depend on outside funding for compute feel it first. The counterpoint is that twenty billion dollars is still an enormous private round, and one bad day isn't a trend.

### Quick hits

Reddit had its worst day as a public company, down twenty-nine percent, after a weaker-than-expected forecast. Its chief executive said traffic from Google search went down meaningfully this quarter because of AI, and Reddit plans to test requiring people to log in to read. That's the other side of today's headline: AI answers are already moving traffic away from publishers.

Waymo and Toyota say they'll begin testing a fleet of self-driving Toyota bZ4X cars in Japan in twenty twenty-seven. TechCrunch reports the cars will be built at Toyota's Takaoka plant, and are also meant for the UK and other right-hand-drive markets.

And a reality check on shopping agents. DoorDash's chief executive, Tony Xu, said on the company's earnings call that it's seeing "maybe several hundred orders in total" from third-party AI agents. DoorDash handles millions of deliveries a day. Agents are getting a lot of attention, but not many orders yet.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that AI is running into the rest of the world's rules. Publishers want a say over training. Regulators are asking whether big platforms took that choice away. China decides which AI features ship on an iPhone. And lenders are deciding what AI build-outs cost. The one thing I would watch next is which other companies commit to the shared training signal, starting with Meta.

That's Daily AI Signal for today. Thanks for listening.
