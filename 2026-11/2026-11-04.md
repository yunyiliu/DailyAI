### 头条：苹果把新版 Siri 开放给所有开发者

先说苹果。

美国时间周二，苹果在开发者网站上宣布，新版 Siri，也就是 Siri AI，向所有开发者开放。从 iOS，iPadOS，macOS 和 visionOS 的 27.3 测试版开始，任何 App 都可以接入。

怎么接？用的是苹果已有的 App Intents 框架。简单说，App 把自己的内容和操作交给 Siri，用户用自己的话提要求，Siri 就去调用。苹果举的例子，是查快递，订座位，还有下单。

还有两个变化，比听上去更重要。第一个叫 skills，开发者可以教 Siri，把好几个操作串起来，完成一个复杂的请求。第二个，Siri 现在能调出 Spotlight 语义索引里的任何内容，不再局限于苹果预先定义好的几类格式。换句话说，Siri 能看到的 App 内容，多了很多。

苹果自己的应用，比如日历，邮件，地图，信息，备忘录，照片和 Safari，也在陆续加上这些能力。苹果举的例子是，找到你上周去过的一家咖啡店，或者把一年多没打开过的文件归档。

不过要说清楚，这不是面向用户的发布。苹果说，这些接入要几个月后才会推送给用户，App Store 的提交，要到春天才开放。Siri AI 需要支持 Apple Intelligence 的设备，目前支持英语，法语，日语，韩语，葡萄牙语和西班牙语。在欧盟，iPhone，iPad 和手表上暂时用不了，13 岁以下的用户也不能用。

页面最后还有两句小字，很值得看。一句是，苹果之后会公布，通过 Siri AI 实现的功能，要遵守哪些条款和使用政策。另一句是，依赖云端模型的 Apple Intelligence 功能，包括 Siri AI，有每日使用上限，以后扩大使用量，要另外付费。

这件事真正重要的地方在于，过去两年，AI Agent 最大的问题是，谁站在用户和 App 中间。OpenAI 希望是 ChatGPT，谷歌希望是 Gemini，苹果的答案是，操作系统自己来做，App 接进来就行。如果你做 iPhone 应用，下一个入口，可能不是一个页面，而是一句话。

我的判断是，后面那份条款，和这次的接口一样重要。Siri 怎么在你和竞争对手之间做选择，能读到哪些数据，要不要收费，这些才决定谁能受益。当然，这还只是预览版，Siri 串联多个操作到底靠不靠谱，苹果没有给出数据。

接下来要看的，是苹果的 Siri AI 条款，还有那个付费档位的价格。

### 次要新闻：怎么证明，回答你的真是那个模型

第三条，一个很少有人想过的问题。

你通过 API 调用一个模型，怎么确定，用的就是你付费的那个模型，而不是被悄悄换成了更便宜的？

本周，Anthropic 的前沿红队，和瑞士洛桑联邦理工学院，还有麻省理工的研究者，发布了一项研究，叫可证明推理，系统名叫 FlashProve。它用的是零知识证明。模型提供方先对一套权重做承诺，之后每一个回答，都附带一份证明，说明这些输出，确实来自那套权重。任何人都能验证，但看不到权重本身。

数字来自 Anthropic 的文章。在 4000 亿参数的混合专家模型 Llama 4 Maverick 上，用一张 H100，每秒大约能证明 110 个 token，花的 GPU 时间，大约是直接运行模型的 70 到 95 倍。验证一份证明，在普通 CPU 上大约 17 秒。代码以 Apache 协议开源。

限制也写得很清楚。这还是研究原型，只支持贪心解码，不是平常的随机采样，上下文最多大约 1000 个 token。据 TechCrunch 报道，目前还没有计划在 Anthropic 或其他公司部署。

我的判断是，贵 70 倍，日常肯定用不了，但密码学证明的成本，过去降得一向很快。对通过中间商买模型，或者只能相信厂商说法的企业来说，这是第一次有人认真给出，可验证的方案。

### Main story — Siri AI opens to all developers

On Tuesday, Apple posted on its developer site that Siri AI, its rebuilt version of Siri, is now open to any developer. It arrives as a developer preview in the iOS, iPadOS, macOS and visionOS twenty-seven point three betas.

Here's what changed. Apps can now hand their content and actions to Siri AI through App Intents, the framework Apple already uses for Shortcuts and older Siri integrations. Apple's examples are checking a delivery, booking a reservation and placing an order. You ask in your own words, and Siri carries out whatever custom action the app has built.

Two additions matter more than they sound. The first is skills. Apple says developers can teach Siri AI how to combine several actions into a single experience for complex requests. The second: Siri AI can now surface any item in the Spotlight semantic index, not only things that fit one of Apple's predefined schemas. In plain terms, Siri can see a lot more of what's inside your apps.

Apple is rolling the same capabilities into its own apps, including Calendar, Files, Mail, Maps, Messages, Notes, Photos and Safari. Its examples: find a coffee shop you visited last week, or archive files you haven't opened in over a year.

Now the timing, because this is not a consumer launch. Apple says these integrations ship to customers in a few months, and App Store submissions open in the spring. Siri AI needs an Apple Intelligence device set to a supported language: English, French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese or Spanish. It isn't available in the EU on iPhone, iPad or Apple Watch, and it isn't available to users under thirteen.

Two lines in the fine print are worth reading. Apple says it will follow up with more details on the terms and usage policies for experiences built through Siri AI. And a footnote says Apple Intelligence features that rely on server-side models, Siri AI included, have daily usage limits, and that expanded access will be available for a fee in the future.

Why this matters. For two years the agent question has been: who sits between you and your apps? OpenAI would like that to be ChatGPT. Google would like it to be Gemini. Apple's answer is the operating system, with apps plugging in underneath. If you build an iPhone app, your next interface might be a sentence, not a screen.

What most headlines are missing is that the rules aren't published yet. Apple controls the store, the assistant, and now a future paid tier for heavy use. My read is that the policy document still to come matters as much as the API released this week. How Siri picks between your app and a competitor's, what data it can read, and whether there's a fee will decide who benefits.

The catch: this is a preview. Developers can build now, but users won't see third-party integrations for months, and Apple hasn't shared any data on how reliably Siri AI chains several actions together. Voice assistants have promised this kind of thing before.

What to watch next: Apple's terms and usage policies for Siri AI, and the price of that paid tier.

### Anthropic's FlashProve

Here's a problem most people never think about. When you call a model through an API, how do you know you got the model you paid for, and not a cheaper one quietly swapped in?

This week Anthropic's Frontier Red Team, working with researchers from EPFL and MIT, published a research post on what they call provable inference. Their system, FlashProve, uses zero-knowledge proofs. The provider first commits to a set of model weights. Then every response comes with a proof that those committed weights produced those output tokens. Anyone can check the proof without ever seeing the weights.

The numbers come from Anthropic's post. On Llama 4 Maverick, a four-hundred-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model, proving runs at about a hundred and ten tokens per second on a single H100. That's roughly seventy to ninety-five times the GPU time of simply running the model. Checking a proof takes about seventeen seconds on an ordinary CPU. The code is open source under the Apache license.

The limits are real, and the authors list them. It's a research prototype. It covers greedy decoding on an exact integer version of the model, not the usual random sampling. Context tops out at about a thousand tokens. And according to TechCrunch, there's no plan yet to deploy it at Anthropic or anywhere else.

Why it matters. Model substitution is a real concern for anyone buying AI through a reseller, or relying on a vendor's claim that a particular model reviewed their code. My read: seventy times the cost is far too much for everyday use, but cryptographic proofs have a track record of getting much cheaper. This is a serious first sketch of "trust, but verify" for model APIs.

On Tuesday, at its Fal.Con Europe conference, CrowdStrike announced deals to buy two AI security companies. Fiddler AI monitors and explains how AI agents behave after they're deployed, and becomes a product called Falcon Observe. Dreadnode tests models and agents with simulated attacks before they go live.

CrowdStrike didn't disclose a price. The Wall Street Journal reported about four hundred million dollars for Fiddler and ninety-five million for Dreadnode. CrowdStrike says it will pay mostly in cash and expects the deals to close in its fiscal fourth quarter, which ends January thirty-first.

My read: security vendors now treat agents like a new kind of employee that needs watching. And companies will let more agents loose once they believe they can stop them.
