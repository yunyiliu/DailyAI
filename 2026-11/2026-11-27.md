### 开场

这里是 Daily AI Signal，今天是 11 月 27 日，星期五。

### 头条：芯片新规，从文件走到了出货

先说头条。美国芯片远程访问新规的影响，开始落到具体的出货和模型上。

昨天我们讲过，这条新规 11 月 19 日生效。给中国公司提供算力的海外数据中心，超过一定规模，就要申请许可。马来西亚、新加坡、泰国、印尼和巴西，进口先进芯片也要许可。已经在做这类业务的数据中心，有 90 天时间去申请。

周四，英国《金融时报》报道，英伟达已经通知代工伙伴，停止向马来西亚、新加坡、泰国和印尼发运装有英伟达芯片的服务器。报道援引三位知情人士。按报道的说法，戴尔和超微这些服务器厂商，已经告诉这四个国家的客户，英伟达不会放行他们的订单。这次暂停是不是临时的，目前还不清楚。一位云服务公司高管对《金融时报》说，90 天的倒计时在走，可没人知道这个许可到底怎么申请，整个地区一片混乱。英伟达、戴尔和超微的回应，我们还没看到。

同一天，路透社报道，智谱原本计划 11 月发布下一代旗舰模型 GLM-5.5，但到现在还没发。一位知情人士说，发布可能推迟到 2027 年第一季度，甚至更晚。报道提到两个原因，一个是新规让海外算力用不上了，另一个是美国几家大模型公司，加强了对蒸馏的防范。蒸馏，简单说，就是用别人模型的输出，来训练自己的模型。关于月之暗面，也就是 Kimi 背后的公司，一位知情人士说，它的旗舰模型，一般半年左右更新一代，如果海外算力完全断掉，这个间隔可能拉长到一年甚至更久。两家公司都没有回应路透社。

不过，当天晚上，一位智谱高管在朋友圈发文，说下午的所谓爆料，都是假消息。按国内科技媒体的转述，他没有点名具体模型，也没有给出发布时间。我们也没有看到智谱的正式声明。所以，一边是匿名消息，一边是非正式的否认，两边都要谨慎看。

这件事为什么重要？之前，这条新规主要还停在纸面上，规定谁需要许可。这一周，它变成了实际动作。如果报道属实，英伟达自己就在许可还办不下来的时候，先把四个国家的货停了。东南亚那些正常做生意的买家，也被卷了进来。

对中国的 AI 公司来说，租用海外算力，和向美国模型学习，原本是追赶前沿的两条主要路径。现在，两条路同时在收紧。

我的判断是，这条规则，正在由芯片厂商在上游执行，速度比许可审批快得多。真正的决定权，落到了美国商务部发许可的那张桌子上。接下来，我会看两件事。第一，90 天之内，许可能不能批下来。批得快，这次停货就只是一次短暂的合规暂停。第二，GLM-5.5 会不会在 12 月照常发布。发了，说明推迟的报道不准，没发，那条否认就站不住了。

### 瑞士：首次确认 AI Agent 网络攻击，已经得手

再看网络安全。瑞士联邦网络安全办公室，这周发布通报，标题是，涉及 AI Agent 的网络攻击，瑞士首次确认案例。

通报里最关键的一句是，使用 AI Agent 的攻击，成功入侵了受害者的系统。受害方包括企业，也包括关键基础设施运营方，至少涉及两个行业。通报把这类工具叫作，进攻型 Agent 框架。它们会自己找漏洞，自己挑攻击工具，看结果，再计划下一步。通报举了两个公开可用的例子，ARTEX 和 CyberStrikeAI，还说这类工具的数量，从 10 月以来差不多翻了一倍。不过，通报没有说瑞士的案子具体用了哪种工具，也没有说是谁干的。

早一天，意大利国家网络安全机构也发了类似通报，说在意大利发现了大规模的入侵活动，点名了四种工具，其中也有 ARTEX 和 CyberStrikeAI。

路透社在报道里给出了具体的数字，但和瑞士通报本身的说法对不上，我们先不采用。

真正值得看的，是建议。攻击的入口并不新，就是弱密码、默认密码，还有暴露在公网上、没打补丁的系统。昨天我们讲过，英国网络安全中心也是这个看法。但瑞士这次直接要求，公网系统的关键补丁，最晚在发布后 24 小时内装上，所有能从公网访问的内部系统，一律加上多因素认证，没有例外。

我的判断是，24 小时这个时间窗口，才是最重要的信号。安全机构已经默认，攻击方会用机器的速度来扫描和利用漏洞。英国、瑞士、意大利，几天之内，三个国家的机构都在说类似的话。防守的办法并不神秘，就是把基本功，做得快得多。

### OpenAI：计费出错，ChatGPT 额度会用得更快

第三条，和付费用 ChatGPT 的人直接相关。

周四，OpenAI 的 Thibault Sottiaux 发帖说，GPT-6.2 上线的时候，出了一个计费错误，大部分用户在用 Astra 和 Sol 这两个模型时，被错误地打了折。现在已经修好了，同样的工作，会消耗更多的额度。按媒体引述他的帖子，和上周比，用 Astra，额度会快百分之四十用完，用 Sol，会快百分之二十五。

作为补偿，Go、Plus、Pro 和 Business 用户，各得到一次额度重置的机会，可以在网页版或桌面版的用量设置里使用，把额度提前补满，但不会永久提高上限。据 Decrypt 报道，OpenAI 不会追溯收费，其他模型也不受影响。

昨天我们还讲过，OpenAI 正在把 Chat 和 Work 合并成一个共用的额度，先给一小部分 Pro 用户，12 月扩大范围。

我的看法是，OpenAI 主动公开承认，还给了补偿，这一点值得肯定。但这件事也说明，按额度计费最大的问题，是用户看不到换算比例。一个本不该有的折扣，持续了大概九天，很多人对 GPT-6.2 的第一印象，正是在这段时间里形成的。

### 快讯

接下来是快讯。

第一条，华为。据彭博社报道，华为明年 2 月，会在旧金山的国际固态电路会议上，发表一篇关于定制 HBM 高带宽内存的论文。彭博社说，从论文标题看，它的数据引脚密度，是三星和 SK 海力士量产 HBM 的两倍。不过，速度和带宽都还没公布，离量产还有多远，也不清楚。最先进的 HBM 受美国出口管制，中国很难买到，所以 2 月这篇论文值得关注。

第二条，加拿大。加拿大把 AI 算力补贴基金，追加 7 亿加元，总额到了 10 亿加元。新规则偏向本土云。从加拿大本土控股、数据留在加拿大的云服务商那里买算力，最多可以报销百分之八十，从其他在加拿大运营的服务商买，最多百分之五十。这一轮面向医疗和生命科学，申请截止日期是明年 1 月 4 日。算力主权，已经直接写进了补贴比例里。

第三条，英伟达。英伟达在 Hugging Face 上，放出了图像生成模型 Nemotron Labs Image 的权重，总参数 300 亿，激活参数 30 亿。在英伟达自己做的对比里，它在一部分测试上超过 GPT Image 2，在另一部分上落后。要注意的是许可，它只能用于非商业的研究和评估，不能直接拿去做产品。

### 结语

今天这几条新闻，背后有一个共同的趋势，AI 正在模型之外，遇到越来越多的摩擦。有没人知道怎么申请的出口许可，有 24 小时之内必须打上的补丁，还有算错了的用量额度。模型还在变强，但围绕模型的整套系统，正在经受考验。

接下来，我最关注的，是美国商务部的许可审批。东南亚的许可批得快，这一周只是一次小波折。批得慢，全球 AI 算力建在哪里，可能就要重新洗牌了。

### Main story: the chip rule moves down the supply chain

Yesterday we covered the US remote-access rule starting to bite. It took effect on November nineteenth. It requires a license to supply advanced chips to data centers that serve Chinese companies above a set amount of compute, and it adds license requirements for advanced chips going to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil. Data centers already serving those customers got ninety days to apply.

Here's what changed on Thursday.

First, the Financial Times reported that Nvidia told its manufacturing partners to stop shipping servers with its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. The FT cites three people familiar with the matter. According to the report, server makers including Dell and Supermicro have told customers in those countries that Nvidia won't authorize their orders. It's not clear whether the halt is temporary. One cloud executive told the FT that nobody knows how to apply for the license while the ninety-day clock is ticking, and called it "sheer chaos" in the region. We haven't seen comment from Nvidia, Dell or Supermicro.

Second, Reuters reported that Zhipu, which sells internationally as Z dot A I, had planned to release its next flagship, GLM five point five, in November, and hasn't. One person told Reuters the launch could slip to the first quarter of twenty twenty-seven or later. Sources pointed to two pressures: the loss of overseas compute under the new rule, and tougher defenses at US labs against distillation, which is training a model on another model's outputs. On Moonshot, the company behind Kimi, a person familiar with its operations said its usual gap of about six months between flagship models could stretch to a year or more if overseas access disappears entirely. Neither company responded to Reuters.

There's a counterpoint. That evening, a Zhipu executive posted on WeChat that the afternoon's so-called leaks were fake. His post, as reported by Chinese tech media, didn't name the model or give a date, and we haven't seen a formal company statement. So treat both sides with care: anonymous sources on one side, an informal denial on the other.

Why does this matter? Until now, the rule was mostly paperwork: who needs a license. This week, it's turning into physical action. If the FT is right, Nvidia itself is stopping shipments to four countries before anyone there knows how to get a license. Legitimate buyers in Southeast Asia are caught in the middle.

For Chinese labs, renting compute abroad and learning from US models were the two main ways to keep pace with the frontier. Both are now being squeezed at the same time.

My read: the rule is being enforced upstream, by the chip vendor, faster than the licensing system can respond. That pushes the real decision to the Commerce Department's license desk.

So here's what I'd watch. First, whether licenses start flowing inside the ninety days, which would make this halt a short compliance pause. Second, whether GLM five point five ships in December anyway. If it does, the delay report was wrong. If it doesn't, the denial won't look good.

### Switzerland confirms AI-agent attacks that worked

Switzerland's Federal Office for Cybersecurity published an advisory this week titled "Cyberattacks involving AI agents: first confirmed cases in Switzerland."

The key line: the use of AI agents led to successful compromises of victims' systems. The victims include organizations and critical infrastructure operators across at least two sectors. The office describes the tools as agentic offensive frameworks. They are AI agents that search for vulnerabilities on their own, choose attack tools, check the results and plan the next step. It names two publicly available examples, ARTEX and CyberStrikeAI, and says the number of such tools has almost doubled since October. It doesn't say which tools hit Swiss targets, and it doesn't say who's behind the attacks.

Italy's national cybersecurity agency put out a similar bulletin a day earlier. It reported mass compromise campaigns in Italy and named four tools, including ARTEX and CyberStrikeAI.

Reuters added specific numbers, citing the Swiss office, but those don't match the advisory's own wording, so we're leaving them out until that's cleared up.

The interesting part is the advice. The way in is nothing new: weak or default passwords, and known vulnerabilities in internet-facing systems. Yesterday we covered Britain's cyber agency making the same point. But the Swiss office now tells organizations to install critical patches on internet-facing systems no later than twenty-four hours after release, and to put multi-factor authentication on all internal systems reachable from the internet, like webmail and VPN, without exception.

My read: that twenty-four-hour window is the real signal. Security agencies now expect attackers to scan and exploit at machine speed. Three national agencies, in Britain, Switzerland and Italy, have said versions of this within a few days. The defense isn't exotic. It's doing the basics much faster.

### OpenAI's GPT-six-point-two billing mistake

If you pay for ChatGPT, this one affects you directly.

On Thursday, OpenAI's Thibault Sottiaux posted, quote, "We made a billing mistake at launch of GPT-6.2 that incorrectly discounted GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol usage for most users." He said it's fixed, so the same work will now use more of your plan's allowance. According to reports quoting his post, users will hit their limits forty percent faster on Astra and twenty-five percent faster on Sol than last week.

As an apology, Go, Plus, Pro and Business users get one banked reset. You can redeem it in your usage settings on the web or desktop app to refill your allowance early. It doesn't raise your limit permanently. According to Decrypt, OpenAI won't charge anyone retroactively, and other models aren't affected.

This comes on top of what we covered yesterday: OpenAI is merging Chat and Work into one shared usage meter, starting with a small group of Pro users and expanding in December.

Two takeaways. Credit to OpenAI for saying this in public and offering a reset, instead of quietly changing the math. But the episode shows the deeper problem with usage allowances: users can't see the exchange rate. A discount nobody intended ran for about nine days, and many people formed their view of GPT-six-point-two during that time. My read is that more visible metering would help everyone, including OpenAI.

### Quick hits

Huawei is heading to the top chip-design conference. Bloomberg reports that Huawei will present a paper at ISSCC in San Francisco in February on a custom high-bandwidth memory design. Bloomberg says the paper's title indicates double the data-pin density of the HBM that Samsung and SK Hynix mass-produce. No speed or bandwidth has been disclosed, and it's unclear how close Huawei is to making it. The most advanced HBM is under US export controls for China, so this is one to watch in February.

Canada has raised its AI Compute Access Fund to one billion Canadian dollars, adding seven hundred million. The new terms favor home-grown clouds. Companies can get up to eighty percent of compute costs covered if they buy from a Canadian-owned provider that keeps data in Canada, versus up to fifty percent from other providers based in Canada. The current call is for health and life sciences, with applications due January fourth. Sovereignty is now written straight into the subsidy rate.

And Nvidia has posted weights for an image generator, Nemotron Labs Image, thirty billion parameters with three billion active, an autoregressive model built from Nvidia's Nemotron three Nano. In Nvidia's own comparisons it beats GPT Image 2 on some benchmarks and trails on others. Note the license: it's for non-commercial research and evaluation only, so it's not something you can ship in a product.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that AI is running into friction outside the model itself. There are export licenses nobody knows how to file, a twenty-four-hour window to patch before agents find you, and usage meters that turned out to be miscalibrated. The models keep getting better. The systems around them are being tested.

The one thing I'd watch next is the Commerce Department's license desk. If approvals for Southeast Asia come quickly, this week looks like a hiccup. If they don't, the map of where AI gets built is about to change.
