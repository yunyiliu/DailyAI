He proposes that frontier labs agree on common limits, measured by perplexity and other metrics, that they won't cross until monitoring improves, for example with a successor to chain-of-thought monitors. Quick hits: Microsoft's agent-framework package compromised on PyPI, and OpenAI's MedChemBench.

### 开场

这里是 Daily AI Signal，今天是 11 月 23 日，星期一。

### 头条

先说头条。OpenAI 首席科学家 Jakub Pachocki，周日发了一篇文章，公开了 OpenAI 内部用来衡量预训练进度的一个数字，还提议各家前沿实验室，一起约定一条暂时不跨过的线。

这个数字叫困惑度，英文是 perplexity。简单说，就是模型读到没见过的文字时，有多意外。数字越低，说明模型预测得越准。OpenAI 实际看的，是困惑度取对数，也就是工程师训练时盯着的交叉熵损失。测的数据，是 OpenAI 自己内部代码库里，一份固定不变、没有参与训练的样本。

文章给出的数字是这样的。GPT-3.5 大约是 0.99，GPT-4 大约是 0.91。文章说，当 OpenAI 的模型降到 0.8 以下，网络安全和数学这些领域，开始出现变革性的能力。到了 0.76 左右，有一个模型，开始在公开的数学难题上做出突破性的结果，文章没有说是哪个模型。

他还做了预测。模型进入 0.68 到 0.7 这个区间时，会出现更多变革性的能力。最快在 2027 年，预训练可能会迎来一批根本性的进展。他也预计，对齐审查会变得更难，模型也会更容易察觉自己正在被测试。

那他提议什么？第一，各家前沿实验室，要在模型第一次投入使用之前，就定好统一的评估方法，对安全论证做红队测试，也就是专门找漏洞。第二，基于困惑度和其他指标，设定共同的上限，大家承诺不跨过去，直到监控 AI 行为的能力，达到一个具体的里程碑。他举的例子是，做出思维链监控的下一代方法。思维链监控，就是通过读模型的推理过程，来发现它有没有做坏事。

文章没写的东西，同样重要。它没有给出这条线具体是多少，没有点名任何其他实验室，也没说由谁来检查，大家有没有遵守。

他自己也列了四个弱点。各家用的评估数据不同，数字没法直接比。困惑度和模型实际能做什么，对应得并不清楚。预训练之后的进步，它看不出来，用他的话说，思维链推理并不会降低困惑度。还有，没人知道这个数字最低能到多少。

为什么重要？过去说到了危险的能力水平就该慢下来，一直很空，什么算危险，怎么量，都说不清。这个提议，把上限和训练中能实际追踪的一个数字挂上了钩。OpenAI 也付出了代价，等于把自己的进度，摆在了竞争对手面前。

问题在于，公开数字是真的，上限还只是提议。要让共同上限有用，各家得在可比的数据上测，而文章自己也承认这很难。还有一个不太舒服的地方，在这条曲线上领先的公司，最希望别人同意在一条线前停下来。

我的判断是，文章里最值得注意的一句，是关于思维链监控的。OpenAI 的首席科学家公开说，现在各家用来看模型推理过程的主要工具，可能不够应付接下来的模型，开发应该等更好的工具出来。

接下来要看的是，谷歌 DeepMind、Anthropic、Meta、xAI，有没有哪一家，也公开自己的数字。如果没有，这就只是一篇写得很好的文章。

### 次要新闻

接下来看安全。亚马逊威胁情报团队发了一份报告，标题是，一个与中国有关的攻击者，用 Cairn AI Agent，自己找到了一个零日漏洞。零日漏洞，就是软件开发者自己都还不知道的漏洞。Cairn 是一个开源的渗透测试框架，核心是 AI Agent。

据英国科技媒体 The Register 对这份报告的转述，攻击从 9 月 8 日开始，目标是东亚地区、中国大陆以外的大学。这些 Agent 盯上了 MONAI Label，一个用来标注医学影像的开源服务器软件。它们识别出软件的框架，摸清了接口，找到一个不检查文件路径的上传接口。亚马逊首席信息安全官 CJ Moses 写道，从摸清接口，到能在服务器上随意写文件，Agent 只用了大约 11 分钟。之后，它们又用同一个漏洞，拿到了最高权限。

亚马逊判断攻击者和中国有关，依据是，提示词用的是简体中文，脚本里的时间设成了中国标准时间。报告没有点名具体组织。整个过程也不是完全没人管，据报道，操作者后来又给了这些 Agent 一个进入另一所大学网络的通道。

漏洞本身是真的。MONAI Label 的维护者上周发布了严重级别的安全公告，0.8.5 及以前的版本都受影响，而且默认不需要登录。修复后的 0.8.6 版本，周日已经发布。

我的判断是，要看攻击目标。冷门的科研软件，默认不用登录，放在大学网络里，以前不值得一个高手花一周去攻。有了 Agent，这类冷门软件也值得攻击了。当然，这只是一家公司的报告，而且这些 Agent 的很多其他尝试都失败了。

再看一条后续。周六我们讲过，安全公司 Dragos 在大约 200 万条提示词里埋了假密钥，发现至少三家 AI 托管服务商被入侵过。周日，模型路由平台 OpenRouter 宣布，根据自己的调查，下架了三家服务商，但没有公布名字。它还把 125 个接口，移出了零数据保留的名单，其中一部分是研究人员点到的，一部分是出于谨慎。OpenRouter 说，已经通知了可能受影响的客户，调查结果没有显示 OpenRouter 自己被入侵。

几家服务商也发了说明。Phala 说，攻击者控制了它的一台推理服务器，能看到处理中的请求。Together AI 说，6 月和 7 月的部分提示词样本被人下载，原因是一个凭证被暴露在公开的代码仓库里，用它的原话说，是一个 AI 编程 Agent 提交了这个凭证。Novita 说，因为路由配置出错，一部分 MiniMax 模型的请求，被转发到了 MiniMax 自己的接口。Atlas Cloud 则说，是它自己决定，把模型从 OpenRouter 上撤下来的。到底哪三家被下架，目前还不清楚。

这对开发者意味着，平台上写着零数据保留，只有链条上每一家都靠得住才算数。永远不要把真的密钥写进提示词，上游服务商，最好自己选。

### 快讯

最后是快讯。第一条，上周二，微软官方的 Python 包 agent-framework 被投毒。据安全公司 Wiz 的报告，发布到 PyPI 上的两个版本，带着窃取凭证的代码，只要启动 Python 就会运行，哪怕根本没导入这个包。这两个版本在线不到一个小时，这个包每周下载量大约 4.1 万次。Wiz 说，攻击者似乎掌握了微软发布流程的一份证书和私钥。目前还没有看到微软的声明。如果装过 1.26.1 或者 1.26.2 版本，要尽快更换密钥。

第二条，OpenAI 发布了一个药物化学推理测试，叫 MedChemBench，一共 99 道题，来自 117 篇论文和专利。OpenAI 说，它的模型在读懂分子结构上表现很好，但在挑选化合物、预测性质这些题上，只拿到大约一半的分数。OpenAI 自己也说，这个结果不能说明模型能发现新药。

### 结语

今天这几条新闻，背后是同一件事，衡量。OpenAI 想要一个各家共用的数字，看模型走到了哪一步。Dragos 得埋下假密钥，才能测出谁在偷看提示词。亚马逊能把 Agent 的攻击讲清楚，是因为找到了一台运行这些 Agent 的服务器。真正难的，都是先看清楚到底发生了什么。

我接下来最关注的，是会不会有第二家前沿实验室，公开自己的困惑度数字。真有的话，这个提议才算开始。以上就是今天的 Daily AI Signal。

### Hook

### Today's top stories

Today: OpenAI proposes common limits on AI development, measured by perplexity. Amazon says an attacker's AI agents found a brand-new software flaw on their own. And OpenRouter drops three inference providers after the prompt-theft study we covered on Saturday.

This is Daily AI Signal for Monday, November twenty-third.

### Main story: OpenAI's perplexity proposal

Here's what happened. On Sunday, OpenAI published an essay by its chief scientist, Jakub Pachocki. The title is "Perplexity and Intelligence."

Perplexity is one of the most basic numbers in training a language model. In plain terms, it measures how surprised the model is by text it hasn't seen. Lower means the model predicts better. OpenAI tracks the log of perplexity, which is the same thing as the cross-entropy loss engineers watch during pretraining. It measures this on a fixed, held-out set of its own internal code.

Now the numbers. According to the essay, GPT-3.5 scored about zero point nine nine on that dataset. GPT-4 scored about zero point nine one. Pachocki writes that once OpenAI's models got below about zero point eight, transformative capabilities showed up in fields like cybersecurity and mathematics. And a model at about zero point seven six started producing what he calls groundbreaking results on open math problems. The essay doesn't name that model.

Then the forecast. He expects further transformative capabilities as models enter a window of about zero point six eight to zero point seven. And he writes that as soon as twenty twenty-seven, we could conceivably see an avalanche of fundamental pretraining advances. He also expects alignment auditing to get harder, and models to get better at noticing when they're being evaluated.

So here's the proposal. Pachocki wants frontier developers to agree on shared standards for evaluating these models and red-teaming their safety cases, in place by the time a model is first used. And he wants, quote, "common limits, based on perplexity and other measurements, that developers are committed to not crossing until our ability to understand and monitor AI behavior achieves a certain concrete milestone." His example of that milestone: a successor to chain-of-thought monitors. That's the technique of reading a model's reasoning to catch bad behavior.

What the essay doesn't do matters just as much. It doesn't propose a number for the limit. It doesn't name any other lab. And it doesn't say who would check that anyone is keeping to it.

Pachocki is upfront about the weak spots, too. He lists four. Labs use different evaluation data, so their numbers aren't directly comparable. Perplexity doesn't map neatly to what a model can actually do. It misses progress that happens after pretraining, because, in his words, chain-of-thought reasoning doesn't reduce perplexity. And nobody knows where the floor is.

Why does this matter? Because talk of slowing down at dangerous capability levels has always been vague. Dangerous according to whom, measured how? This proposal ties a limit to a quantity you can actually track during training. And OpenAI paid a price to make it. It just showed its competitors roughly where it stands.

Here's what most headlines are missing. The disclosure is real. The limit is not, at least not yet. A shared limit only works if everyone measures on comparable data, and the essay itself explains why that's hard. There's also an uncomfortable incentive. A lab that's ahead on this curve gains the most if everyone else agrees to stop at a line.

My read is that the most important sentence is the one about chain-of-thought monitors. OpenAI's chief scientist is saying, in public, that the main tool labs use today to watch their models' reasoning may not be enough for what comes next, and that development should wait for something better.

What to watch next: whether any other lab, Google DeepMind, Anthropic, Meta or xAI, publishes its own number. If nobody does, this stays a well-written essay.

### AI agents found a zero-day on their own

Next, a security story that's been talked about for a long time and now has a concrete case.

Amazon's threat intelligence team published a report titled "Chinese-nexus actor's Cairn AI agents found a zero-day on their own." Cairn is an open-source penetration-testing framework built around AI agents. According to The Register's account of the report, the campaign targeted universities in East Asia outside mainland China, starting on September eighth.

Here's what the agents did. They identified the framework behind a piece of software called MONAI Label, an open-source server used to label medical images. They mapped its API and found an upload endpoint that didn't check file paths. Amazon's chief information security officer, CJ Moses, wrote that in about eleven minutes the agents went from mapping the API to writing arbitrary files on the server. Later they used the same flaw to get a shell running as root.

Amazon ties the operator to China based on prompts written in Simplified Chinese and scripts set to China Standard Time. No group is named. And it wasn't fully hands-off. The Register reports the human operator later gave the agents a way into a second university network.

The flaw is real. MONAI Label's maintainers published a critical advisory last week. Every version up to zero point eight point five is affected, and by default the server runs with no login. A fixed version, zero point eight point six, came out on Sunday.

My read: look at the target. Niche research software, no login by default, sitting on a university network. Nobody would spend a skilled human's week on that. Agents make the long tail of obscure software worth attacking. The catch: this is one vendor's report, and many of the agents' other attempts failed.

### OpenRouter drops three providers

On Saturday we covered a study from the security firm Dragos. It planted fake secrets in about two million prompts and found that at least three AI hosting providers had been breached. Here's the update.

On Sunday, OpenRouter, the marketplace that routes requests to many model providers, said it had off-boarded three providers based on its own investigations. It didn't name them. It also removed a hundred and twenty-five endpoints from its Zero Data Retention eligibility. Some were flagged by the researchers, and some were removed as a precaution. It says it notified affected customers, and that the findings showed no breach of OpenRouter itself.

OpenRouter linked notices from several providers, and they tell different stories. Phala says an attacker compromised one of its inference servers and could see requests while they were processed. Together AI says prompt samples from June and July were downloaded after a credential was exposed in a public code repository. In its words, an AI coding agent committed that credential. Novita says a routing error sent some MiniMax requests on to MiniMax itself. And Atlas Cloud says it pulled its own models off OpenRouter, its decision, not OpenRouter's. We don't know which of these, if any, are the three that were removed.

Why this matters: "zero data retention" on a marketplace is only as good as every company behind it. And notice the Together case. A coding agent leaked a secret, which then exposed customers' prompts. If you build on these APIs, never put a live key in a prompt, and choose your upstream provider deliberately.

### Quick hits

Microsoft's official agent-framework package for Python was poisoned last Tuesday. According to Wiz, two versions published to PyPI carried a credential stealer that ran every time Python started, even if the package was never imported. They were up for under an hour. The package gets about forty-one thousand downloads a week. Wiz says the attacker appears to have used a compromised Microsoft release-pipeline certificate. We haven't seen a Microsoft statement. If you installed version one point two six point one or one point two six point two, rotate your credentials.

And OpenAI released MedChemBench, a test of reasoning in medicinal chemistry: ninety-nine problems drawn from a hundred and seventeen papers and patents. OpenAI says its models did very well at reading molecular structures, but earned only about half the credit on choosing compounds and predicting their properties. OpenAI itself says the results don't show that models can discover new drugs.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is measurement. OpenAI wants a shared number for how far models have come. Dragos had to plant fake secrets to measure who was reading prompts. And Amazon could describe the agents' attack in detail because it found a server hosting them. In every case, the hard part is seeing what's actually happening.

The one thing I would watch next is whether a second frontier lab publishes its own perplexity number. That would turn a proposal into a conversation.
