### 头条

先说头条。英伟达董事会，周三晚上开会，讨论要不要撤掉首席执行官黄仁勋。到我们写完这篇稿子的时候，董事会还没有公布结果。

这件事我们这周一直在跟。上周日，英伟达董事会宣布，就黄仁勋可能成为外国情报活动目标的报道，展开独立调查。涉及的女性叫 Alice Huang，和黄仁勋没有亲属关系，她以前在新加坡公司 Megaspeed 担任高管，这家公司买过价值数十亿美元的英伟达硬件。美国哥伦比亚广播公司援引《纽约时报》报道说，美国情报部门认为，她被中国国家安全部招募。中国外交部说，所谓中国间谍的说法完全没有根据。黄仁勋在给员工的信里，承认自己对妻子不忠。当时董事会说，没有理由认为国家安全或者公司机密受到损害，并表示信任他。

周三，情况变了。据《华尔街日报》报道，董事会正准备撤掉黄仁勋，最快当天就开会。哥伦比亚广播公司援引两名知情人士，也是这个说法。随后，《纽约时报》报道，黄仁勋还和一名向他汇报的英伟达高管有婚外情。路透社说无法核实这篇报道，英伟达和这名高管都没有回应。彭博社报道，英伟达正在和一家外部机构合作，物色可能的接班人。新闻网站 Semafor 报道，如果黄仁勋离开，部分董事倾向让首席财务官克雷斯担任临时首席执行官，但整个董事会还没有做决定。

美国财经媒体 CNBC 证实，周三晚上，董事会在开会讨论是否撤换他。所以准确地说，到写稿的时候，黄仁勋仍然是英伟达的首席执行官，关于撤换的说法，都来自匿名消息人士。

规则是这样的。据路透社报道，英伟达董事会有 11 名董事，除了黄仁勋，都是独立董事。按照公司章程，过半数董事就可以撤换他，有没有理由都可以。黄仁勋持有公司大约百分之三点六的股份。他是英伟达的联合创始人，已经掌舵 33 年，目前没有明显的接班人。

市场反应很快。周三盘后，英伟达股价跌了超过百分之八。路透社说，如果跌幅保持，从董事会宣布调查到现在，英伟达市值会蒸发大约 6000 亿美元。竞争对手反而上涨，AMD 盘后涨了大约百分之四，博通涨了大约百分之三。英伟达 11 月 17 日公布季度财报。

我的判断是，短期内，芯片供应受的影响不大，已经下的订单照样交货，产品路线也不会因为换人就改。更大的影响在政治上。这件事牵涉到一名被指控的中国情报人员，和一家买过英伟达硬件的公司，以后在华盛顿，谁再想为放松对华出口限制说话，都会难很多。接下来要看两件事，一是董事会的正式公告，二是 11 月 17 日的财报电话会。

### 次要新闻

第二条，谷歌把最强的模型 Gemini 4 Carbon，开放给了所有付费开发者。

两周前，我们讲过这个模型，当时只给一小部分网络安全防御方使用。周二，谷歌宣布，所有付费的开发者和企业，都可以通过 API 使用 Carbon。优惠价是每百万输入 token 2 美元，每百万输出 token 10 美元。11 月 30 日优惠期结束后，价格翻倍，变成 4 美元和 20 美元。谷歌 AI Ultra 的订阅用户，也会陆续在 Gemini 应用和搜索的 AI 模式里用上。

谷歌自己公布的成绩，包括在几个第三方编程和智能体测试里排第一，每个任务的成本，不到第二名外部模型的一半。不过这些都是谷歌自己的说法。

有意思的是，谷歌在同一篇文章里说，对于大部分软件开发和知识类工作，Gemini 4 Argon 仍然是最合适的选择。Carbon 会反复探索和检查，用的 token 更多，适合长时间、高难度的智能体任务。我的判断是，整个行业都在走向这种分层，一个贵的模型做难活，一个便宜的模型做大部分事情。

第三条，还是谷歌。谷歌 DeepMind 推出 AlphaProof Code，用 AI 智能体做形式化验证，简单说，就是用数学证明，来确认一段软件确实满足某个安全性质，由机器检查，而不是靠人看。

DeepMind 说，他们证明了 Signal 加密协议实际运行代码的关键安全性质，不只是纸面上的设计，而是编译后，在设备上运行的代码。比如，设备被临时攻破之前和之后，消息都仍然受到保护。

数字是这样的。由 Gemini Carbon 驱动的智能体，用 Lean 证明语言写了超过 600 万行证明。人类专家审核了证明目标，智能体把大约百分之一的代码，标出来交给人看。整个过程用了 4 周。DeepMind 说，类似的工作，过去要专家团队做好几年。

为了测试，团队把两个以前修好的漏洞，放回一份隔离的代码副本里，两次系统都给出了机器检查过的证明，说明有漏洞的版本不安全。需要说明的是，DeepMind 没有说在 Signal 里发现新的漏洞。

我的判断是，这是 AI 找漏洞的反面，AI 来证明代码没问题。但证明只覆盖你要求它证明的东西，所以薄弱环节，变成了人来决定，到底要证明哪些性质。

第四条，谷歌周三推出 Gemini Decisions API，背后是一个新模型，叫 Gemini Decision 1。它不写文字，只回答封闭式问题，是或否，多选一，或者打分。一次请求最多可以问 1000 个问题，每个答案都带一个 0 到 1 的置信度。价格是每百万输入 token 0.1 美元，输出免费。

这对开发者意味着，智能体每天做的很多事，其实不是写东西，而是做小决定，比如调用哪个工具，交给哪个模型，这个请求能不能处理。置信度低的时候，程序就可以转给人来判断。

### 快讯

接下来是几条快讯。据路透社援引三名知情人士报道，中国 AI 公司正在抢着租用海外数据中心里的英伟达算力，担心黄仁勋这件事，会让美国收紧远程使用先进芯片的规定。它们关注的《远程访问安全法案》，今年 1 月在众议院以 369 票对 22 票通过，还在等参议院表决。英伟达、腾讯和阿里巴巴没有回应。

阿里巴巴开放了 Qwen4-Max 的预览版，这是新一代通义千问的旗舰模型，支持 100 万 token 的上下文，能理解图片和视频，兼容 OpenAI 和 Anthropic 两种接口。目前只能通过阿里的 Token Plan 订阅使用，预览期到 11 月 25 日结束。官方文档还没有公布评测成绩，也没有说会不会开源。

最后是字节跳动。据彭博社报道，字节准备在几周内，推出一款类似 Meta 的 Muse 的 AI 助手。更值得一听的，是联合创始人张一鸣在内部会上的说法。据报道，他认为字节能在最前沿和 OpenAI、Anthropic 竞争，但最早也要到 2029 年或者 2030 年，原因之一是训练数据比对手少得多。他还说，公司不应该依赖蒸馏。字节跳动没有回应彭博社。

### Main story: Nvidia's board and Jensen Huang

We've followed this all week. On Sunday, Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation into reports that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The woman involved, Alice Huang, no relation, used to run Megaspeed, a Singapore company that bought billions of dollars of Nvidia hardware. According to the New York Times, as cited by CBS News, US intelligence believes she was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security. China's foreign ministry calls that story entirely baseless. Huang told employees he had been unfaithful to his wife. At that point, the board said it had no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, and that it had confidence in him.

Wednesday, that changed. The Wall Street Journal reported that the board was preparing to oust Huang, and could meet as soon as that day. CBS News, citing two sources, said the same. The New York Times then reported that Huang had also had an affair with an Nvidia executive who reports to him. Reuters said it couldn't verify that report, and neither Nvidia nor the executive responded. Bloomberg reported that Nvidia is working with an outside firm to identify possible replacements. And Semafor reported that some directors favor the chief financial officer, Colette Kress, as interim CEO if Huang goes, though the full board hadn't decided.

CNBC confirmed late Wednesday that the board was meeting to decide whether to remove him. When we finished writing, no decision had been announced. So, to be precise: as of this recording, Jensen Huang is still Nvidia's CEO, and everything about his removal comes from unnamed sources.

Here are the mechanics. According to Reuters, Nvidia has eleven directors, and all of them except Huang are independent. Under the company's bylaws, a majority can remove him, with or without cause. Huang owns about three point six percent of the company. He co-founded Nvidia and has run it for thirty-three years, and TechCrunch notes there's no obvious successor or public succession plan.

The market reaction was immediate. Nvidia shares fell more than eight percent in after-hours trading on Wednesday. Reuters says that if those losses hold, Nvidia will have lost about six hundred billion dollars in market value since the investigation was announced. Rivals moved the other way after hours: AMD up about four percent, Broadcom about three. Nvidia reports quarterly results on November seventeenth.

So why does this matter for AI, not just for Nvidia shareholders? Nvidia sells the chips that most frontier models are trained and run on. Huang is also the person who has argued Nvidia's case on China export rules in Washington.

My read is that the near-term effect on chip supply is small. Orders already placed still ship, and roadmaps don't change because a CEO does. The bigger effect is political. A scandal involving an alleged Chinese intelligence asset and a company that bought Nvidia hardware makes it much harder for anyone in Washington to argue for looser rules on China. And as you'll hear in the quick hits, Chinese buyers are already acting on that.

The other thing most headlines skip is how much Nvidia runs through one person. Huang has said he has around sixty direct reports, and Fortune describes him as sitting at the center of most decisions. That's worked for three decades. It's also exactly the kind of key-person risk that markets tend to ignore until a week like this one.

The counterpoint: the board has options short of removal, and the allegations about Huang are untested. What I'd watch next is the board's announcement, and then the earnings call on the seventeenth, because whoever is on that call will have to explain what it means for customers.

### Gemini 4 Carbon opens up

Two weeks ago we covered Google's Gemini 4 Carbon, which it calls its strongest model, when it was available only to a small group of cyber defenders. On Tuesday, Google opened it up.

Carbon is now in the API, through Google AI Studio and Gemini for business, for all paying developers and enterprises. The introductory price is two dollars per million input tokens and ten dollars per million output tokens. That runs out on November thirtieth, and then the price doubles, to four and twenty. Carbon is also rolling out to Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app, AI Mode in Search, and Google's Antigravity coding tool. Access specifically for cyber work still goes through Google's Fairwind program.

Google's benchmark claims, and these are Google's, include first place on CursorBench, Perplexity's WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench, at less than half the cost per task of the next-best outside model. On safety, Google says Carbon blocked ninety-nine percent of attack attempts from its own red-teaming agent. It also says, in the same post, that resistance to prompt injection still needs work.

The interesting part is that Google tells most people not to use it. In its own words, Gemini 4 Argon remains the ideal choice for the majority of software engineering and knowledge work. Carbon uses more tokens, because it explores and checks its work, so it's meant for long, hard agent jobs.

My read is that this is where the whole industry is landing: a premium model for difficult multi-step work, priced and positioned as the exception. And notice the timing. Going from cyber defenders only to every paying customer took about two weeks.

### AlphaProof Code and Signal

Here's what Carbon was doing behind the scenes. On Tuesday, Google DeepMind introduced AlphaProof Code, a system of AI agents for formal verification. That means writing mathematical proofs that a piece of software has a property, checked by a machine, not by a reviewer's judgment.

DeepMind says it used it to prove key security properties of the actual code behind the Signal protocol, the encryption behind the Signal messaging app. Not just the design on paper, but the compiled code that runs on devices. One example is that messages stay protected before and after a device is temporarily compromised.

The numbers: agents powered by Gemini Carbon wrote more than six million lines of proof in Lean, a proof language. Human experts reviewed the specifications, and the agents flagged about one percent of the codebase for human review. The whole thing took four weeks. DeepMind says comparable work used to take expert teams years.

To test it, the team put two previously fixed bugs back into a separate copy of the code. In both cases, the system produced a machine-checked proof that the buggy version was insecure. To be clear, DeepMind doesn't report finding any new bugs in Signal. Signal's CTO, Ehren Kret, is quoted in the post supporting the approach, and the team is open-sourcing its verification work.

Why this matters: we've spent weeks covering AI that finds exploits. This is the defensive version. Formal verification has always been too slow and expensive for most real code. If agents can do the grind in weeks, proving that critical software is correct could become normal practice.

The catch is that a proof only covers what you asked it to prove. My read is that the weak point moves to the specification, the human step of deciding which properties matter. The thing to watch is whether outside cryptographers go through the open-sourced proofs.

### Gemini Decisions API

One more from Google, and it's the one developers might use first. On Wednesday, Google launched the Gemini Decisions API, with a new model called Gemini Decision One. It doesn't write text. It answers closed questions: yes or no, pick one of several options, or score something on a scale. You can send up to a thousand questions in one request, and every answer comes with a confidence score between zero and one.

Pricing is ten cents per million input tokens, and output is free. Google says end-to-end latency is about one hundred fifty to two hundred fifty milliseconds.

This matters because a lot of what an agent does all day isn't writing. It's small decisions: which tool to call, whether a request breaks a policy, which model should handle a query. My read is that a cheap, fast model with a confidence score is plumbing that agent builders actually need, because a low score tells your code when to hand the case to a person.
