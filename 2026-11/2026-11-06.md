### 开场

这里是 Daily AI Signal，今天是 11 月 6 日，星期五。

### 头条：美国选民对数据中心说不，AI 股票大跌

先说这周最大的新闻。AI 的扩张，同时撞上了选票和股市。

周二是美国中期选举。除了国会选举，全美还有大约 41 项和数据中心有关的地方投票，其中 21 项在俄亥俄州。据 CNBC 统计，除了一项以外，结果都是反对数据中心。

俄亥俄的结果很一边倒。据《财富》杂志报道，至少 14 个市镇通过了禁令或者限制措施。其中一个小城，禁令以大约八比二的比例通过。另一个小城，大约百分之八十的选民，否决了为数据中心项目改用地性质。

到了周四，股市跟着反应。据 CNBC 报道，纳斯达克指数当天下跌百分之五点五，是 2025 年 4 月以来跌得最多的一天。英伟达和高通，都跌了百分之九以上，英伟达一天蒸发了将近 4500 亿美元市值。AMD，美光和应用材料，跌幅都超过百分之十。

原因不止一个。高通给出的业绩预期不好。但 CNBC 的分析，把选举放在了前面。投资人担心，民主党在国会取得优势，地方上又反对，再加上利率上升，资金压力大，AI 基础设施的建设，会慢下来。

同一天，OpenAI 发布了一份全国社区契约，还有一份政策蓝图。在这份蓝图里，OpenAI 把它的数据中心，叫作算力园区。据科技媒体 Engadget 报道，OpenAI 承诺，自己用的电，全额自己付，供电需要的电网升级，也自己出钱，不让周边居民分摊。每个社区都会有一份用水方案，优先用闭环或者低耗水的冷却方式，每个园区的用水和用电，都会公开。还有一条，OpenAI 说，不会再要求政府官员签保密协议，只保留安全信息，商业机密等少数例外。据彭博社报道，OpenAI 表示，这些内容准备了好几个月，在选举结果出来之前，就已经计划好了。

这件事真正重要的地方在于，过去我们说，AI 算力的瓶颈，是芯片，电力和资金。这周又多了两个，一个是当地居民的选票，一个是资本市场的信心。

我的判断是，数据中心已经变成了地方政治问题。接下来，其他大公司很可能都会拿出类似的承诺，拿地建园区的成本，还会继续上涨。

当然也要说清楚，这些都是地方投票，不是全州范围的投票。股市一天的下跌，原因也很复杂。OpenAI 的契约，目前也只能靠它自己执行，公布的数据，目前也没看到第三方核查的安排。

### 次要新闻：AI 让 Anthropic 的进步速度，大约翻了一倍

第二条，一个很关键的问题，AI 是不是已经在加速 AI 自己的研发？

这周早些时候，我们讲过 Anthropic 更新后的上市文件，里面提到，公司可能很快就会达到，它自己安全政策里定义的，由 AI 带来的大幅加速。周四，独立评估机构 METR，发布了一份外部评估，这是我们看到的第一份。

METR 从 8 月中旬到 10 月底，拿到了 Anthropic 的内部数据，包括内部模型的测试成绩，算力和效率数据，员工调查，还有大约 15 次访谈。不过，它没有直接调用还没发布的模型。

结论是，METR 判断，Anthropic 在定义清楚的任务上，模型能力提升的速度，大约是 2024 年 3 月到 2025 年 10 月的两倍。按 Anthropic 内部的能力指数算，是一点九倍。METR 也说，真实数字在一点三倍到三倍之间，都不意外。它的最佳估计是，现在的加速，大约百分之八十来自 AI 本身。METR 还说，没有发现能让这种快速进步停下来的明显瓶颈。

但故事还有另一半。METR 说，它认为最有信息量的证据，大部分被 Anthropic 删掉了。Anthropic 的理由是，这些信息可能帮助其他公司跑得更快。Anthropic 还撤回了一项原计划的核查，也就是算力在能力研发和安全研究之间怎么分配。METR 自己也承认，公众只能更多依赖它的判断，这并不理想。

我的判断是，AI 在加速 AI，已经不只是公司自己的说法了，有外部机构拿着内部数据说，大概是两倍。但证据由公司决定公开多少，外人还没法核实。

### 次要新闻：OpenAI 一次跑出 948 个 Linux 提权漏洞

第三条，网络安全。

周三，OpenAI 发了一篇文章。它用一个还没发布的网络安全模型，搭了一套自动化流程。OpenAI 说，这套流程一次运行，分析了 2308 个 Linux 内核的疑似问题，复现了 1724 个漏洞，最后做出 948 个本地提权漏洞的利用代码。简单说，就是一个普通用户，可以借这些漏洞，拿到整台机器的最高权限。OpenAI 说，这次一共花了大约 121 万美元的 API 费用，平均每个漏洞大约 1280 美元。

这套流程也会写修复补丁，再由人审核。OpenAI 说，已经把几百份报告和补丁，交给了内核维护者。但要说清楚，这不等于 948 个修复都已经推送到用户那里。

第二天，OpenAI 又发布了 GPT-6 Cyber 的系统卡。这是 GPT-6 Astra 专门面向授权安全工作的版本。按 OpenAI 自己的准备度框架，它在网络安全上被评为最高的危急级，和 Astra 一样。想用它，要经过身份核验，个人用户还要用实体安全密钥。OpenAI 没有说，跑 Linux 漏洞的，是不是这个模型。

我的判断是，找漏洞的成本降到这个程度，真正的瓶颈，就变成了审核和发布补丁的人。

### 次要新闻：Kimi K3.1 Pro 开放权重

第四条，一个跟进。

上个月，我们讲过月之暗面发布 Kimi K3.1 Pro，并承诺 11 月 4 日前开放权重。现在，权重已经放上了 Hugging Face。官方模型卡显示，总参数 2.8 万亿，每个 token 激活 1040 亿，上下文大约 100 万 token，支持图片输入。

两点提醒。第一，它用的是月之暗面自己的许可协议，不是标准的开源协议，商用之前要仔细看。第二，这么大的模型，能自己部署的机构很少。模型卡上的成绩，是月之暗面自己测的，还要等独立评测。

### 快讯

接下来是快讯。

Thinking Machines Lab 发布了 Inkling 2 Small，总参数 5940 亿，激活 170 亿。现在可以在它自己的 Tinker 平台上用，权重还没公开。公司说，完成外部安全测试之后，下周会用 Apache 2.0 协议，开放全部权重。

美国司法部，周四成立了超级智能工作组，由两位司法部高级官员牵头。目标是打击外国政府，跨国犯罪集团，还有诈骗者，滥用他们所说的超级智能。公告没有说明，超级智能具体指什么。

Anthropic 在 Claude 里推出了个人理财功能的测试版，面向美国的付费用户。账户通过 Plaid 连接，权限是只读的，Claude 不能动钱。据 TechCrunch 报道，它能看到最多两年的交易记录，相关对话不会用来训练模型。OpenAI 在 5 月，已经给 ChatGPT 上线过类似功能。

最后，据英国《金融时报》报道，微软放弃了一项选择权，这项选择权本来可以让它在 Nscale 得州数据中心的合同规模翻一倍以上。Nscale 正在准备上市，估值大约 350 亿美元。据报道，Nscale 正在和其他客户谈这个园区。

### 结语

今天这些新闻，其实是 AI 的两个限制，同时摆到了台面上。一个是物理的，土地，电，水，还有邻居同不同意。另一个是技术本身的速度，已经有外部机构说，AI 正在加速 AI。

接下来我最想看的，是其他公司会不会跟上 OpenAI 的契约。如果自己付电费，公开用水量，成了拿到许可的代价，那每一个还在图纸上的算力园区，账都要重新算。

### Today's top stories

Also today: an outside evaluator says AI has roughly doubled Anthropic's pace of progress, though most of its evidence was redacted. OpenAI says its systems turned out nine hundred and forty-eight Linux root exploits in a single run. And Moonshot's giant Kimi model is now downloadable.

### Main story — The AI buildout meets the ballot box

Start with Tuesday's midterms. Beyond the races for Congress, there were about forty-one local ballot measures on data centers across the country, according to Fortune. Twenty-one of them were in Ohio. By CNBC's count, voters went against data centers in all but one.

The Ohio results were lopsided. Fortune reports that at least fourteen Ohio municipalities passed bans or restrictions. In Trenton, a ban passed roughly eighty to twenty. In Wilmington, around eighty percent of voters rejected rezoning for a data center project across four separate referendums.

Then came Thursday. The Nasdaq fell five and a half percent, its steepest drop since April twenty twenty-five, according to CNBC. Nvidia and Qualcomm each fell more than nine percent, and Nvidia lost almost four hundred and fifty billion dollars in market value in one session. AMD, Micron and Applied Materials fell by double digits. Gizmodo put the Nasdaq's loss in market value at an estimated two and a half trillion dollars.

There was more than one cause. Qualcomm gave a disappointing forecast. But CNBC's account puts the election front and center: investors worry that Democratic gains in Congress, local opposition, rising rates and heavy capital needs add up to too much debt and too little revenue, and to a slower buildout.

The same day, OpenAI published what it calls a National Community Compact, along with a policy blueprint for what it calls compute campuses. According to Engadget's account of the announcement, OpenAI says it will pay the full cost of the electricity it uses, plus the grid upgrades needed to serve its sites, so neighbors aren't charged for them. It will write a water plan for each community, prioritize closed-loop or low-water cooling, and publish water and electricity use for each site. And it says it won't require public officials to sign non-disclosure agreements, apart from narrow exceptions such as security information and trade secrets. The blueprint asks lawmakers to make rules like these apply to the whole industry. According to Bloomberg, OpenAI says it had been working on this for months and planned it before the results came in.

Here's why this matters. Until now, the limits on AI compute were chips, power and financing. This week added two more: local voters who can block a site, and public-market investors who will reprice the entire supply chain the moment they think the buildout is slowing.

The line I'd pay most attention to is the one about non-disclosure agreements. The power bill is a cost OpenAI can absorb. Giving up secrecy in local negotiations changes how these deals get made, and it's the kind of concession that's hard to take back.

My read: data centers are now a local political issue, the way pipelines and wind farms became one. Expect every major builder to publish its own version of this compact, and expect the cost of getting a site approved to keep rising.

The catch: these were local measures, not statewide votes. One bad trading day had several causes, Qualcomm among them. And OpenAI's compact is enforced by OpenAI. We haven't seen any independent check on the numbers it promises to publish.

What to watch: whether other big builders sign similar commitments, and how this sell-off reads into Nvidia's next earnings report.

### Secondary — METR: AI has roughly doubled Anthropic's pace

Earlier this week we covered Anthropic's amended IPO filing, which warned that the company may soon meet its own safety policy's definition of dramatically accelerated progress driven by AI. On Thursday, the evaluation group METR published the first outside estimate of that acceleration we've seen.

METR spent from mid-August to late October with access to Anthropic's internal data: benchmark scores for internal models, compute and efficiency figures, staff surveys, and about fifteen interviews. It did not get direct access to unreleased models.

The headline: METR judges that Anthropic is likely improving its models on well-specified tasks at around twice the rate it did from March twenty twenty-four to October twenty twenty-five. On Anthropic's internal capability index, the estimate is one point nine times, with a confidence interval of one and a half to two point eight. METR adds it wouldn't be surprised if the truth were closer to one point three times, or three times. It says it is confident the speed-up did not come from scaling training compute faster. Its best guess is that around eighty percent of the current acceleration is due to AI itself. And it says it found no convincing bottlenecks to continued rapid progress.

Now the other half of the story. METR says Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence it found most informative. Anthropic had the right to redact any non-public information, and says it removed material that could help other developers go faster. Anthropic also withdrew a planned check of how it splits compute between capabilities and safety work. METR's own conclusion is that the public has to rely on its judgment much more than would be ideal.

This matters for two reasons. AI speeding up AI research is the core worry in frontier safety policies, and this is the first outside, data-backed estimate that it's happening at a top lab. And the redactions show the limit of the current model of oversight: the lab decides what the public gets to see.

The limits are real. METR's range is wide, the acceleration may be confined to well-defined tasks, and benchmarks may not track real-world impact. My read: the claim that AI is accelerating AI isn't just lab marketing anymore. But until evaluators can publish their evidence, outsiders can't check the work.

### Secondary — OpenAI's nine hundred and forty-eight Linux root exploits

On Wednesday, OpenAI published a post titled "A thousand fewer paths to root." It describes an automated pipeline powered by an upcoming, unnamed cyber model. In one run, it says, the pipeline analyzed two thousand three hundred and eight candidate Linux kernel findings, reproduced one thousand seven hundred and twenty-four of them, and produced nine hundred and forty-eight local privilege escalation exploits. Those are bugs that let a user with limited access take full control of the machine. OpenAI says the run cost one point two one million dollars in API credits, roughly twelve hundred and eighty dollars per bug.

The pipeline also wrote candidate patches, with human review, and OpenAI says it has sent hundreds of reports and patches to kernel maintainers. To be clear, that is not nine hundred and forty-eight fixes already shipped.

A day later, OpenAI published the system card for GPT-6 Cyber, a version of GPT-6 Astra tuned for authorized security work. OpenAI rates it Critical in cybersecurity under its Preparedness Framework, the same as Astra. Access goes through a program called Daybreak Red, with identity checks, IP allowlisting, and physical security keys for invited individuals. On OpenAI's own ExploitGym test, it scores about forty-eight percent, against about forty-two for Astra. OpenAI hasn't said whether this is the model behind the Linux run.

My read: when finding and weaponizing a kernel bug costs about as much as a used laptop, the bottleneck moves to the people who review and ship the fixes. Maintainers, not models, become the scarce resource. And if you run untrusted code on shared machines, the kernel boundary alone looks thinner this week. Put a sandbox in front of it.

### Secondary — Kimi K3.1 Pro weights are out

Last month we covered Moonshot's launch of Kimi K3.1 Pro, and its promise to release the weights by November fourth. They're now up. The official Hugging Face model card lists two point eight trillion total parameters, one hundred and four billion active per token, and a context window of about one million tokens, with image input.

Two caveats. It ships under Moonshot's own license, not a standard open-source one, so read it before any commercial use. And very few organizations can actually serve a model this size. The benchmark table on the card is Moonshot's own, and the card itself warns that the comparisons used different test setups. Independent results are what to wait for.

### Quick hits

Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Small, a mixture-of-experts model with five hundred and ninety-four billion parameters, seventeen billion active. It's on the company's Tinker platform now, with closed weights. The company says full weights come next week under Apache two point oh, after outside safety testing. It also says the model trails closed models like GPT-6.1 Sol on its alignment evaluations.

The Justice Department launched a Super Intelligence Task Force on Thursday. It's led by Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward and Associate Deputy Attorney General Stephen Weiss. Its stated job is to stop misuse of what the department calls super intelligence by foreign governments, criminal groups and fraudsters. The announcement doesn't define the term.

Anthropic launched a Personal Finance beta in Claude for Pro, Max and Ultra subscribers in the US. Accounts connect through Plaid, and access is read-only, so Claude can't move money. TechCrunch reports it can see up to two years of transactions, and that those conversations aren't used for training. OpenAI shipped a similar feature for ChatGPT in May.

And the Financial Times reports that Microsoft declined an option that would have more than doubled its compute contract at Nscale's Texas site. Nscale is preparing an IPO at around thirty-five billion dollars, and it's reportedly talking to other customers for the site.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that AI's two hardest constraints are now on display at once. One is physical: land, power, water, and the consent of the people who live next door. The other is the speed of the technology itself, with an outside evaluator saying AI is already accelerating a frontier lab's work.

The one thing I would watch next is whether the rest of the industry matches OpenAI's compact. If paying your own power bill and publishing your water use become the price of permission, that changes the economics of every campus on the drawing board.

That's Daily AI Signal for today. See you tomorrow.
