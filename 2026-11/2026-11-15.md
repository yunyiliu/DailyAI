### 头条

先说头条。美联储给 AI 热潮算了一笔账。

周五，美联储发布了一年两次的《金融稳定报告》。这份报告里，有两部分专门讲 AI，放在一起看，很有意思。

第一部分是一个调查。纽约联储的工作人员，问了 23 位市场人士，来自银行、券商、投资基金和咨询公司，未来一年到一年半，什么最可能冲击金融系统。排第一的，是 AI。据 CNBC 报道，百分之八十七的人提到了 AI，今年春天的调查里，这个比例只有百分之五十。排第二的，是风险资产大幅回调，百分之五十二。

他们担心的，主要是三件事。一是估值，如果 AI 公司的盈利不及预期，股价可能大跌。二是网络安全，可能来自国家背景的黑客，报告原话还提到，也可能来自前沿 AI 模型本身造成的破坏。三是报告说的，复杂的 AI 相关融资。

第三点，正好对上报告的另一部分。报告里有一个专栏，标题就叫，数据中心建设中的杠杆。

数字是这样的。截至今年 10 月，为先进算力融资发行的债务，超过了 6800 亿美元。2023 年全年，是 1300 亿美元，差不多是那时的五倍。2023 年，这类债务在非金融企业投资级债券发行里，占比不到百分之六，今年大约是百分之三十一。和先进算力相关的公开债务，存量已经超过 1.1 万亿美元。

但美联储说，债务还不是最大的数字。它统计了七家公司，亚马逊、谷歌母公司 Alphabet、博通、Meta、微软、英伟达和甲骨文。已经签了、但还没开始的租约，超过 1 万亿美元。照付不议合同和残值担保这些承诺，超过 2 万亿美元。美联储也提醒，这些都是没有折现的数字，不能和债务直接比。但规模确实很大。

这些钱是怎么转起来的？按美联储的描述，大型云厂商和数据中心开发商签长期租约，开发商拿着租约去借钱盖楼。芯片厂商会为 GPU 未来的价值做担保，帮 AI 公司融资买它的 GPU。大公司往往还会入股这些小公司。美联储用了一个词，循环融资，还特意加了引号。

我觉得最该划重点的，是这句话。美联储说，如果行情转差，这些大公司可能会自己扛下损失，减轻对整个金融系统的冲击。但同时，它们实际承担的风险，可能比资产负债表上显示的，要大得多。

还有一个数字。美国最大的几家银行控股公司，对大型科技公司、数据中心开发商、AI 相关的特殊目的公司、私募信贷和公用事业公司，加起来有接近 1 万亿美元的敞口。美联储说，这些都在每年的压力测试里。

这件事为什么重要？过去两年，大家问的是，AI 投入能不能赚回来。美联储问的是，如果赚不回来，损失由谁承担。它的回答是，大科技公司可能会扛下损失，但很多是通过合同和担保，看起来不像债务。

当然也要说清楚。调查只是 23 个人的看法，美联储明确说，不代表它自己的观点。上万亿的租约和担保没有折现，会高估它们今天的价值。美联储也没有预测崩盘，它是在告诉你，如果出事，损失会往哪里走。

我的判断是，时间点很关键。这周二，英伟达发财报，这是黄仁勋离开后的第一次，由代理首席执行官带队。周三，Anthropic 计划给上市定价。美联储刚刚问的问题，这周市场就要用价格来回答。

### 次要新闻

第二条，谷歌说，它的 AI Agent 团队，证明了一个 2013 年提出的数学猜想。

周五，谷歌研究院发了一篇长文，介绍它的编程工具 Antigravity 里，一个叫 teamwork 的功能。简单说，就是让很多个 Agent 同时干活，再配一个检查员，数学用 Lean 证明检查器，代码用测试，科学问题用模拟器。

谷歌说，这套系统证明了信息论里的 Courtade–Kumar 猜想，而且是完整证明。证明用 Lean 写成了形式化版本，大约 5000 万行代码，谷歌和合作高校的研究人员也参与了。另外，它还把著名的韦伊猜想的证明，转成了计算机可以逐行检查的版本，大约 180 万行。这不是新结果，但是把一个很长的人类证明，变成了机器能核对的东西。

我更关注的是背后的套路。Agent 负责生成，检查器负责把关。检查器不会被说服，这才让模型说的话，变成可以核对的东西。

不过，这些都是谷歌自己的说法。外部专家，还没有公开评价这个证明。5000 万行代码，也不可能有人逐行读，靠的是检查器，还有把问题本身设定对。

第三条，OpenAI 解散了独立的机器人团队。

据《财富》杂志周五的独家报道，OpenAI 已经拆掉了独立运作的机器人团队，OpenAI 也确认了这次调整。做机器人研究的人，并进了首席研究官 Mark Chen 的主研究团队。做机器人应用和硬件的人，并进了总裁 Greg Brockman 负责的应用部门。

为什么这么做？《财富》说，OpenAI 的研究人员，已经把 GPT-6 Astra 直接接到机器人上，用来完成一些任务。文章还引用了英伟达的一项研究，在一个模拟的物体操作测试里，用 Astra 驱动的 Agent，成功率是百分之七十四点八，排行榜上最好的专用机器人模型，是百分之五十一。

OpenAI 对《财富》表示，机器人仍然是公司长期愿景的核心。

我的判断是，这不是 OpenAI 放弃机器人，而是它认为，不需要单独做一个机器人大脑。这对专门做机器人基础模型的公司，是直接的挑战。但反方意见也有道理，麻省理工的 Russ Tedrake 说，机器人公司手里有真实场景的部署数据。而且模拟测试，毕竟不是工厂车间。

第四条，做 Agent 开发工具的 LangChain，融了 3 亿美元。

周五，LangChain 宣布完成 3 亿美元 C 轮融资，估值 30 亿美元，由谷歌旗下的 CapitalG 领投。它说，有超过 7000 家机构在用它的 LangSmith 平台，来运行、评估和管理 Agent。收入没有公布。

我的判断是，钱正在流向模型外面那一层，Agent 怎么运行，怎么测试，能碰哪些数据。问题是，模型公司自己也想做这一层。

### 快讯

接下来是快讯。

很多本地 AI 应用背后的开源推理引擎 llama.cpp，周五发布了 1.0 版本。最实际的变化是，以后它的接口如果有不兼容的改动，只会出现在大版本更新里。在它上面做开发的人，终于有了稳定的承诺。它目前支持超过 150 种模型架构变体。

Anthropic 的上市有了具体日期。据雅虎财经报道，Anthropic 计划周三定价，周四在纳斯达克开始交易，股票代码 ANT。

最后一条，和数学史有关。据英国《卫报》报道，赫尔辛基大学的逻辑学家 Jan von Plato，先把哥德尔一千多页的速记笔记转写出来，再交给 Claude 梳理。Claude 整理出 663 条定理，有哥德尔证明过的，也有只写了思路的。用证明助手检查，大约 400 条直接成立，大约 200 条要修改，大约 50 条是错的。外部专家还没有仔细审核。一位逻辑学家对《卫报》说，哪些归功于 Claude，哪些归功于哥德尔，要留给历史学家去判断。

### 结语

今天这些新闻放在一起看，AI 正在被外部的人检验。美联储在数债务，证明检查器在给数学打分。这周，英伟达周二的财报，Anthropic 周三的定价，会把美联储的问题，变成一个价格。我最想看的，不是英伟达的业绩超没超预期，而是它会不会谈到，它给自己的客户做的那些担保和投资。这正是美联储刚刚画出来的那个圈。

### Today's top stories

### Main story: the Fed counts the AI buildout

Here's what happened. On Friday, the Federal Reserve published its twice-yearly Financial Stability Report. Two parts of it are about AI, and they're worth reading together.

First, the survey. Staff at the New York Fed asked twenty-three market contacts, people at banks, broker-dealers, investment funds and advisory firms, what could shake the financial system over the next year to eighteen months. AI was the most cited risk. According to CNBC, eighty-seven percent of them named it, up from fifty percent in the spring. The next item on the list, a correction in risky assets, was at fifty-two percent.

The respondents named three worries. One, valuations: if AI earnings come in below expectations, stocks could correct. Two, cyber risk, from state hackers, or, in the report's words, from "damage caused by frontier AI models themselves." Three, what the report calls "complex forms of AI-related financing."

That third one is where the second part of the report comes in. There's a box on what the Fed calls leverage in the data center buildout, and it has real numbers.

Debt issued to finance advanced computing passed six hundred and eighty billion dollars by October of this year. In all of twenty twenty-three, it was a hundred and thirty billion. So, about five times as much. In twenty twenty-three, this kind of borrowing was under six percent of investment-grade bond issuance by non-financial companies. This year it's about thirty-one percent. Outstanding public debt tied to advanced computing is now over one point one trillion dollars.

Now the part most headlines will skip. The Fed says the debt isn't the biggest number. It looked at seven companies: Amazon, Alphabet, Broadcom, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia and Oracle. Leases that are signed but haven't started yet are worth more than one trillion dollars. Take-or-pay contracts and residual value guarantees are worth more than two trillion. Those are undiscounted figures, and the Fed says they aren't directly comparable to debt. But they're large.

Here's how the money moves, in the Fed's description. A cloud giant signs a long lease with a data center developer. The developer uses that lease to borrow and build. Chip makers guarantee what their GPUs will be worth later, which helps AI companies finance buying those GPUs. And on top of that, the big firms often invest equity in the smaller ones. The Fed puts the phrase "circular financing" in quotation marks, but it uses it.

And then the line I'd underline. The Fed says these big companies could absorb losses in a downturn, which would soften the blow for the rest of the system. But it also says their economic exposure "may be substantially larger than suggested by their on-balance-sheet liabilities."

One more number. The largest U.S. bank holding companies have close to one trillion dollars of exposure to big tech firms, data center developers, AI-related special purpose vehicles, private credit funds and utilities. The Fed says those exposures are checked in its annual stress test.

Why does this matter? For two years, the question about AI spending was whether it would pay off. The Fed is asking a different question: if it doesn't, who takes the loss? Its answer: the biggest tech companies may absorb the losses, through contracts and guarantees that don't look like debt. And the riskier slices sit with high-yield funds, life insurers and private credit.

A few cautions. The survey is twenty-three people's opinions, and the Fed says plainly that it doesn't represent the Fed's own view. The trillion-dollar lease and guarantee numbers aren't discounted, so they overstate what those obligations are worth today. And the Fed isn't forecasting a crash. It's mapping where losses would travel.

My read is that the timing is what makes this interesting. Nvidia, the company whose guarantees and investments sit in the middle of that circle, reports earnings on Tuesday, under an interim CEO. And Anthropic, a big buyer of that compute, plans to price its IPO on Wednesday. This week, public markets get to vote on the same question the Fed just asked.

### Google: agent teams and a twelve-year-old conjecture

On Friday, Google Research published a long post about a feature in its Antigravity coding tool called teamwork. It runs many AI agents in parallel, and pairs them with something that checks their work: the Lean proof checker for math, test suites for code, simulators for science.

The headline claim: Google says these agent teams proved the Courtade–Kumar conjecture, an information theory problem from twenty thirteen, in full generality. Google says the proof was formalized in Lean, which produced about fifty million lines of code, with human researchers from Google and academic partners involved.

It also says a related framework produced a machine-checked formalization of the Weil conjectures, about one point eight million lines of Lean. That's not a new result. It's turning a famous, very long human proof into something a computer can check line by line.

Google is also offering free access, at its highest usage tier, for six months, to ten thousand academic researchers.

The interesting part is the pattern: agents that generate, and a verifier that can't be talked into anything. The verifier is what turns "the model says" into something you can check.

The catch: these are Google's claims. The authors say they verified and edited the proofs before publishing, but independent experts haven't weighed in on the Courtade–Kumar proof yet. And fifty million lines of Lean is not something a human reads. You trust the checker, and you check that the statement was set up right.

### OpenAI breaks up its robotics team

Fortune reported on Friday that OpenAI has disbanded its stand-alone robotics team, and OpenAI confirmed the reorganization. The robotics researchers now sit in the main research group under chief research officer Mark Chen. The applied robotics and hardware people moved to the Applied organization under president Greg Brockman.

Why? According to Fortune, OpenAI researchers have hooked GPT-6 Astra straight up to robots and used it directly for some tasks. Fortune cites an Nvidia study where an Astra-powered agent hit seventy-four point eight percent success on a simulated manipulation benchmark, against fifty-one percent for the best specialized robot models on that leaderboard.

OpenAI told Fortune robotics is still a core part of its long-term vision. Fortune also reports OpenAI is building its own humanoid, with Sam Altman aiming for a first public demo in twenty twenty-seven.

My read: this isn't OpenAI leaving robotics. It's OpenAI saying it doesn't need a separate robot brain. That's a direct challenge to the companies building robot-specific foundation models. The counterpoint came from MIT's Russ Tedrake in the same piece: robotics companies have real-world deployment data that a chatbot company doesn't. And a simulated benchmark is not a factory floor. Fortune didn't say how many people were affected.

### LangChain raises three hundred million dollars

LangChain announced a three hundred million dollar Series C on Friday, at a three billion dollar valuation, led by CapitalG. It says more than seven thousand organizations use LangSmith, its platform for running, evaluating and governing agents. It didn't disclose revenue.

This matters because the money is moving to the layer around the model. Not the model itself, but the runtime, the testing, and the controls on what agents can spend and touch. The catch is that the model makers want that layer too. Anthropic opened a private beta of self-hosted managed agents just this week. LangChain says it'll show new LangSmith features this Friday.

### Quick hits

llama.cpp, the open-source engine behind a lot of local AI apps, hit version one point oh on Friday, along with the ggml library under it. The practical change: from now on, breaking changes to its C API only come with a new major version. If you build on llama.cpp, that's the stability promise you've been waiting for. It supports more than a hundred and fifty model architecture variants.

Cloudflare open-sourced Kitesurf, its browser for AI agents that runs entirely on its Workers platform, under the Apache two point oh license. It runs page JavaScript and renders screenshots without a full browser container, and tools like Puppeteer can connect to it. Cloudflare calls it experimental and under heavy development.

Anthropic's IPO now has dates. Yahoo Finance reports the company plans to price its shares on Wednesday and start trading on Nasdaq on Thursday, under the ticker A N T.

And a story from the history of math. The Guardian reports that Helsinki logician Jan von Plato used Claude to work through Kurt Gödel's notebooks from the early nineteen-forties, more than a thousand pages, mostly in an obsolete German shorthand that von Plato had already transcribed. Claude listed six hundred and sixty-three theorems Gödel proved, sketched or had an idea for. Checked in a proof assistant, about four hundred held as stated, about two hundred needed repairs, and about fifty were false. The most striking find: a nearly complete proof of a result Gödel claimed in nineteen sixty-six he'd had decades earlier, off by one wrong definition. Outside experts haven't reviewed it yet. One logician told the Guardian that how much is Claude and how much is Gödel "is a job for the historians."

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that AI is being judged by outsiders now. The Fed is counting the debt. Proof checkers are grading the math. And this week, Nvidia's earnings on Tuesday and Anthropic's IPO pricing on Wednesday turn the Fed's question into a price. The one thing I'd watch is not whether Nvidia beats. It's whether it says anything about the guarantees and investments it has made in its own customers. That's the circle the Fed just drew.

That's Daily AI Signal for today.
