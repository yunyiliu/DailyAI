### 开场

这里是 Daily AI Signal，今天是 11 月 8 日，星期日。

### 头条：微软在算力上，开始挑了

先说头条。微软在 AI 算力上，开始变得挑剔了。

据彭博社报道，微软正在逐步结束一份向甲骨文租用 AI 算力的合同。这份合同是 2023 年签的，当时是为了支撑 AI 版的必应搜索。知情人士说，微软决定不再续约，最近几个月一直在陆续关掉这部分租来的算力。原因是，这批服务器用的是英伟达的老一代芯片，已经不符合微软的要求。合同的具体金额，彭博没有查到。

两天前我们讲过，据英国金融时报报道，微软放弃了在 Nscale 得州数据中心扩大合同的选择权。这一次，是第二个信号。

彭博还提到一个更大的背景。微软已经承诺，向 CoreWeave、Nscale、Nebius 这类新兴云服务商，租用超过 800 亿美元的算力。报道说，微软对这些合作的高成本和工期延误越来越不满，已经收紧了对数据中心项目的审查，退出了一些它认为风险高的项目，内部也在讨论，要不要再签新的合同。

甲骨文的回应是，市场对它 AI 基础设施的需求依然强劲，老一代的 GPU 也一样。甲骨文 9 月还说过，用了四年的 GPU，续约价格比原来高出百分之二十。微软没有置评。

同一周，还有一条关注度不高的消息。知名机器学习研究者 John Langford 在个人博客上写道，微软研究院的 AI Frontiers 团队，已经结束了。这个团队大约 50 人，做过多智能体框架 Autogen，和能操作电脑的智能体 Fara。Langford 说，团队在 9 月初被并入了微软 AI 部门，而那边的思路，用他的话说，是只做规模扩展，刻意不做研究。他安顿好团队成员之后，离开了微软。我们没有看到微软对此的回应。

为什么把这两件事放在一起看？

我的判断是，它们指向同一个方向。微软在两件事上都变得更挑剔了，一是租谁的 GPU，二是为什么样的研究花钱。钱都在往最新的芯片和规模扩展上集中。

第一点的影响，不只在微软。现在很多 AI 融资，都建立在一个假设上，一块 GPU 买回来，能持续赚钱五六年。数据中心租约，用芯片做抵押的贷款，新兴云厂商的估值，都靠这个假设。现在一个承诺租用超过 800 亿美元算力的大租户说，老芯片不合用了，这对这个假设是一个反面的信号。当然，甲骨文的说法正好相反。对不同的客户，两种情况可能同时存在。但一块用了三四年的 GPU 到底值多少钱，这个问题已经摆到了台面上。

也要说清楚局限。这只是一份老芯片的合同，更大范围的审查，也来自匿名信源。这不说明微软在 AI 上整体少花钱了，只说明它想换一种花法。

接下来最值得看的，是微软和其他新兴云厂商的合同到期时，会不会续约。

### 次要新闻

第二条，一笔机器人收购，在中国被叫停了。

去年 10 月，软银同意收购瑞士 ABB 集团的机器人业务，企业价值大约 54 亿美元。ABB 现在表示，软银通知它，中国的外商投资安全审查工作机制办公室，已经禁止这笔交易中，对 ABB 机器人中国业务的收购。ABB 说，双方保持密切沟通，正在建设性地推进交易。

这件事和 AI 的关系，在于报道里的原因。据英国金融时报报道，有关部门担心，机器人数据会被转移到海外，用来训练 AI 模型。报道还提到，审查中还要求保留一部分在中国的生产能力。而且，这笔交易今年 2 月已经通过了中国的反垄断审查。所以这次不是竞争问题，而是安全审查。

报道普遍认为，这对软银押注的物理 AI，也就是用在机器人和机器上的 AI，是一次挫折。据报道，中国是这块机器人业务最大的市场。

我的判断是，过去几年，芯片被各国当成战略资源，现在机器人数据也开始被这样对待。示范动作、工厂日志、传感器数据，这些是训练机器人模型的原料，而且很稀缺。做物理 AI 的公司，如果数据来自某个国家的工厂，以后要先想清楚，这些数据能不能出境。

当然，这些原因来自金融时报的信源，不是官方说法。交易也没有彻底终止，调整之后的方案，比如把中国业务拆出去，仍然有可能推进。

第三条，是昨天那条芯片新闻的后续。

昨天我们讲到，据路透社报道，大约 60 万块英伟达 H200 芯片堆在香港，等待中国批准进口。这个周末，审视来自美国这一边。

据纽约时报报道，美国商务部批准了一家叫 New Matrix 的北京公司，把 H200 芯片组装成 AI 服务器。但记者去它登记的地址，看到的是一栋空壳楼，天花板上还垂着电线。报道说，这家公司的股东，和一家 2024 年被美国列入实体清单的投资机构有关联。另一家获批的公司，办公室看起来也是空的。纽约时报也说明，没有发现芯片流向中国军方的证据。美国商务部回应说，每一张许可证都考虑了国家安全风险。

另外，据彭博社报道，英伟达现在要求东南亚的云服务商，如果想拿到英伟达官方云合作伙伴的身份，就必须接入它的 Fleet Intelligence 监控软件。这套软件能记录数据中心里每一块芯片的位置。

还有，美国众议院三位委员会主席，要求黄仁勋在 11 月 18 号，也就是英伟达发布财报的第二天，出席一场闭门简报会，说明他和一家云计算公司 Megaspeed 高管的接触。英伟达回应说，地区云客户拿到的只是普通的商业信息，和国家安全无关。

我的判断是，出口管制正在从纸面规定，走向实地核查，查买家到底是谁，查芯片到底在哪。最值得注意的，是英伟达把芯片定位和合作伙伴身份挂钩，这等于不等法律出台，先把核查变成了做生意的条件。

### 快讯

接下来是几条快讯。

AI 抢产能，已经挤到了普通芯片。芯片厂商 Microchip 的首席执行官 Steve Sanghi 在财报电话会上说，AI 公司把整座工厂都包了下来，把其他人都挤了出去。他说的是外包的封装和测试产能。标准产品的交货周期，从原来的 4 到 8 周，拉长到了 6 到 32 周。周五，Microchip 股价下跌百分之十。

Grok 接口输出的文字，开始默认加上水印。根据开发者文档，从 11 月 7 号起，七个 Grok 模型输出的文字，都带有一种看不见的统计水印，返回信息里会标明是否加了水印。想关掉，必须确认自己符合法律上的例外情况。文档说，这样做是为了符合欧盟人工智能法案。

一名安全研究者用 AI 智能体，跑了大约 1.76 万次登录测试，在 OpenSSH 里找到一个从 2016 年就存在的漏洞。在 Alpine 这类基于 musl 的 Linux 系统上，特定设置下，攻击者可以登录本来不允许用密码登录的账户。新版 OpenSSH 已经修复，用 Alpine 容器又开着密码登录的，建议尽快更新。

最后，Anthropic 发布了一篇关于智能体记忆的研究文章。它把记忆分成几层，还加了一个离线环节，叫做“做梦”，在空闲时回顾过去的对话记录，纠正错误的笔记，把一些笔记提到记忆的最上层。在一项 25 个问题的内部测试里，其中一种设置下，随着运行的轮次增加，得分从百分之十三，提高到百分之四十八。Anthropic 自己也提到，做梦需要额外算力，一条错误的笔记，也可能跟着智能体进入之后的每一轮。

### 结语

把今天的新闻放在一起看，AI 背后的实物资源，正在被逐一核查。微软在核查，哪些芯片还值得租。北京在核查，机器人数据流向哪里。华盛顿在核查，谁在买 GPU，英伟达也开始追踪，芯片放在哪里。

接下来最值得关注的，是 11 月 17 号和 18 号，先是英伟达发布财报，然后是众议院要求黄仁勋出席的闭门简报。

### Hook

A company that has committed more than eighty billion dollars to renting AI computing power is handing some of it back. Not because it needs less. Because it wants newer.

### Today's top stories

Today: Microsoft winds down an AI capacity deal with Oracle, and one of its research groups shuts down. China blocks SoftBank from buying ABB's robotics business in China, reportedly over robot data. And Washington takes a hard look at who's been cleared to buy Nvidia's H200 chips.

This is Daily AI Signal for Sunday, November eighth.

### Main story: Microsoft gets choosy

Two days ago we covered a Financial Times report that Microsoft passed on an option to more than double its compute contract at Nscale's site in Texas. Now there's a second data point.

Bloomberg reports that Microsoft is winding down a deal to rent AI computing capacity from Oracle. The deal dates back to twenty twenty-three, when Oracle agreed to provide servers for the new AI version of Bing. According to people familiar with the matter, Microsoft declined to renew the contract and has been turning off that rented capacity in recent months. The reason, per Bloomberg: the Oracle hardware runs older-generation Nvidia chips, and it no longer fits Microsoft's specifications. Bloomberg couldn't determine the full size of the deal.

Here's the bigger picture. Bloomberg says Microsoft has committed more than eighty billion dollars to rent capacity from so-called neoclouds, companies like CoreWeave, Nscale and Nebius. It reports that Microsoft has grown frustrated with high costs and delays, has tightened oversight of data center projects, has stepped away from projects it sees as risky, and has debated internally whether to sign new neocloud deals at all.

Oracle's response: demand for its AI infrastructure remains strong, including for earlier-generation GPUs. Back in September, Oracle said it was renewing contracts for four-year-old GPUs at a twenty percent premium. Microsoft declined to comment.

Now the second Microsoft story, which got much less attention. John Langford, a well-known machine learning researcher, wrote on his blog on Friday that Microsoft Research's AI Frontiers group is over. His words: "So AI Frontiers is over." It was about fifty people. It built Autogen, the multi-agent framework, and Fara, a computer-use agent. Langford says the group was reorganized into Microsoft AI in early September. He describes that organization's philosophy as, quote, "scaling, and aggressively not research." He placed his team as best he could, and he's left Microsoft. We haven't seen a Microsoft response to the post.

So why put these together?

My read: they point the same way. Microsoft is getting choosier on two fronts: whose GPUs it rents, and what research it pays for. In both cases, the money is concentrating on the newest hardware and on scaling.

The first part matters well beyond Microsoft. A lot of AI financing rests on one assumption: that a GPU bought today keeps earning money for five or six years. Data center leases, chip-backed loans, neocloud valuations, all of it. When a renter that size says older chips no longer fit its needs, that's a data point against the assumption. Oracle says the opposite, that old GPUs are renewing at a premium. Both can be true for different buyers. But the question of what a three- or four-year-old GPU is worth is now out in the open.

The catch: this is one contract for older chips, and the wider review is anonymous-source reporting. It isn't a sign that Microsoft is spending less on AI overall. It's a sign it wants to spend it differently.

The one thing to watch: whether other neocloud contracts with Microsoft come up for renewal, and whether they're renewed. That's where this becomes a trend, or doesn't.

### Secondary: China blocks a robot deal over data

Next, a deal that just hit a wall in China.

In October of last year, SoftBank agreed to buy ABB's robotics business, at an enterprise value of about five point four billion dollars. ABB now says SoftBank told it that China's foreign investment security review office has prohibited the acquisition of the ABB Robotics business in China. ABB says the two companies are in close dialogue and working constructively on the transaction.

What makes this an AI story is the reported reason. According to the Financial Times, Chinese authorities were concerned that robotics data would be transferred overseas and used to train AI models. Reports also mention a separate demand to keep some manufacturing capacity in China. The deal had already cleared China's antitrust review in February. So this wasn't about competition. It was a security review.

Coverage calls it a setback for SoftBank's push into physical AI, meaning AI for robots and machines. China is reported to be the robotics unit's biggest market.

Here's my read. For years, governments have treated chips as strategic. Now robot data is getting the same treatment. Demonstrations, factory logs, sensor data: that's the raw material for training robot models, and it's scarce. If you're building physical AI and your data comes from a factory in one country, expect regulators to ask where it goes.

The limit: the reasons come from FT sources, not an official Chinese statement, and the deal isn't dead. A restructured version, maybe without the China business, could still go ahead.

### Secondary: Washington looks at the H200 pipeline

Yesterday we covered Reuters' report that about six hundred thousand Nvidia H-two-hundred chips are waiting in Hong Kong for Beijing's approval. This weekend, the scrutiny came from the American side.

The New York Times reported that the Commerce Department cleared a Beijing company called New Matrix to assemble H-two-hundred chips into AI servers. When reporters went to its listed address, they found what the Times described as a hollow shell with wires hanging from the ceiling. The company's owners have ties to Wise Road, a private equity firm the US blacklisted in twenty twenty-four. The Times says a second approved company also appeared to have an empty office. The Times did not find evidence that chips went to China's military. Commerce told the paper that every license weighs national security risk. Nvidia said it makes no sense for China's military to depend on American technology.

Second, Bloomberg reports that Nvidia now requires cloud providers in Southeast Asia to enroll in Fleet Intelligence, its monitoring software, if they want official Nvidia Cloud Partner status. The software records the location of individual chips in a data center. It's not clear whether this applies to existing partners or outside the region.

And three House committee chairs have asked Jensen Huang for a closed-door briefing on November eighteenth, the day after Nvidia's earnings, about his contacts with an executive at Megaspeed, a cloud company. Nvidia says its regional cloud customers get "ordinary business information," and zero information relevant to national security.

Why does this matter? Export controls are moving from rules on paper to checks on the ground: who the buyer really is, and where the chip physically sits. The notable part, in my view, is Nvidia tying location tracking to partner status. That turns verification into a business requirement, without waiting for a law.

For context, Nvidia says H-two-hundred shipments to China were under one percent of its data center revenue in the quarter that ended in July. So this is less about Nvidia's numbers and more about the rules of the game.

### Quick hits

The AI chip crunch is now hitting ordinary chips. On its earnings call, Microchip's CEO Steve Sanghi said AI companies "came in and took over the whole factory. They pushed everybody out." He was talking about outside packaging and testing capacity. Lead times for standard parts went from four to eight weeks, to six to thirty-two weeks. The stock fell ten percent on Friday.

Grok's API now watermarks text by default. According to the developer docs, starting November seventh, output from seven Grok models carries an invisible statistical watermark, and a response header tells you when it's on. Teams can switch it off only by confirming a legal exception applies. The docs say it's there to comply with the EU AI Act. If you build on Grok, the text you ship is now marked unless you opt out.

A researcher used AI agents to run about seventeen thousand six hundred login tests against OpenSSH, and found a flaw that had been there since twenty sixteen. On Linux systems built on the musl library, like Alpine, with certain settings, attackers could log into accounts that weren't supposed to accept passwords. It's fixed in OpenSSH ten point six p two. If you run Alpine containers with password login on, update.

And Anthropic published a research post on how its agents remember. It splits memory into layers, plus an offline pass it calls "dreaming" that reviews old sessions, fixes wrong notes and moves notes up the memory stack. On one internal test of twenty-five questions, in one setup, scores rose from thirteen percent to forty-eight percent of graded criteria as the agent ran more sessions. Anthropic's own caveats: dreaming costs compute, and a wrong note can follow the agent into every session.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that AI's physical inputs are getting audited. Microsoft is auditing which chips are worth renting. Beijing is auditing where robot data goes. Washington is auditing who buys GPUs, and Nvidia is starting to track where they sit.

The one thing I'd watch next is November seventeenth and eighteenth: Nvidia's earnings, then the closed-door briefing House chairs have asked Jensen Huang for. That's when we'll hear how much of this changes how chips get sold.
