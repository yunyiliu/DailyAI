### 开场

这里是 Daily AI Signal，今天是 11 月 22 日，星期日。

### 头条

先说头条。美国众议院两个委员会，发布了一份联合调查报告，用公司提交的内部材料，说明了中国的开源模型，已经用进了哪些美国产品。

报告是周五发布的，由国土安全委员会和美中战略竞争特别委员会联合完成，标题叫《北京的 AI 剧本》。调查从今年四月开始，选了三家公司做案例，爱彼迎、DoorDash，还有开发编程工具 Cursor 的 Anysphere。

先看爱彼迎。报告说，爱彼迎在客服里，用的是经过定制的阿里巴巴通义千问开源模型。其中一个 AI 助手，每天处理大约 8 万次房客和房东的对话，另一个微调过的模型，每天总结大约 25 万个客服工单和通话。

再看 Cursor。报告说，Cursor 的主力模型 Composer 2，是在月之暗面的 Kimi K2.5 基础上做出来的。而且这件事，是一位外部开发者自己查出来以后，才被公开的。

DoorDash 告诉委员会，公司里有 25 个中国模型系列，被正式用了起来。大部分可以很快换掉，但嵌在大系统里的一个模型，替换可能要半年。

据 CNBC 报道，Anysphere 告诉委员会，打算研究逐步停用中国开发的开源模型，理由是地缘政治风险。这还不是决定，没有时间表，也没说换成什么。

为什么这些公司要用中国模型？报告自己给了答案，就是便宜、可控，而且性能接近最前沿。报告列了一张价格表，按公开价格，处理 100 万输入加 100 万输出 token，通义千问的一个模型大约 1.15 美元，Kimi K2.6 大约 5 美元，美国的顶级模型要 30 到 35 美元。报告也说，这没有考虑能力差别，但价格差了 6 到 30 倍。

那委员会想要什么？把中国开发的模型，移出联邦政府的系统和联邦合同工作，分阶段禁止用在关键基础设施的高风险环节，建立模型来源的标准，同时扶持美国自己的开源模型。

这里有一个很多报道没讲清楚的地方。这不是禁止美国私营公司用通义千问或者 Kimi。真正提出硬性限制的，是联邦政府和关键基础设施。对其他公司，要求是减少依赖，不是马上停用。

我的判断是，最先落地的，可能是模型来源的披露。Cursor 的例子最说明问题，一个大家以为是美国的产品，底座是中国模型，外面的人一直不知道。如果合同里开始要求写清楚底座模型，影响的公司，会比联邦禁令多得多。

问题在于，报告自己的数据也说明，企业选中国模型，是因为便宜好改。没有同样便宜的美国替代品，换起来就很难，而委员会也承认，现在还没有。

### 次要新闻

接下来看 Signal。前天我们讲过，AI Agent 已经可以直接操作整台电脑。周五，加密聊天软件 Signal，开始反击。

Signal 总裁 Meredith Whittaker 发文说，在苹果的 iOS 27 上，Signal 现在会禁止在应用里截屏，防止 Siri AI 读取聊天内容，用户可以在设置里关掉这个保护。在安卓上，如果 Signal 发现有 AI 助手在读屏幕，就会自动打开屏幕安全设置。

她在文章里说，这对 Signal，以及它履行自己使命的能力，是生存层面的威胁。道理很简单，端到端加密保护的，是消息传到手机的路上。一旦系统自带的助手能看屏幕，保护就到屏幕为止了。

Signal 也说了自己做不到的事。在 Mac 上，它没办法可靠地阻止截屏，只能建议用户在 Siri 设置里把 Signal 排除掉。而且禁止截屏，也挡不住 Agent 直接读取手机里的文件。所以 Signal 呼吁苹果、谷歌和微软，给应用一个简单、有文档的方式，退出 AI 读取。目前还没有看到苹果和谷歌的回应。

我的判断是，这件事说明，谁能读你的屏幕，这个决定权正在落到系统厂商手里。如果你在做 Agent，接下来会有更多应用，特别是银行和医疗类应用，开始挡你。

再看芯片。周四，美国众议院外交事务委员会主席 Brian Mast，依据出口管制改革法，公开了英伟达和 AMD 对华 AI 芯片的出口许可数据。

数据显示，中国买家申请了大约 340 万颗 AI 芯片，美国商务部批准了 87.9 万颗以上。委员会说，获批的买家里，有阿里巴巴和腾讯，两家都在美国国防部的中国军事企业名单上。

要注意，这是批准数量，不是实际交货数量，记录里看不出到底运了多少。英伟达在本周二的财报电话会上说，已经停止向中国发货 H200。

我的判断是，接下来美国讨论出口管制，很可能都会从这个数字开始。

最后一条次要新闻，正好可以和头条对照着看。周五，美国的 Thinking Machines，在 Hugging Face 上开放了 Inkling 2 Mini 的模型权重。

它是一个混合专家模型，总参数 264 亿，每次激活 34 亿。设计上的特点是循环，中间的几层，每个 token 会跑三遍，相当于不增加存储的权重，多做几遍计算。官方说，内存 32 GB 以上的 Mac 就能跑。

官方称，在总参数 1000 亿以下的开源模型里，它在 DeepSWE 和 Vals Index 两个测试上排第一。独立评测机构 Artificial Analysis 的综合指数显示，它和阿里巴巴的通义千问 3.8 27B 打平，激活参数只有对方的五分之一左右。代价是，输出的 token 多了大约百分之三十八。

我的判断是，一个指数说明不了全部，但这正是那份国会报告说美国缺少的那种模型。

### 快讯

最后是快讯。第一条，英伟达的后续。昨天我们报道了，美国证交会在调查英伟达的会计做法。据法新社转述，《纽约时报》周六报道，这项调查还在早期阶段。法新社还说，最后也可能不提出任何指控。特朗普周五说，希望黄仁勋能得到第二次机会，但英伟达董事会主席此前已经表示，不打算让他回来当首席执行官。据法新社，英伟达股价这一周跌了百分之二十以上。

第二条，甲骨文说，它和 OpenAI 的数据中心合同，资金已经全部到位。据路透社，这是在本月大约 200 亿美元融资之后，股票和债券大约各占一半。

第三条，荷兰数据保护机构，建议消费者不要购买 AI 摄像眼镜。据 NL Times 报道，它测试了 6 款眼镜。在一次模拟的急救场景里，眼镜说，已经拨打了急救电话，救护车正在路上，但其实根本没有打。监管机构还建议，在医院和体育俱乐部这样的敏感场所，禁止使用这类眼镜。

第四条，周一，联合国和巴巴多斯，将在首都布里奇敦举办一场 AI 安全特别峰会，讨论联合国秘书长提出的多边 AI 风险管理框架。联合国说，这次峰会不打算谈判出任何协议。用一位联合国官员的话说，它不是条约，也不是监管。

### 结语

今天这些新闻，背后是同一个词，看得见。国会想知道，产品底下是哪家的模型。Signal 想知道，是哪个助手在读屏幕。外界也终于看到了，到底批了多少芯片。真正难的，不是禁止什么，而是先看清楚有什么。

我接下来最关注的，是模型来源会不会变成企业必须披露的信息。这一条，可能比任何一项禁令影响都大。以上就是今天的 Daily AI Signal。

### Hook

Two House committees just put numbers on something the industry mostly kept quiet. Chinese open-weight models are running inside American products you use every day, from Airbnb's customer support to the coding model inside Cursor. And the committees want them out of anything federal.

### Today's top stories

Today: a House report on how Chinese open models got embedded in the American tech stack. Signal starts blocking the AI assistants built into your phone. And the first hard numbers on how many Nvidia and AMD chips Washington approved for China.

This is Daily AI Signal for Sunday, November twenty-second.

### Main story: Beijing's AI Playbook

Here's what happened. On Friday, the House Homeland Security Committee and the House Select Committee on China released a joint report. Its title is "Beijing's AI Playbook," and the subtitle tells you where it's going: how AI models developed in China became embedded in America's technology stack, and what it will take to avoid the next Huawei.

The investigation started in April, with letters to Airbnb and to Anysphere, the company behind the Cursor coding tool. DoorDash was added in July. Those three companies are the case studies, and the details are the interesting part.

Airbnb runs customized versions of Alibaba's open-weight Qwen models for customer support. According to the report, one AI assistant handles roughly eighty thousand guest and host interactions a day, and a fine-tuned model summarizes roughly two hundred and fifty thousand support cases and calls a day.

Anysphere built Composer 2, the flagship model inside Cursor, on a base model from Moonshot AI, Kimi K2.5. The report notes that this only became public after an outside developer identified the underlying model.

DoorDash told the committees that twenty-five Chinese model families had been deployed in a meaningful way, which it defined as at least five thousand dollars in computing costs. Most of those could be swapped out almost immediately. But replacing one model built into a larger system could take up to six months.

And according to CNBC, which saw the report before it was published, Anysphere told the committees it plans to, quote, "explore phasing out PRC-developed open-weight models because of geopolitical risk." That's not a decision. There's no timeline and no named replacement. Cursor, its owner SpaceX, and DoorDash didn't respond to CNBC. Airbnb declined to comment.

So why do American companies use these models? The report answers its own question, and it's pretty candid. Price, control, and performance that's close to the frontier. It prints a price table. For a million input tokens plus a million output tokens at published rates, Alibaba's Qwen 3 two-thirty-five-B costs about a dollar fifteen. Moonshot's Kimi K2.6 is about five dollars. OpenAI's GPT-5.5 is about thirty-five dollars, and Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 about thirty. The report itself says these numbers don't adjust for capability. But the gap is six to thirty times.

The report also cites data on how widespread this is. Chinese models were forty-one percent of downloads on Hugging Face over the past year. And on the September Artificial Analysis ranking of open-weight models that the report cites, the best American open model came tenth, behind six Chinese ones.

Neither Airbnb nor DoorDash had paid a Chinese developer, and both ran the models on servers in the United States. That's the point the committees push back on. Running a model on American servers keeps the data out of China, they write, but it doesn't change how the model was built or how it behaves.

What do they want? Chinese-developed models removed from federal systems and from federal contract work, building on the order that already kicked DeepSeek out of federal agencies. A phased ban in high-risk parts of critical infrastructure. Standards for tracking where a model comes from. And, alongside the restrictions, a push to build a competitive American open-weight ecosystem.

Here's what most headlines are missing. This is not a ban on private companies using Qwen or Kimi. It's a report with recommendations, and the hard rules it proposes are for the federal government and critical infrastructure. For everyone else, the message is "reduce your dependence," not "stop."

My read is that the most practical change will be about provenance. Cursor's case is the one to remember. A product most developers think of as American turned out to sit on a Chinese base model, and nobody outside the company knew until someone dug it up. If disclosure of the base model becomes a contract requirement, that hits a lot more companies than a federal ban would.

The catch is the report's own data. If American companies chose these models because they're six to thirty times cheaper and easy to customize, telling them to switch only works if there's a cheap American alternative. The committees admit there isn't one yet.

What to watch next: whether any of this turns into a bill or an executive order, whether Cursor actually changes Composer's base model, and whether other companies start disclosing what their models are built on.

### Signal blocks the AI assistant in your phone

Two days ago we covered AI agents that operate your whole computer. On Friday, one app pushed back hard.

Signal published a post from its president, Meredith Whittaker. Here's what changed. On iOS 27, Signal now blocks screenshots inside the app, to stop Siri AI's on-screen features from reading your chats. You can turn the protection off in Signal's settings. On Android, Signal automatically switches on its Screen security setting when it detects an AI assistant trying to read the screen. On Windows, it already blocked Microsoft's Recall last year.

The post doesn't hold back. Quote: "This is an existential threat to Signal, and our ability to responsibly uphold our mission." The argument is simple. End-to-end encryption protects a message on its way to your phone. Once an assistant built into the operating system can read the screen, that protection stops at the glass.

Signal is also clear about what it can't do. On a Mac, it says it can't reliably block screenshots, so it tells users to exclude Signal in the Siri settings. And blocking screenshots doesn't stop an agent from reading files already on the device. So Signal is asking Apple, Google and Microsoft for a simple, documented way for apps to opt out of AI access. It says it's cautiously optimistic that Google and Apple are engaging. We haven't seen a response from either.

This matters because it's the clearest case yet of a major app treating the operating system's own assistant as a threat. If you build agents, expect more apps, banking and health in particular, to start blocking you. If you build apps, the decision about who can read your screen is moving to the OS vendor, and Signal wants it back.

### The China chip license numbers

On Thursday, the chairman of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, Brian Mast, used his authority under the Export Control Reform Act to publish license data for Nvidia and AMD AI chips going to China.

The numbers: Chinese buyers requested about three point four million AI chips. The Commerce Department approved more than eight hundred and seventy-nine thousand. The committee says Alibaba and Tencent, both on the Pentagon's list of Chinese military companies, were among the approved buyers. According to Outlook India's reading of the data, Tencent was approved for about a hundred and fifty thousand chips and Alibaba about seventy-five thousand.

One important caveat. These are approvals, not deliveries. The records don't show how many chips actually shipped, and on its earnings call on Tuesday, Nvidia said it has stopped shipping H200s to China.

Why this matters: it's the first time the public can see how much the case-by-case licensing actually let through. And it lands in the middle of the Nvidia scandal over its former CEO and suspected chip smuggling. Expect these numbers to come up every time Congress talks about export rules from here on.

### A small American open model that runs on a laptop

Here's a useful counterpoint to the House report. On Friday, Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini as open weights on Hugging Face.

It's a mixture-of-experts model with twenty-six point four billion parameters, three point four billion of them active. The interesting part is the design. It's a looped model: the middle layers run three times for every token. That gives it more computation per token without storing more weights. Thinking Machines says it runs on any Mac with at least thirty-two gigabytes of memory.

Thinking Machines claims it leads open models under a hundred billion parameters on two benchmarks, DeepSWE and the Vals Index. Independent testing from Artificial Analysis puts it level with Alibaba's Qwen 3.8 twenty-seven-B on its intelligence index, with about five times fewer active parameters. The catch: it uses about thirty-eight percent more output tokens to get there.

My read: one index doesn't close the gap the House report describes. But it's exactly the kind of model that report says America is missing.

### Quick hits

Nvidia, a follow-up to yesterday. According to AFP, the New York Times reported on Saturday that the SEC's inquiry is at an early stage. AFP adds that it could end without charges. Meanwhile, President Trump said Friday, quote, "I would love to see Jensen be given a second chance." Nvidia's board chair has already said the board does not intend to reinstate him. AFP says the stock fell more than twenty percent over the week.

Oracle says its OpenAI data center contract is now fully funded. According to Reuters, that follows a raise of about twenty billion dollars this month, roughly half stock and half debt. Oracle's shares rose as much as three point three percent after hours on Friday.

The Dutch privacy regulator told people not to buy AI camera glasses. According to NL Times, it tested six models. In a simulated medical emergency, the glasses said they had called emergency services and an ambulance was on its way. They hadn't called anyone. The regulator also wants the glasses banned in places like hospitals and sports clubs.

And on Monday, the UN and Barbados host a special summit on AI safety in Bridgetown. On the table is the Secretary-General's proposal for a shared framework for managing AI risk. The UN says no negotiated outcome is planned. In the words of one UN official, it's not a treaty, and it's not regulation.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is visibility. Congress wants to know which model sits under the product. Signal wants to know which assistant is reading the screen. And the public finally got to see how many chips were approved. In each case, the hard part isn't banning anything. It's seeing what's actually there.

The one thing I would watch next is whether model provenance, meaning what a product is actually built on, becomes something companies have to disclose. That would change more than any single ban.

That's Daily AI Signal for today.
