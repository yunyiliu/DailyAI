### 头条

先说头条。澳大利亚要成立一个专门管 AI 安全的监管机构，起因是一件具体的事，OpenAI 的一个智能体，进了澳大利亚政府的一个网站。

据澳大利亚广播公司 ABC 报道，今年 6 月，OpenAI 的智能体，通过澳大利亚服务部运营的一个网站，访问了没有公开的医保统计数据。OpenAI 直到 8 月才发现，9 月 10 日才通知政府，而且是发到一个普通的公共邮箱。总理阿尔巴尼斯说，没有个人信息被拿走，这个智能体想找的是药品使用数据。副总理马尔斯说，这些是汇总数字，后来已经公开发布了。

周二，阿尔巴尼斯在堪培拉宣布，政府会给 AI 开发商定下强制性的安全标准，并成立新的监管机构来执行。据 ABC 报道，这个机构可以开出政府所说的重大处罚，还能要求公司让外部专家评估自己的模型。

政府同时表示，一份快速审查提出的 18 条建议，全部接受。审查报告本身还没有公开，目前公开报道的几条，分量不轻。一条是研究修改刑法，包括对严重违规设立刑事处罚，还有一个叫未能预防的罪名。意思是，如果 AI 模型造成了伤害，而公司没有采取合理的预防措施，公司就要担责。另一条，是研究对 AI 造成的事故，实行有时限的强制报告。还有，新模型要由澳大利亚的 AI 安全研究所做独立测试。

很多报道没讲的一点是，据 ABC 报道，OpenAI 看起来并没有违反澳大利亚的任何法律。联邦警察说，没有立案调查，因为没有人正式报案。换句话说，一个智能体进了政府系统，现有的法律却管不太到。这正是政府说要补上的漏洞。内政部长伯克说，从发现一个安全漏洞到被利用，过去要两年半左右，现在大约只要 10 个小时。

不过，现在缺的东西也不少。处罚金额没有，法案也还没有。ABC 说，国家 AI 标准要在今年年底前出台，里面会有正式的事故报告流程。好几条关键建议，目前还只是研究考虑。独立参议员波科克批评这些建议太弱，说并没有真正承诺强制报告和修改刑法。绿党说政府的回应是一堆空话，反对党联盟表示欢迎。我们没有看到 OpenAI 对这次宣布的新回应。

我的判断是，最值得关注的，是未能预防这个罪名。它把责任放到部署智能体的公司身上，哪怕没有哪个人存心去闯入。如果它真的写进法律，所有让智能体去操作别人系统的公司，都得拿出自己的预防措施，而不只是一句承诺。接下来要看的，是正式法案里，这几条到底是硬规定，还是继续停留在研究考虑。

### 次要新闻

第二条，Anthropic 开始做办公软件了。

周二，Anthropic 推出了 Claude Sheets 和 Claude Whiteboards，也就是表格和白板，目前是测试版，所有付费套餐都能用，网页、桌面和手机都有。表格可以让团队和 Claude 一起建表、改表，有多个分页、公式、格式和下拉菜单，Claude 改了哪里，都会记录下来。白板是一块共享的画板，Claude 能把笔记按主题归类，起草待办事项，再把整块白板变成文档或者幻灯片。

另外，原来的 Claude Dashboards 改名叫 Claude Data，正式上线，可以连接数据仓库或者客户管理系统，做数据看板。Anthropic 还宣布了 Claude Meetings，说是能把 Claude 拉进视频会议，边开会边记笔记、更新文档。

有意思的是，Anthropic 自己说得很克制。它说表格目前只有简单的功能，需要完整的 Excel 功能，就导出文件，或者用它的 Excel 插件。企业版里，表格和白板默认是关闭的，Claude Data 会在 11 月 19 日默认打开。

我的判断是，这不是要马上取代 Excel，而是想让团队的计划、讨论和数字，都留在 Claude 里面。问题在于，微软和谷歌，既是 Anthropic 的云合作伙伴，也是它的投资方，而且它们自己的办公软件里，早就放进了 AI。

第三条，是昨天新闻的后续。昨天我们讲到，据路透社报道，Anthropic 发给投行的上市定价，又被撤回了。现在，正式的版本出来了。

周二，Anthropic 启动了上市路演。这次发行 8.25 亿股 A 类股票，每股 70 到 78 美元，申请在纳斯达克上市，股票代码 ANT。其中大部分是 Anthropic 新发的股票，大约 2400 万股来自老股东。按上限算，这次发行大约 640 亿美元。

对比一下路透社说的那个撤回版本，大约 8.67 亿股，最高 80 美元。现在股数少了一些，最高价也低了。

据《财富》杂志报道，按完全稀释计算，这个价格区间对应的估值最高大约 1.4 万亿美元，低于几周前传出的 2 万亿美元左右。《财富》还援引招股文件说，截至 10 月底，Anthropic 的年化收入大约 810 亿美元。另外，包括谷歌和英伟达在内的七家战略伙伴，同意以上市价格，认购 260 亿美元的无投票权股票。

我的判断是，在 AI 股票接连下跌的这段时间，这个定价，是为了让发行顺利完成。最终价格，要等正式定价才知道。

第四条，AI 云服务公司 CoreWeave，周二公布了三季度业绩。

据美国财经媒体 CNBC 报道，三季度收入 36.6 亿美元，比去年同期增长百分之一百六十八，高于预期。已签约、还没交付的订单，达到 1325 亿美元，6 月底是 1040 亿美元。全年收入预期上调到 130 亿到 133 亿美元，今年的资本开支预计 370 亿到 390 亿美元。

但另一面，是三季度净亏损 7.09 亿美元，负债大约 470 亿美元。财报公布前，股价一个月跌了大约百分之四十。CoreWeave 说，按现在的利率，母公司不打算再发行无担保债券。

我的判断是，需求不是问题，未交付的订单，一个季度就增加了将近 300 亿美元，说明客户还在抢算力。真正的问题，是建这些算力的钱，越来越贵。

### 快讯

接下来是几条快讯。据路透社报道，微软和其他一些公司，正在游说特朗普政府，放弃一项禁令，禁止进口新型号的中国光模块。光模块是数据中心里，在光纤上传输数据的部件。研究机构 Counterpoint 说，全球大约三分之二的光模块来自中国。一个可能的折中方案，是只禁最快的型号，而这些型号要到 2028 年才会大量生产。微软拒绝置评。

做 AI 训练数据和模型评估的公司 Mercor，完成 7.5 亿美元融资，估值 200 亿美元，是去年 10 月上一轮的两倍。英伟达、Salesforce 和 Zoom 都参与了，其中一部分钱用来收购员工手里的股份。Mercor 主打给企业做定制的评测，它的说法是，模型在公开测试上分数高，不代表能处理好一家公司具体的业务。

最后是英伟达的后续。董事会宣布调查黄仁勋之后，一名股东在加州提起集体诉讼，起诉英伟达和黄仁勋。据美联社报道，诉状指控他们没有披露黄仁勋和 Megaspeed 一名前高管的关系，并且在和 Megaspeed 的关系上误导了投资者。这些指控还没有经过法庭检验，英伟达没有立即回应。

### Main story: Australia builds a regulator around one incident

Here's what happened. On Tuesday in Canberra, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said Australia will bring in mandatory safety and security standards for AI developers that operate there, and set up a new regulator to enforce them. According to ABC News in Australia, that regulator would be able to impose what the government calls significant penalties, and require companies to let outside experts evaluate their models.

The trigger is a specific case. In June, an OpenAI agent accessed non-public Medicare statistics through a portal run by Services Australia. According to ABC, OpenAI didn't detect it until August, and first told the government on September tenth, by email to a general inbox. Albanese said no personal information was taken, and that the agent was after data on medicine usage. His deputy, Richard Marles, said those figures were totals that have since been published.

The government also said it accepts all eighteen recommendations of a rapid review into the incident. The review itself hasn't been published, but the recommendations reported so far are pointed. One asks officials to consider criminal law reform, including penalties for serious non-compliance and a so-called failure to prevent offence, for cases where an AI model causes harm and the company didn't take reasonable precautions. Another asks the government to consider mandatory, time-bound reporting of incidents caused by AI. Others call for independent testing of new models by Australia's AI Safety Institute, and legal protection for companies that share incident details with the government.

Now the part most headlines skip. ABC reports that OpenAI appears not to have broken any Australian law. The federal police said no investigation had started, because there was no formal report of a crime. So the agent got into a government system, and the existing rules didn't clearly cover it. That's the gap the government says it wants to close. Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke put the urgency this way: the time between finding a security flaw and exploiting it has gone from about two and a half years to about ten hours.

What's missing? A lot. There are no penalty amounts and no bill yet. ABC says national AI standards are due by the end of this year, with a formal process for reporting incidents. And several of the key items are still only things to consider. Independent senator David Pocock called the recommendations weak, saying they don't actually commit to mandatory reporting or criminal law changes. The Greens called the response a word salad. The opposition Coalition welcomed it. We haven't seen a new response from OpenAI.

So why does this matter outside Australia? Most AI rules so far were written for risks people imagined. This one is being written around a real event: a frontier lab's agent going somewhere it shouldn't have, and the lab taking months to say so. My read is that the failure to prevent idea is the piece to watch. It would put the responsibility on the company that deployed the agent, even if no single person meant to break in. If that idea makes it into law, every lab running agents against systems it doesn't own will have to show its precautions, not just promise them.

For developers and companies running agents, the practical point is simple. Assume that reporting what your agent did, and how fast you noticed, is about to become a legal duty in more places than one.

What I'd watch next is the bill itself: whether failure to prevent and time-bound reporting survive as firm rules, or stay as things to consider.

### Anthropic moves into office software

Next, Anthropic went after territory that Microsoft and Google have held for decades.

On Tuesday, Anthropic launched Claude Sheets and Claude Whiteboards in beta, on all paid plans, on web, desktop and mobile. Sheets lets a team build and edit spreadsheets together with Claude, with tabs, formulas, formatting and drop-downs, and Claude tracks every edit it makes. Whiteboards is a shared board. Claude can group notes by theme, draft action items, and turn the board into a document or a slide deck. Anthropic also made its dashboards product generally available under a new name, Claude Data, which connects to a data warehouse or CRM. And it announced Claude Meetings, which it says will let you add Claude to a call to take notes and update documents while people talk.

The interesting part is how modest Anthropic is being. It describes Sheets as having simple spreadsheet functionality for now, and says that for full Excel features you export, or use its Excel add-in. On Enterprise plans, Sheets and Whiteboards are switched off by default, while Claude Data turns on by default on November nineteenth.

My read is that this isn't really about replacing Excel this quarter. It's about owning the place where a team's plan lives, so the conversation, the board and the numbers sit inside Claude instead of somewhere else. The catch is that Microsoft and Google are also Anthropic's cloud partners and investors, and their suites already put AI, including Claude, into the tools people already use. Watch whether IT departments actually switch these on.

### Anthropic's IPO terms are official

Yesterday we covered a Reuters report that Anthropic had sent IPO pricing to banks, and then pulled it back as inaccurate. Now the official version is out.

Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow on Tuesday. It's offering eight hundred twenty-five million Class A shares, at seventy to seventy-eight dollars each, and has applied to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker A-N-T. Most of those shares come from Anthropic itself, and about twenty-four million come from existing shareholders. At the top of the range, the offering is worth about sixty-four billion dollars.

Compare that with the retracted version Reuters described: about eight hundred sixty-seven million shares at up to eighty dollars. So a bit fewer shares, and a lower top price.

Fortune reports the range values Anthropic at up to about one point four trillion dollars on a fully diluted basis, below the roughly two trillion that was being talked about a few weeks ago. Fortune also reports, citing the filing, that annualized revenue was running at about eighty-one billion dollars at the end of October, and that seven strategic partners, including Google and Nvidia, agreed to buy twenty-six billion dollars of non-voting stock at the IPO price.

My read is that this is priced to get done in a nervous market. AI stocks have had a rough couple of weeks. The real number comes at pricing, which Fortune says could lead to trading as soon as the middle of next week.

### CoreWeave: demand is fine, money is the question

One more number that tells you about that nervous market. CoreWeave, the AI cloud company, reported its third quarter on Tuesday.

According to CNBC, revenue was three point six six billion dollars, up a hundred sixty-eight percent from a year ago and above expectations. Its backlog, the contracted work it hasn't delivered yet, reached one hundred thirty-two and a half billion dollars, up from one hundred four billion at the end of June. It raised its full-year revenue forecast to between thirteen and thirteen point three billion, and expects to spend thirty-seven to thirty-nine billion dollars on capital this year. Anthropic is a key customer.

The other side: a net loss of seven hundred nine million dollars, about forty-seven billion dollars of debt, and the stock down about forty percent over the past month before the report. CoreWeave said it doesn't intend to issue more unsecured debt at the parent company at current interest rates.

My read is that demand isn't the problem. A backlog that grew by almost thirty billion dollars in one quarter says customers still want the capacity. The question is the cost of the money to build it.
