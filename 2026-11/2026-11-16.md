### 头条

先说头条。美国财政部长公开要求，英伟达下一任首席执行官，要从外面找。

上周四，黄仁勋辞去了英伟达首席执行官的职务。周日，美国财政部长贝森特在福克斯新闻的节目里说，事情没有到此为止。

他说，英伟达需要一次彻底的改组，董事会应该到公司外面，去找下一任首席执行官。据法新社报道，他还说，希望在 10 天之内，得到英伟达和内部调查给出的答复。

他最关心的是一个问题。原话的意思是，英伟达是不是在推动这些看起来违规的销售，来抬高自己的收入和利润。

先交代一下背景。黄仁勋辞职，起因是他和一家新加坡公司 Megaspeed 的一位前高管之间的关系。据 CNBC 报道，美国官员怀疑这位前高管为中国从事间谍活动，Megaspeed 也被怀疑，违反出口管制，把英伟达芯片转运到中国。Megaspeed 表示，自己的业务合法合规。

贝森特说，那位前高管是不是间谍，对他来说不重要。他要查的是，英伟达在出口管制上，有没有合规的文化。他提到，英伟达在中国的销售下降的时候，新加坡的收入却在上升。他还打了个比方，如果英伟达是一家银行，这样不合规，银行监管机构现在已经进驻了，很多高管会被撤换。

这里要说清楚。这些是财政部长在电视上提出的疑问，不是调查结论，目前没有任何一项被证实。法新社提到，英伟达上个月说过，去年它调查过和 Megaspeed 有关的指控，并且予以否认。《财富》杂志说，周日它向英伟达求证，没有马上得到回复。

同一天，还有两件事。

美国众议院中国问题特别委员会主席穆勒纳尔，在 CBS 的节目里说，黄仁勋已经同意，接受三个众议院委员会的问询，英伟达的代表也会参加，具体日期还没定。

另一边，想让黄仁勋回来的声音也越来越大。《金融时报》报道说，黄仁勋和身边的人，讨论过回归的可能。这是《金融时报》引用知情人士的说法，由《财富》转述。公开的是一封联名信，要求董事会在独立调查查清事实期间，让黄仁勋回来。据法新社报道，马斯克等硅谷人士周日签了名，另外还有超过 16 万 5 千人签名。

这件事为什么重要？一周前，这还是一个人的问题。贝森特把它变成了一家公司的问题，收入里，有没有不该发生的销售，谁知情。

我的判断是，英伟达董事会现在两头受压。一头是股东和十几万个签名，希望创始人回来。另一头是财政部长，要外部人选，还在问，合规上的问题，是不是一直出到了最高管理层。财政部决定不了英伟达的首席执行官，但英伟达需要的出口许可，在政府手里。据法新社报道，美国司法部也在进行刑事调查。

也要看到另一面。贝森特正在和商务部公开争执，为什么 Megaspeed 一直没有被列入实体清单。所以这些话里，也有部门之间的较量。

接下来看什么？英伟达明天发财报。据法新社报道，上周它的股价跌了超过百分之十二，市值少了大约 5730 亿美元。这是代理首席执行官带领的团队，第一次公开回答问题。

### 次要新闻

第二条，十个国家签了一份 AI 供应链的宣言。

周日，在旧金山，美国和九个伙伴国家，签署了《旧金山宣言》。它属于美国国务院主导的 Pax Silica 计划，也就是美国拉盟友一起做的 AI 供应链合作。

签字的十个国家是，美国、澳大利亚、印度、以色列、日本、韩国、卡塔尔、新加坡、阿联酋和英国。宣言承诺一起保护两类东西。一类是敏感技术，包括先进 AI 背后的产品、软件、材料和制造能力。另一类是基础设施，包括港口、物流、通信和能源网络。各方还计划成立五个工作组。

值得注意的有两点。第一，新加坡签了字，而 Megaspeed 就是一家新加坡公司。第二是时间。据韩国《中央日报》报道，中美两国元首，计划周三到周四，在深圳的亚太经合组织会议期间会面。这家报纸还引用知情人士的话说，宣言文本里，没有点名中国。

问题在于，这是一份宣言，加上几个工作组。我看到的报道里，都没有提到有约束力的规则，或者时间表。真正要看的，是工作组会不会拿出共同的执法办法，尤其是芯片经第三国转运这个问题。

第三条，美团的开源大模型，部署文档是按华为芯片写的。

两周前，我们讲过美团的 LongCat-2.5，当时权重还没有放出来。现在，权重已经上了 Hugging Face，用的是 MIT 许可证，可以商用。

这个模型很大。按美团的模型卡，总参数 1.6 万亿，每个 token 激活大约 480 亿。它能处理文字和图片，主要面向长时间的编程和 Agent 任务。

我更关注的不是分数，而是部署文档。美团给出的示例，是一个 194 张华为昇腾加速卡的集群，2 张负责图像编码，64 张负责读入提示词，128 张负责生成回答。文档里没有提到英伟达的 GPU。模型卡还提到，在国产 AI 加速器上做了基础设施优化，但没有说是哪种芯片，用了多少。

再说分数，这些都是美团自己的数据，其中一部分是内部测试，模型卡也写了，仅供参考。在编程 Agent 测试 Terminal Bench 2.1 上，美团报告的成绩是 88.4，和 DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 的 90.6、Claude Opus 5 的 89.1 接近。在更难的软件工程测试 DeepSWE 上，它是 64.6，DeepSeek 是 74.2，差距比较明显。

我的判断是，真正重要的是，开源权重，加上国产硬件上的整套推理方案，现在都公开了。不过，194 张加速卡，是云厂商和大企业的规模，小团队用不起。目前也还没有第三方的测试结果。

第四条，一个让 AI Agent 和商家打交道的协议，发布了 1.0 版本。

上周我们讲过，美联航修改条款，禁止 AI Agent 代客订票。今天这条，是相反的思路。

AI 创业公司 Sierra，发布了个人 Agent 协议的 1.0 版本，简称 Poppy。简单说，商家放一个文件，告诉来访的 AI Agent，它可以做什么。用户自己登录，决定自己的 Agent 能访问哪些东西。

Sierra 说，Shopify 上的每一家店铺，现在都会自动发布这个文件，商家不用做任何事。Meta 的 Agent 产品 Muse，访问每个网站时，都会先找这个文件。又有 60 家公司成为设计伙伴，包括达美航空、凯悦、Intuit 和 Salesforce，加起来超过 100 家。

这对开发者意味着，面对 Agent，商家以前只有两个选择，要么封掉，要么被爬。现在有了第三个。问题在于，设计伙伴不等于已经上线。Sierra 的文章里，也没有出现 OpenAI、谷歌和亚马逊。这种协议，要大的 Agent 都来用，才有意义。

### 快讯

最后是一条快讯。

据 The Decoder 报道，谷歌的 Gemini 4 Carbon，登上了数学测试 MathArena 的第一名，得分百分之八十五点四。OpenAI 的 GPT-6.1 Sol 是百分之八十点六。但成本差得很远，The Decoder 说，Carbon 的测试成本，大约是 Sol 的 15 倍。

### 结语

今天这几条新闻放在一起看，AI 芯片越来越不像一件普通商品，更像一种被管控的资产。财政部长在问它是怎么卖出去的，十个国家承诺一起守住供应链，一家中国公司公开了一套跑在国产芯片上的方案。我最想看的，是明天英伟达的财报电话会。不是收入数字，而是有没有人，正面回答贝森特的那个问题。

### Main story: Washington wants a different Nvidia

Here's what happened. On Sunday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent went on Fox News and talked about Nvidia. Jensen Huang resigned as CEO last Thursday. Bessent's point was that the resignation doesn't settle anything.

He called for a "dramatic shakeup" at the company. He said the board should look outside Nvidia for its next chief executive. And according to AFP, he said he wants answers from Nvidia and from its internal investigation within the next ten days.

Then he asked the question that matters most. Quote: "were they pressing these seemingly illicit sales to goose their revenues and their earnings?"

Some context. Huang stepped down after reports about his relationship with a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company. According to CNBC, US officials suspect her of spying for China, and Megaspeed is suspected of diverting Nvidia chips to China in violation of export controls. Megaspeed says it runs a legitimate and lawful business.

Bessent said he doesn't care about that part. His words, per AFP: "It doesn't matter to me whether she's a spy or not." What he wants to know is whether Nvidia had what he called a culture of compliance around export rules. He pointed to a rise in Nvidia's reported Singapore revenue while its China sales fell. And he made a comparison that will get attention in boardrooms. Quote: "if this were a bank and they hadn't complied like this, the OCC would be in there right now and a lot of the executives would be removed." The OCC is a federal bank regulator.

To be fair to Nvidia: these are questions from a cabinet secretary on television, not findings. Nothing has been proven. AFP notes that Nvidia said last month it had investigated the Megaspeed allegations last year and refuted them. Fortune says Nvidia didn't immediately respond to a request for comment on Sunday.

Two more things happened the same day.

On CBS, Congressman John Moolenaar, who chairs the House select committee on China, said Huang has agreed to be questioned by three House committees: Foreign Affairs, Intelligence, and the China committee. Nvidia representatives will come in too. There's no date yet. Moolenaar also said the case raises the importance of getting export controls into the annual defense bill, and that this will be his focus for the next few weeks.

And the pull in the other direction is getting stronger. The Financial Times reports that Huang has talked with confidants about the possibility of coming back. That's the FT's reporting, from unnamed sources, relayed by Fortune. What's public is an open letter asking the board to, quote, "bring Jensen back while an independent investigation establishes the facts." AFP says Elon Musk and other Silicon Valley leaders signed it on Sunday, joining more than a hundred and sixty-five thousand other signatures.

So here's why this matters. A week ago, this was a story about one man's judgment. Bessent has turned it into a question about the company: did the revenue include sales that shouldn't have happened, and who knew?

What most headlines are missing is how boxed in the board is. On one side, shareholders and a very long petition want the founder back. On the other, the Treasury Secretary wants an outsider and is asking whether the compliance failures go all the way up to the C-suite. The interim CEO, Colette Kress, is also the CFO, and it's the company's numbers Bessent is asking about.

My read: Treasury doesn't pick Nvidia's CEO. But the government controls the export licenses Nvidia needs, and according to AFP, the Justice Department is carrying out a criminal probe. That's the leverage.

There's a catch on Bessent's side too. He's in a public fight with the Commerce Department over why Megaspeed was never blacklisted. He says other departments pushed for it, and Commerce "had the pen." So some of this is a turf war.

What to watch: Nvidia reports earnings tomorrow. AFP says the stock lost more than twelve percent last week, about five hundred and seventy-three billion dollars in market value. It's the first time the interim team has to take questions in public. And CNBC, citing Semafor, reports that Bessent, the Commerce Secretary and the President are all expected in China this week for the APEC summit.

### Ten countries sign an AI supply chain pact

On Sunday in San Francisco, the United States and nine partners signed something called the San Francisco Declaration. It's part of Pax Silica, the State Department's AI supply chain initiative.

The ten signers are the US, Australia, India, Israel, Japan, South Korea, Qatar, Singapore, the United Arab Emirates and the UK. They commit to protecting two things together. One is sensitive technology: the goods, software, materials and manufacturing know-how behind advanced AI. The other is infrastructure: ports, logistics, communications and energy networks. They also plan five working groups, including one on supply chain security hubs.

The State Department's line is, quote, "No nation can secure the foundations of the SI era alone." SI is its shorthand for super intelligence.

The interesting part is the timing and one name on the list. Singapore signed, and Singapore is where Megaspeed is based. And Korea JoongAng Daily reports that Presidents Trump and Xi are due to meet at APEC in Shenzhen on Wednesday and Thursday. The same paper quotes a source saying the text doesn't single out China.

The catch: this is a declaration and some working groups. None of the reports I read describes a binding rule or a deadline. Germany, the Netherlands and Sweden are expected to sign as well, according to the State Department's Jacob Helberg. I'd watch whether those working groups produce shared enforcement on chips re-exported through third countries. That's the gap the Megaspeed case is about.

### Meituan's open model runs on Huawei chips

Two weeks ago we covered LongCat two point five, a model from Meituan, the Chinese delivery giant. Back then the weights weren't out. Now they are, on Hugging Face, under the MIT license, which allows commercial use.

It's big. Meituan's model card says one point six trillion parameters in total, with about forty-eight billion active per token. It takes text and images, and it's aimed at long coding and agent tasks.

Here's what changed, and it's not the benchmark table. It's the deployment guide. Meituan's sample setup is a cluster of one hundred and ninety-four Huawei Ascend accelerator cards: two for image encoding, sixty-four for reading the prompt, one hundred and twenty-eight for generating the answer. The guide doesn't mention Nvidia GPUs. The model card also credits infrastructure optimizations on domestic AI accelerators, though it doesn't say which chips or how many.

On performance, these are Meituan's numbers, and some are in-house, which the card itself calls indicative only. On Terminal Bench two point one, a coding agent test, Meituan reports eighty-eight point four. That's close to DeepSeek's V four point one Flash at ninety point six and Claude Opus 5 at eighty-nine point one. On a harder software engineering test, DeepSWE, it trails clearly: sixty-four point six against seventy-four point two for DeepSeek.

My read: the bigger story is that a full recipe, open weights plus a serving stack on Chinese hardware, is now public. The catch is the size. A hundred and ninety-four accelerators is a job for a cloud provider, not a startup. And nobody independent has tested it yet.

### A protocol for agents that businesses actually want

Last week we told you United Airlines changed its contract to ban AI agents from booking tickets. Here's the opposite approach.

Sierra, an AI startup, released version one of the Personal Agent Protocol, which everyone calls Poppy. The idea: a company publishes a file that tells a visiting AI agent what it's allowed to do, and the customer signs in and decides what their agent can access. Sierra says it's built mostly on existing standards like MCP and OpenAPI, with sign-in handled through OAuth.

Two things make this more than a spec. Sierra says every Shopify store now publishes a Poppy file automatically, with no work from the merchant. And Meta's Muse agent now looks for Poppy first on every site it visits. Sierra says sixty more companies joined as design partners, including Delta Air Lines, Hyatt, Intuit and Salesforce, for a total of more than a hundred.

This matters because businesses have had two choices with agents: block them, or get scraped by them. This is a third. The catch: design partner doesn't mean live. And Sierra's post doesn't list OpenAI, Google or Amazon. A protocol like this works only if the big agents show up.

### Quick hits

Google's Gemini 4 Carbon now tops the MathArena math benchmark, according to The Decoder. Carbon scored eighty-five point four percent. Claude Fable five point two was second, and OpenAI's GPT six point one Sol third at eighty point six. But look at the cost. The Decoder says Carbon cost about fifteen times as much as Sol to run, and Fable about forty times. One Carbon run launched a script that went for more than eleven hours.

Apple launched a study app in the US to learn how people use AI for health questions, TechCrunch reports. It's built with OpenEvidence, answers are grounded in medical journals, and it will take up to two hundred thousand participants. Apple says the data won't be used to train AI models.

And a follow-up. Fractile, the British chip startup we flagged two weeks ago, made its round official last week. TechCrunch puts it at seven hundred and twenty million dollars, and the UK government, which invested, says the company is valued at over five billion pounds. Fractile says its first chips and systems ship next year.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that the AI chip is being treated less like a product and more like a controlled asset. A Treasury Secretary is asking how they were sold. Ten governments are promising to guard the supply chain. And a Chinese company just published a way to run a very large model on Chinese ones. The one thing I would watch next is tomorrow's Nvidia call. Not the revenue number. Whether anyone on that call answers Bessent's question.

That's Daily AI Signal for today.
