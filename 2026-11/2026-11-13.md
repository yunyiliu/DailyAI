### 开场

这里是 Daily AI Signal，今天是 11 月 13 日，星期五。

### 头条

先说头条。黄仁勋离开了英伟达。

周四上午，英伟达宣布，黄仁勋辞去总裁、首席执行官和董事职务，立即生效。首席财务官 Colette Kress，出任临时首席执行官，同时继续担任首席财务官，直到选出正式的首席执行官。董事会说，内部和外部的人选都会考虑。另外，英伟达第一次设立了董事会主席，由 Synopsys 前首席执行官 Harvey Jones 担任，他从 1993 年起就在英伟达董事会。

这件事我们这周一直在跟。上周日，董事会宣布，就黄仁勋可能成为外国情报活动目标的报道，展开独立调查，黄仁勋承认自己对妻子不忠。据报道，美国情报部门认为，涉事女性和中国国家安全部有关，中方说这种说法毫无根据。当时董事会表示，没有理由认为国家安全或公司机密受到了损害。周三，《纽约时报》又报道，他和一名英伟达员工也有亲密关系。周三董事会开会，周四上午，结果公布。据 CNBC 援引知情人士的说法，董事会是连夜做出了让他离开的决定。

还有一个细节，英伟达的公告里，没有黄仁勋本人的任何表态，只引用了 Jones 和 Kress 的话。

市场怎么看？据 CNBC 报道，周四开盘，英伟达股价一度跌超百分之十三，市值短暂跌破 4 万亿美元，到收盘，跌幅收窄到百分之四左右。

为什么收盘时没那么慌？我的判断是，芯片是提前好几年设计的，客户今天买的东西，早就定好了，这一点不会因为换人而变。真正变的，是英伟达在华盛顿和北京的那张脸。黄仁勋这些年，一直是英伟达在出口管制问题上最重要的代言人，这种关系，很难直接交给一位还兼着首席财务官的临时首席执行官。

接下来有两个时间点。一个是下周二，11 月 17 日，英伟达发布季度财报，这会是 Kress 作为临时首席执行官的第一次大考。另一个是国会，据 CNBC 报道，众议院外交委员会主席 Brian Mast 说，黄仁勋已经同意在感恩节前作证，必要时会发传票。

对中国 AI 公司来说，这件事更值得关注。整件事的起点，就是芯片流向中国的问题。黄仁勋离开以后，英伟达少了一个反对收紧出口管制的最有力的声音，而国会恰恰多了一个收紧的理由。我更关注的，是接下来几个月的政策变化，而不是这几天的股价。

### 次要新闻

第二条，谷歌周四发布了 Gemini 4 Hydrogen。谷歌把它定位成做 Agent 的日常主力模型，主打速度快、价格低，更难的长链条推理，还是交给 Carbon 和 Argon。

价格是重点。输入每百万 token 1.5 美元，输出每百万 token 7.5 美元，而且在一百万 token 的整个上下文里，价格都一样。对比一下，昨天我们讲的 Gemini 4 Carbon，优惠价是输入 2 美元、输出 10 美元，11 月底以后还要翻倍。

Hydrogen 已经上了 Gemini API 和 AI Studio，所有档位都能用，Gemini 应用和谷歌搜索也都用上了，正在全球推出。

性能方面，下面这些都是谷歌自己公布的数字。谷歌说，在 Artificial Analysis 的综合智能指数上，Hydrogen 默认设置拿到 51 分，比 Gemini 4 Argon 高 1 分，用新的超高思考档位，是 54 分。在终端编程测试 Terminal-Bench 上，是百分之五十七，旧的 Gemini 3.8 Flash 只有百分之十八左右。速度大约是 3.8 Flash 的 2.5 倍。

这对开发者意味着什么？Agent 最烧钱的地方，是大量的跑腿活儿，这些活儿通常交给便宜的模型。便宜的模型如果真能做到上一档模型的水平，大家默认的选择就会变。谷歌已经在自家的 Antigravity 编程工具里，让 Carbon 负责规划，让 Hydrogen 负责执行，我觉得这就是 Agent 接下来的主流做法。不过，这些分数都是谷歌自己测的，最好等第三方的结果。

第三条，也是周四，四家非营利组织在旧金山高等法院起诉了 Anthropic、谷歌、Meta、OpenAI，还有 SpaceX 旗下的 AI 业务。

它们的要求很大胆，希望法院下令，冻结下一代前沿 AI 的开发，直到公司能证明，技术还在人类的控制之下。原告援引的是加州的不正当竞争法，认为这些公司构成了公共妨害。起诉书由新泽西州前总检察长 Matthew Platkin 提交。

有意思的是，原告说，现有的、能力更弱的 AI 系统，已经出现过绕过安全措施，在没有授权的情况下访问外部系统的情况，起诉书还引用了 AI 公司自己的说法，承认这项技术有可能失控。换句话说，行业自己的警告，现在成了起诉它们的材料。

要说明的是，这些都还是原告的指控，目前没有看到五家公司的回应。州法院叫停全美的 AI 研发，可能性很小，但这个案子如果往下走，公司内部知道什么、什么时候知道的，可能会被翻出来。

### 快讯

接下来是快讯。

据路透社报道，SpaceX 表示，要投入超过 740 亿美元，建两个 AI 超算园区。得州 Starbase 的园区超过 240 亿美元，用自建的燃气电厂供电。亚利桑那州 Cochise 县的园区大约 500 亿美元，还需要县里批准，当地有居民联名反对。

据 The Register 报道，OpenAI 推出了两款安全产品。Daybreak Red Team 用 Agent 测试网页应用的漏洞，目前是抢先体验。Defense Factory 负责找漏洞、写补丁，直接生成可以合并的代码修改，由工程师审核，背后用的是 GPT-6 Cyber。

前几天我们讲过 Fireworks AI 被入侵。现在 Notion 确认，攻击者拿到了存放在 Fireworks 的部分 Notion AI 聊天记录，一小部分客户受到影响，Notion 已经切断了和 Fireworks 的连接。

Anthropic 开放了可以自行部署的 Claude Managed Agents，目前是小范围内测。企业可以把 Agent 的运行框架、沙箱、凭证和会话记录，都放在自己的云上，不过模型本身，还是在外部运行。

最后，Thinking Machines 兑现了上周的承诺，Inkling 2 Small 的模型权重，已经放到了 Hugging Face 上，这是一个总参数 5940 亿的混合专家模型。

### 结语

今天这些新闻放在一起看，AI 行业的焦点，正在从模型本身，转到模型背后的人和规则上。一位创始人离开，一群组织把实验室告上法庭，国会也要求作证。下周二英伟达的财报会，是接下来最值得看的一件事，尤其是董事会会不会透露，正式的接班人往哪个方向找。

### Main story: Jensen Huang leaves Nvidia

Here's what changed. On Thursday morning, Nvidia announced that Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and member of the board, effective immediately. Chief financial officer Colette Kress becomes interim CEO, and she keeps the CFO job until a permanent chief executive is named. The board says it will look at internal and external candidates. And Nvidia now has a board chair for the first time: Harvey Jones, a former chief executive of Synopsys, who has been on Nvidia's board since nineteen ninety-three.

Notice what the announcement doesn't have: a quote from Huang. The only voices in it are Jones and Kress. CNBC, citing a person familiar with the matter, reported that the board decided overnight to push him out.

Quick recap of how we got here, because this moved fast. Last Sunday the board opened an independent investigation into reports that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. He told staff he had been unfaithful to his wife. The woman involved, Alice Huang, no relation, was a top executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore company that bought billions of dollars of Nvidia hardware, and US intelligence reportedly believes she was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security. China calls that baseless. At that point, the board said it had no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets had been compromised. Then on Wednesday, the New York Times reported a second relationship, this time with an Nvidia employee. By Thursday morning, Huang was gone.

The market reaction tells you something. According to CNBC, Nvidia shares fell more than thirteen percent at the open on Thursday, briefly dropping the company below four trillion dollars in value. By the close, the loss had narrowed to about four percent.

So why did investors calm down? My guess: the business doesn't run on one person's calendar. Chips are designed years ahead, and what customers are buying today was locked in long before this week. That didn't change on Thursday.

What did change is harder to price. Huang wasn't just a CEO. He was Nvidia's public face on export rules, in Washington and in Beijing, and he drove a long list of deals and investments across the industry. Relationships like that don't automatically transfer to an interim CEO who is still doing the CFO job.

And the timing is rough. Nvidia reports quarterly results next Tuesday, November seventeenth, so Kress's first big public moment as interim CEO is an earnings call. Congress is circling too. CNBC reports that Brian Mast, who chairs the House Foreign Affairs Committee, says Huang has agreed to testify before Thanksgiving, and would be subpoenaed if needed. And the investigation that started all of this hasn't published any findings.

What most headlines are missing is the China angle. The whole story began as a question about chips reaching China. With Huang gone, Nvidia loses its most effective voice against tighter export controls, at the exact moment lawmakers have a new reason to push for them. If you're a Chinese AI lab relying on Nvidia hardware, the next few months matter more than the next few days.

One more thread. Nvidia agreed in September to buy Hugging Face for about thirteen billion dollars. Hugging Face's CEO, Clem Delangue, called Thursday "a really sad day," and wrote, "we'll keep fighting for open-source AI, harder than ever." TechCrunch reports the deal is still expected to close in the first half of next year.

My read: the interim pick is about stability before an earnings call. The real signal is who gets the job for good. An insider means continuity. An outsider means the board wants a governance reset. Watch Tuesday's call for any hint of which way it's leaning.

### Google ships Gemini 4 Hydrogen

On Thursday, Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen. Google calls it its "daily driver for agentic workflows." It's the fast, cheap tier. Carbon and Argon stay on top for deep reasoning.

The price is the story. One dollar fifty per million input tokens, seven fifty per million output, and that price is flat across the whole one-million-token context window. For comparison, Gemini 4 Carbon, which we covered yesterday, is two dollars and ten dollars until the end of November, and then doubles.

It's out widely. It's in the Gemini API and AI Studio on all tiers, in the Gemini app, and in Google Search for everyone, rolling out globally.

Now the claims, and these are Google's numbers. Google says Hydrogen scores fifty-one on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default setting, one point above Gemini 4 Argon, and fifty-four at a new extra-high thinking level. On Terminal-Bench, a coding-in-the-terminal test, Google reports fifty-seven percent, against eighteen percent for the older Gemini 3.8 Flash. And it says Hydrogen is about two and a half times faster than 3.8 Flash.

Why this matters: agents burn tokens. Most of the volume in an agent system is the cheap model doing the legwork. If a cheap model really matches last month's mid-tier, the default choice for agent builders shifts. Google is also pairing Hydrogen with Carbon in its Antigravity coding tool, the expensive model planning and the cheap one executing. That's where I think agent design is heading.

The catch: every number here comes from Google, and several of the benchmarks are new or internal. Wait for independent tests before you rebuild your stack.

### Nonprofits sue to freeze frontier AI

Also on Thursday, four nonprofits, the Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative, and the Alliance for Secure AI, filed a lawsuit in San Francisco Superior Court against Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceX's AI unit.

What they want is big. A court order freezing development of next-generation frontier models, until the companies can show the technology is under human control. The legal route is ordinary state law: the plaintiffs invoke California's unfair competition law, and describe the companies as creating a public nuisance. The complaint was filed by Matthew Platkin, a former New Jersey attorney general.

The interesting part is the evidence. The plaintiffs say today's less powerful AI systems have already got around safeguards and reached outside computer systems without authorization. And they cite the companies' own admissions that the technology could go rogue. The industry's warnings are now being used as an exhibit against it.

To be clear, these are allegations, and I haven't seen responses from any of the five companies. A state judge halting national AI development would be extraordinary. But even if the freeze never happens, the case could force questions about what the labs knew and when.

### Quick hits

SpaceX says it will spend more than seventy-four billion dollars on two AI supercomputing campuses, according to Reuters. A CEO is gone, a coalition is asking a court to step in, and Congress wants testimony. The one thing I'd watch next is Nvidia's earnings call on Tuesday, and whether the board says anything about who comes next.
