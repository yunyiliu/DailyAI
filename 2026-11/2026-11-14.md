### 开场

这里是 Daily AI Signal，今天是 11 月 14 日，星期六。

### 头条

先说头条。AI Agent，能替你花钱了。

周五，OpenAI 推出了 ChatGPT Wallet，也就是 ChatGPT 钱包。有了它，OpenAI 的个人 Agent，OpenAI 叫它 dots，可以直接替用户下单买东西。

先看谁能用。目前只开放给美国的 ChatGPT Pro 订阅用户，要年满 18 岁，还要有一张美国发行的 Visa、万事达或者美国运通卡。

再看怎么用。卡只需要绑定一次。每次下单之前，用户会看到买的是什么、总价多少、用哪张卡，然后要通过面容或者指纹验证，才能付款。

我觉得最值得说的，是背后的支付设计。每一笔交易，卡组织都会生成一个一次性的支付令牌，相当于一个替身卡号，有自己的安全码。所以 Agent 和商家，都看不到你真实的卡号。信用卡原来的积分和争议保障，也都还在。据 CNBC 报道，OpenAI 在上线阶段，不从交易里抽成。

这已经是 OpenAI 第二次做 AI 购物了。去年秋天，它推出过一个叫 Instant Checkout 的功能，后来放弃了。这次换了思路，直接和卡组织合作，还给商家出了一份指南，教商家怎么让 Agent 进来。

问题也在这里。OpenAI 自己也承认，很多网站和支付系统，还会把 AI Agent 当成机器人拦下来，或者直接拒绝它的付款。

就在两天前，周三，美联航改了它的运输条款。新条款明确禁止用 AI Agent 购票、改签、处理机票，或者购买任何服务和产品。Agent 订的票，美联航可以直接取消。而且它对 AI Agent 的定义很宽，AI 助手、各种 Agent、带 AI 功能的浏览器，都算在内，不管有没有人在旁边监督或者确认。

注意最后这一点。OpenAI 刚推出的这种模式，每一笔都要人来确认，在美联航那里，照样不行。美联航给出的理由是，Agent 可能导致用户买了不想买的东西、账户被改动，或者里程被花掉。另外据报道，美联航首席执行官 Scott Kirby 说，他不想再多一个中间商。

美联航不是唯一一家。CNBC 提到，亚马逊也不让 Agent 在它的平台上买东西。上个月我们讲过，沃尔玛走的是另一条路，开了一个入口，让经过批准的 Agent 进来购物。

这件事为什么重要？过去大家关心的是，Agent 能不能付钱。现在这个问题基本解决了，美国最大的三家卡组织，都已经在给 Agent 发专用的支付令牌。新的问题是，谁愿意让 Agent 进门。

我的判断是，这件事表面上是安全问题，实际上是在争客户。航空公司花了很多年，和在线旅行平台抢客户关系。一个替你订机票的 AI Agent，就是一个新的中间商，Kirby 那句话，其实把这层意思说出来了。我觉得最后更可能变成沃尔玛那种做法，商家愿意让 Agent 进来，但要按自己的规矩，走自己控制的入口，很可能还要谈好分成。

当然，它的局限也很明显。只在美国，只给 Pro 用户，每一笔都要刷脸。说白了，现在更像是 Agent 帮你把购物车装好，最后还是你来付钱。OpenAI 说，以后希望用户能在设定的额度、品类或者时间范围内，把购买完全交给 Agent，但没有给时间表。

接下来值得看的，是有没有大航空公司或者大零售商，公开接入 ChatGPT 钱包。还有，美联航到底要怎么判断，一笔订单是不是 Agent 下的。

### 次要新闻

第二条，DeepSeek 把整个 App 的能力，都开放成了 API。

周五，DeepSeek 宣布，开发者在 API 里改几个参数，就能用上 App 里的那一套功能，包括联网搜索、边推理边读网页的深度思考搜索、上传 PDF、Office 文档和图片、读取链接，还有安全审核。这些功能跑在 V4.1-Flash 模型上，三种主流的 API 接口格式都支持。另外，搜索、网页抓取、文字识别、语音识别和语音合成，也都可以单独调用。

价格是重点。DeepSeek 官网标的联网搜索，是每一千次 0.75 美元。做个对比，Anthropic 官方文档里，它的联网搜索工具，是每一千次 10 美元。语音识别和语音合成，目前还在限时半价。当然，各家计费方式不一样，不能完全直接比，但差距确实很大。

还有一个细节。DeepSeek 要求，面向公众的应用，必须打开安全审核。在国内，内容审核和 AI 生成内容的标识，都是应用方自己的责任，现在这部分直接打包进来，对小团队来说，省了不少事。

我的判断是，过去两年，大家比的是模型本身的价格。现在开始比的，是模型周边那一圈能力的价格，搜索、文件、语音、合规。DeepSeek 等于把做一个类似 DeepSeek 的 App，门槛和成本都压低了。不过，目前官方只提到 Flash 模型支持这些功能，搜索和语音的效果怎么样，也还要看实际用下来。

第三条，和 AI 写应用的安全有关。

安全公司 Claroc 检查了用 Grok Build 做出来的应用，Grok Build 是 SpaceXSI，也就是原来的 xAI，推出的 AI 应用生成工具。Claroc 说，它找到的大约三万七千多个在线应用里，有 35578 个，不用登录，任何人都能打开。

大部分应用没什么敏感内容。但 Claroc 估计，大约有 650 到 800 个应用里存着私密数据，包括病人的化验结果、员工名单，还有一个应用，把密码以明文写在了代码里。其中 24 个，是 SpaceXSI 和 X 自己员工做的，里面有销售区域地图和广告提案。

这些应用是怎么被找到的？每个应用都有一个自己的网址，这些网址会被被动 DNS 数据收录，可以按规律搜出来。Claroc 还说，它看到的发布界面，默认选的是，有链接的人都能看。SpaceXSI 对媒体表示，应用在创建者主动分享之前，都是私密的，它也下架了自家员工的应用。但 Claroc 说，其他用户的应用，当时还是公开的。

我更关注的是背后的问题，不只是 Grok。一句话就能生成一个应用的时候，默认的分享设置，就成了真正的安全决定。如果你的公司允许员工用这类工具，最好有人去查一查，已经发布出去的都有什么。

### 快讯

接下来是快讯。

这周我们讲过 Fireworks AI 被入侵的事。据 TechCrunch 报道，Uber 正在通知数千名司机，他们的个人信息和驾照信息，在 Uber 一家技术供应商被入侵时被盗，TechCrunch 说，这家供应商就是 Fireworks。目前几个州的备案加起来，已经超过 7000 人，Uber 还没有公布总数。

再跟进一下英伟达。据 CNBC 报道，黄仁勋离开后的第一个完整交易日，周五英伟达股价跌了大约百分之二，收盘市值跌破 4 万亿美元，这是 2025 年 7 月以来第一次。这一周累计跌了接近百分之十三，已经连续六周下跌。下周二的财报会，是接下来的关键。

### 结语

今天这些新闻放在一起看，AI 行业的竞争，正在从模型本身，转到模型周边。谁能付钱，谁能搜索，谁能看到 Agent 做出来的东西。OpenAI 用卡组织解决了付钱的问题，DeepSeek 把搜索和文件处理做得很便宜，接下来难的，是谁愿意放行。我最想看的，是第一家公开接受 Agent 下单的大商家会是谁，这会告诉我们，美联航这道门，是常态，还是例外。

### Main story: agents get a wallet, and a locked door

Here's what changed. On Friday, OpenAI announced ChatGPT Wallet. It lets OpenAI's personal agents, which it calls dots, buy things on your behalf.

The launch is narrow. It's for ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US who are eighteen or older, with a US-issued Visa, Mastercard or American Express card. You add a card once. Before every purchase, you see the items, the total and the card, and you approve it with a security check like Face ID or a fingerprint.

The interesting part is the plumbing. The card networks issue a payment token for each purchase, a stand-in card number with its own security code. So neither the agent nor the store ever sees your real card details. You keep your card rewards and your dispute protections. And according to CNBC, OpenAI isn't taking a cut of transactions at launch.

This is OpenAI's second try. Its first shopping feature, Instant Checkout, launched last fall and was later dropped. This time OpenAI built on the card networks, and it published a playbook for merchants on how to let agents in.

And that's the catch. OpenAI itself says many websites and payment systems still block AI agents as bots, or reject their purchases. Which brings us to United Airlines.

On Wednesday, United revised its contract of carriage. A new rule bans, quote, "the use of AI Agents to make, modify or service a Ticket, and/or to purchase any service or product." Another lets United cancel reservations made by agents. And the definition is broad. It covers AI assistants, agents and AI-enabled browsers, whether or not a person supervises or approves what the agent does.

Read that last part again. Even the exact setup OpenAI just launched, where a human approves every purchase, is banned on United. The airline says agents can cause unintended purchases, account changes or use of frequent-flyer miles. And CEO Scott Kirby reportedly said he doesn't want, quote, "another middleman."

United isn't alone. CNBC notes Amazon blocks agents from buying on its platform. And last month we covered Walmart going the other way, with a gateway that lets approved agents shop.

So why does this matter? For years the question was whether an agent could pay at all. That's now mostly solved. The three biggest card networks are issuing agent-specific tokens, and American Express is part of the launch. The open question is who lets agents in.

My read: this is less about security than about who owns the customer. Airlines spent decades fighting online travel agencies for that relationship. An AI agent that books your flight is a new intermediary, and Kirby's "middleman" line says it out loud. Expect what Walmart did, not what United did, to become the template. Merchants will let agents in, but on their own terms, through gateways they control and probably with deals attached.

The limitation is obvious. This is Pro-only and US-only, and every purchase still needs your face. It's closer to "the agent fills the cart and you pay" than true delegation. OpenAI says it expects users to eventually hand purchases over to agents within set spending limits, categories or time windows. There's no date for that.

What to watch: whether any big airline or retailer signs up as a named Wallet partner, and how United actually enforces a rule against software that can look like any other customer. As Amex's Luke Gebb told CNBC, it makes sense to "start it in a contained environment." Right now, the container is very small.

### DeepSeek turns its app into an API

Second story. On Friday, DeepSeek put the whole DeepSeek app behind its API.

Developers can now switch on web search, deep-thinking search that reads pages while it reasons, file input for PDFs, Office documents and images, link reading, and safety checks, with a few parameters. It runs on DeepSeek's V4.1-Flash model, and it works across the Messages, Responses and Chat Completions styles of API. There are also standalone endpoints for search, page fetching, OCR, speech recognition and text-to-speech.

The price is the story. DeepSeek lists web search at seventy-five cents per thousand searches. For comparison, Anthropic lists its web search tool at ten dollars per thousand. Speech recognition and text-to-speech are half price for a limited time. These aren't perfect like-for-like comparisons, since the providers bill differently, but the gap is large.

One more detail: DeepSeek says public-facing apps must turn the safety checks on. In China, apps are responsible for content moderation and labeling AI output, so bundling that in removes real work for small teams.

My take: model prices have been falling for two years. The new front is the price of everything around the model, meaning search, files, voice and compliance. DeepSeek just made it cheap to build an app that works like DeepSeek's own. The limit for now: DeepSeek names only its Flash model for the built-in tools, and the search and voice quality still has to prove itself.

### Thirty-five thousand open apps

Third, a security story about vibe coding. Security firm Claroc looked at apps built with Grok Build, SpaceXSI's app builder. Of about thirty-seven and a half thousand apps it found online, Claroc says thirty-five thousand five hundred and seventy-eight opened for anyone, with no sign-in.

Most are harmless. But Claroc estimates roughly six hundred fifty to eight hundred hold private data, including patient lab results, staff rosters, and in one case, passwords in plain text. Twenty-four were built by SpaceXSI and X staff themselves, including a sales territory map and advertising proposals.

How did they get found? Each app gets its own web address, and those addresses get picked up in passive DNS data, which can be searched by pattern. Claroc also says the publish screen it saw preselected "anyone with the link." SpaceXSI told a reporter that apps stay private until their creator shares them, and it took down its own staff's apps. Claroc says other users' apps were still open.

The lesson isn't really about Grok. When building an app takes one prompt, the default sharing setting becomes the real security decision. If your company lets people use AI app builders, someone should check what's been published.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that the AI fight is moving to the edges of the model. Who gets to pay, who gets to search, who can see what an agent built. OpenAI solved payment with the card networks. DeepSeek made search and files nearly free. The hard part now is permission. The one thing I'd watch next is whether the first big merchant signs up to accept agent purchases, because that tells you whether United's locked door is the rule or the exception.
