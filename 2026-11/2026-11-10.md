### 头条

先说头条。Anthropic 发布了新模型，它说在测过的几乎所有能力上，都比 Opus 5.5 高一档。而且一次发了两个名字，Claude Fable 5.2 和 Claude Mythos 5.2。

Anthropic 说，这两个其实是同一个模型，区别只在安全限制。Fable 5.2 对所有人开放，可以通过 Anthropic 的接口使用，也上了亚马逊、谷歌和微软的云平台。价格是每百万输入 token 8 美元，每百万输出 token 40 美元。还有一个快速模式，Anthropic 说生成速度最多快 2.5 倍，价格翻倍。

Fable 带着一套分类器，会拦下高风险的网络安全任务，比如写漏洞利用代码，也会拦下大部分生物研究类的请求。被拦下的问题，会交给一个更老的模型来回答。

Mythos 5.2 是同一个模型，放宽了这些限制，主要给通过审核的机构用。Anthropic 设了两个审核项目，一个面向网络安全防御方，一个面向生命科学机构，都要申请。

再看成绩，下面的数字都来自 Anthropic 自己的测试。在一个叫 FrontierSWE 第二版的软件工程基准上，Fable 5.2 大约百分之七十，高于 OpenAI 的 GPT-6 Astra 和谷歌的 Gemini 4 Carbon。不过并不是全面领先，在另一项自动化测试上，Anthropic 自己的表里，Gemini 4 Carbon 分数更高。Anthropic 甚至建议，大多数日常编程和知识工作，先用更便宜的 Opus 5.5。

Anthropic 还展示了几个实验。一名工程师用一千多个子智能体，花了八天，从一个空文件夹开始，做出了一个能在笔记本电脑上启动的原型操作系统，有窗口，有文件存储，还有登录界面。另一个由一千个智能体组成的团队，大约 30 个小时，完成了费马大定理证明的形式化。这些都是厂商自己报告的单次实验。

有一点很少见。Anthropic 说，它在训练时刻意压住了两项能力，一个是给 AI 芯片优化底层代码，一个是改进模型架构，让它们大致停在 Opus 5.5 的水平。简单说，就是帮着造下一代 AI 的能力，故意没有往上提。Anthropic 也承认一个问题，这两个模型更常怀疑自己正在被测试，这让安全评估更难解读。

我的判断是，比成绩更值得关注的，是这种发布方式。上个月底，谷歌把 Gemini 4 Carbon 只开放给一批网络安全防御方。现在 Anthropic 把同一个模型，分成两种安全等级，完整版放在审核后面。最前沿的模型，正在变成分级产品，你是谁，决定了模型愿意帮你做什么。

当然，这些数字几乎都来自 Anthropic 自己。接下来要看独立评测，还有到底有多少机构，能通过这些审核。

### 次要新闻

第二条，一起越查越大的数据泄露。AI 推理和训练平台 Fireworks，周一更新了安全事件通报。

按照 Fireworks 的说法，10 月 23 日，一家替它托管研发机器的第三方云服务商被攻破。第二天，攻击者借此进入了 Fireworks 的开发机器，运行了一个任务，当天被发现。

泄露的范围，比 10 月初第一次通报时更大。现在包括部分账户存储的密钥，账单信息，比如地址、发票记录和额度使用情况。对一部分客户来说，还包括数据集，和没有训练完的模型权重。Fireworks 说，推理服务没有受到影响，它也不保存银行卡号。受影响的客户有多少，没有公布。

Fireworks 说，攻击和一个以牟利为目的的团伙有关。这个团伙盗用大模型的访问权限，再把 token 转卖出去，这种做法叫 LLMjacking，也就是劫持大模型。我的判断是，被偷的 AI 算力，已经成了可以倒卖的商品。如果你的训练数据放在别人的平台上，除了问平台本身安不安全，还要问它的机器托管在哪里。

第三条，是一条后续。我们从 10 月底开始，一直在跟 Anthropic 的上市进展。

据路透社报道，它看到的邮件显示，周一晚些时候，Anthropic 给投行发了一份文件，定价区间是每股 70 到 80 美元，大约 8.67 亿股，按上限算，募资大约 693 亿美元。但很快，第二封邮件说，定价信息不准确，文件会再更新。哪里不准确，没有说明。Anthropic 拒绝置评。

同一份文件还显示，谷歌母公司 Alphabet、英伟达、AMD、博通、美光、微软和台积电，会以上市价格，私募认购总共 260 亿美元的股票。路透社说，路演预计周二开始，下周定价。

需要说清楚，70 到 80 美元这个区间，已经被撤回了，不能当成正式价格。更值得看的是那 260 亿美元。认购的大多是 Anthropic 买芯片、买云服务的供应商。它们以上市价格成为股东，是一种支持，也是一种资金的循环。

第四条，Meta 发布了 Muse 系列里最小的模型，Muse Ember。价格是每百万输入 token 0.1 美元，每百万输出 token 0.5 美元，这是提示在 20 万 token 以内时的价格，上下文窗口 100 万 token。

Meta 把它定位成智能体里的干活模型，负责调用工具、分发任务、提取信息这些重复性的工作。按 Meta 公布的成绩，它在 Artificial Analysis 的综合指数上和 Anthropic 的 Haiku 5.5 打平，有的测试领先，编程测试落后。现在的智能体，往往是一个贵模型做规划，一堆便宜模型干活，最便宜的这一档，争的就是这部分调用量。

### 快讯

接下来是几条快讯。谷歌给第八代 TPU 新增了两款系统。TPU 8tx 搭配 AMD 的服务器处理器，加上硬件级的机密计算，面向受监管的企业和政府。TPU 8i Max 把两颗 8i 芯片封装在一起，谷歌说算力和内存带宽都翻倍，适合超长上下文。谷歌的公告里没有给出上市时间。

昨天我们讲到，美国参议院要对一项审批改革法案进行程序性投票，法案要求数据中心承担自己接入电网的成本。周一的投票结果是 73 比 25，通过了这一关。接下来还要全院表决，再由众议院同意。

同样是昨天头条的后续。英伟达董事会宣布调查黄仁勋之后，周一第一个交易日，英伟达股价收跌大约百分之四点三，同一天 AMD 涨了大约百分之十三。美国众议院外交事务委员会的三名民主党议员，给商务部长卢特尼克写信，要求立即停止向中国出口先进 AI 芯片，并追问商务部对相关报道知道多少，什么时候知道的。英伟达 11 月 17 日发布财报。

最后是机器人。英伟达在机器人学习大会上，发布了开放的机器人模型 GR00T 2，训练数据里包括 10 万小时的人类第一人称数据。英伟达说，在 50 个任务上，每个任务只给一次示范，平均成功率百分之七十一，给五次示范，提高到百分之九十五。

### 结语

把今天的新闻放在一起看，访问权限，正在变成 AI 的核心问题。Anthropic 把最强的能力放在审核后面，Fireworks 的事件说明，有人在偷不该拿到的访问权，英伟达这一周，面对的是芯片到底该卖给谁。

接下来最值得关注的，是 Anthropic 的审核项目，到底能放进来多少机构。这决定了最前沿的 AI，是继续开放，还是慢慢变成会员制。以上就是今天的 Daily AI Signal。

### Hook

### Today's top stories

Today: Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, and what a two-tier release tells us. Then Fireworks AI says its breach reached customer billing data, datasets and unfinished model weights. And Anthropic's IPO pricing went out to banks, and then got pulled back.

This is Daily AI Signal for Tuesday, November tenth.

### Main story: one model, two safety tiers

Here's what changed. On Monday, Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2. Anthropic says they are the same underlying model. The difference is the safeguards.

Fable 5.2 is open to everyone. It's on Anthropic's API and on AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure, priced at eight dollars per million input tokens and forty dollars per million output tokens. There's an optional fast mode that Anthropic says generates up to two and a half times faster, at double the price. Fable comes with classifiers that block high-risk cybersecurity work, things like exploit generation and penetration testing, and most research-biology requests. When a request is blocked, Anthropic says it gets answered by an older model instead.

Mythos 5.2 is the same model with those safeguards relaxed. The main way to get it is for your organization to pass one of Anthropic's verification programs, one for cyber defenders and one for life sciences. Enterprise customers can also reach it through a security product called Claude Security.

Now the numbers, and every one of these is Anthropic's own. On a software engineering benchmark called FrontierSWE version two, Anthropic reports Fable 5.2 at about seventy percent, ahead of OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra at sixty-five and a half, and Google's Gemini 4 Carbon at about sixty-one. Anthropic also says outputs are up to thirty percent faster than the previous version.

But the table isn't a clean sweep. On AutomationBench, Anthropic's own chart has Gemini 4 Carbon ahead. And on another of its charts, Anthropic's cheaper Opus 5.5 scores slightly higher. In fact, Anthropic tells most customers to start with Opus 5.5, and only move up to Fable for the hardest technical work.

The demos say a lot about where Anthropic thinks the value is. Anthropic says one engineer used more than a thousand sub-agents over eight days to build a prototype operating system from an empty folder. It boots on a laptop, with windows, file storage and a login screen. A separate thousand-agent team formalized the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in about thirty hours, where Anthropic's first attempt took eleven days. Anthropic also claims the model helped solve two open problems in black-hole physics, written up in preprints. These are single runs, reported by the vendor.

The interesting part is what Anthropic says it held back. It says it adjusted training to keep two abilities, optimizing low-level code for AI chips and improving model architectures, at roughly Opus 5.5 level. In other words, a lab saying it deliberately kept its top model no better than its cheaper one at the skills that help build the next AI. Anthropic also flags a limitation: both models more often say they suspect they're being tested, which makes safety evaluations harder to read. Outside testers, including the UK AI Security Institute and METR, tested the model, but Anthropic notes they didn't review its blog post or system card.

So why does this matter? My read is that the bigger story is the access model. Late last month, Google released Gemini 4 Carbon only to a group of cyber defenders. Now Anthropic ships one model at two safety levels, and the full version sits behind a verification check. The frontier is turning into a tiered product, where who you are decides what the model will do for you.

For developers, the practical takeaways are simple. If you're running long coding agents, this is a new top option at eight and forty. If you do security or biology work, expect some requests to be blocked and handed to an older model, and know that the full version means applying to a program. And for most everyday work, Anthropic itself says the cheaper model is the place to start.

The catch is that almost every number here comes from Anthropic. What I'd watch next is independent testing, and how many organizations actually get through those verification programs.

### Fireworks: the breach got bigger

Next, a security story every team that fine-tunes on someone else's platform should read.

Fireworks AI, an inference and training platform, updated its incident report on Monday. Here's the timeline it gives. On October twenty-third, a third-party cloud provider that hosts Fireworks' research machines was breached. The next day, an attacker used that access to get onto Fireworks' development machines and run a workload. Fireworks says it caught it the same day.

What was exposed is wider than the first report in early October. Fireworks now says it includes stored secrets for a limited set of accounts, account and billing details like addresses, invoice history and credit usage, and, for certain customers, datasets and incomplete model weights. Fireworks says its inference service and its Training SDK were not affected, and it doesn't store card numbers. It hasn't said how many customers were hit. It has switched off self-serve dataset uploads and downloads while Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 investigates, and it has notified law enforcement.

The interesting part is the motive. Fireworks ties this to a financially motivated group that steals access to language models and resells the tokens through illicit routing services. That's called LLM-jacking. My take: stolen AI compute is now a commodity criminals trade, the way stolen cloud credits were a decade ago. And this attack came in through a supplier, not the front door. If your training data or unfinished weights live on a vendor's platform, ask who hosts that vendor's machines.

### Anthropic's IPO pricing: sent, then pulled

Our third story is a follow-up. We've been tracking Anthropic's IPO filing since late October.

Reuters reports it saw emails showing that late Monday, Anthropic sent banks a filing with a price range of seventy to eighty dollars a share, for about eight hundred sixty-seven million shares. At the top of that range, the offering would raise sixty-nine point three billion dollars. Then a follow-up email said the pricing details were inaccurate and that the filing would be updated. It didn't say what was wrong. Anthropic declined to comment, and Reuters couldn't determine what was incorrect.

The same draft showed a twenty-six billion dollar private placement from Alphabet, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC, buying shares at the IPO price. Reuters says the roadshow was expected to start Tuesday, with pricing the week after. Earlier Reuters reporting said valuation talk had come down from as high as two trillion dollars, with one and a half trillion now seen as the high end.

Two things stand out. First, don't treat seventy to eighty as the range; it was withdrawn. Second, the private placement. Those are largely the companies Anthropic pays for chips and cloud. They'd become shareholders at the IPO price. That's a vote of confidence, and it's also money moving in a circle. Public investors will want to see both sides of that.

### Meta's cheap model for agent grunt work

Meta released Muse Ember on Monday, the smallest model in its Muse family. It costs ten cents per million input tokens and fifty cents per million output tokens for prompts up to two hundred thousand tokens, with a one-million-token context window. It's on Meta's Model API, and Meta says the Muse models are now on Google Cloud and Oracle Cloud too.

Meta pitches it for the repetitive parts of agent work: tool calls, routing, extraction, coding subtasks. On Artificial Analysis' index, Meta reports it ties Anthropic's Haiku 5.5 at thirty-six. It wins on some of Meta's charts and loses to Haiku on a coding benchmark. The bigger trend is that agents make lots of cheap calls under one expensive planner. The fight at the bottom of the price list is about who handles that volume.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that access is becoming the product. Anthropic gates its strongest skills behind verification. Fireworks shows what happens when someone takes access they shouldn't have. And Nvidia's week is about who gets its chips at all.

The one thing I'd watch next is how many organizations actually make it into those verification programs. That number tells you whether the frontier stays open or quietly becomes members-only. That's Daily AI Signal for today.
