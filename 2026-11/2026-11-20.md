### 开场

这里是 Daily AI Signal，今天是 11 月 20 日，星期五。

### 头条

先说头条。能替你操作电脑的 AI Agent，这周碰上了掌管电脑的那家公司。

周四，谷歌开始在 Mac 版 Gemini 应用里，推出“电脑操作”功能。Gemini 会替你点击、打字、填表格，范围包括本地文件、桌面应用和网页。它在一个单独的后台窗口里运行，你可以随时看、随时暂停，关键操作前它会先问你。目前只在美国、只支持英文，面向付费的 Google AI Plus、Pro 和 Ultra 用户。

值得细看的，是谷歌自己的帮助页面。上面写着，一个应用屏幕上的不可信内容，可能操纵 Gemini，跨到你允许的其他应用里去执行操作。页面还提醒，屏幕上显示的双重验证码，Gemini 也能读到。简单说，能看你屏幕的 Agent，也会被屏幕上的内容带偏。

同一天，彭博社的马克·古尔曼报道，苹果正在招人，做自己的 Agent。苹果一则新的招聘启事写道，想象一个 AI Agent，能像一个能干的人那样使用电脑。它能浏览网页，在不同应用之间切换，运行命令，还能写代码、改代码。彭博社说，苹果最早可能在明年年底推出，但项目还在早期。苹果没有回应。

问题在于，彭博社还报道，macOS 27.3 的测试版，加了新的警告页面，提醒用户慎重考虑，要不要让第三方 Agent 控制自己的 Mac。据彭博社，在 ChatGPT 里打开第三方电脑操作工具，现在要 14 步，原来是 4 步。彭博社说，一些 AI 公司认为，这是在给竞争对手设门槛。

苹果为什么收紧？可以看看 Meta。周三，路透社根据 Meta 内部二十多条员工帖子报道，Meta 的 Agent 应用 Muse，下载量超过 1000 万，但员工遇到了不少怪事。有人在问钓鱼天气的对话里，突然收到了看上去是别人的银行余额。还有 Muse 没经同意，就订了一个不能退款的 Airbnb。路透社说，无法确定 Muse 显示的是别人的真实数据，还是编出来的。Meta 发言人说，这些是个别情况，很多是模型幻觉，已经修好了。苹果对路透社表示，希望用户清楚知道自己授权了什么。

这件事真正重要的地方在于，桌面 Agent 要靠操作系统给的权限，比如辅助功能、屏幕录制、控制其他应用。在 Mac 上，这些权限归苹果管。现在，苹果一边重新设计这些权限，一边自己做 Agent。这里面既有安全的考虑，也牵涉竞争。

我的判断是，Agent 的竞争，正在从谁的模型更聪明，转向谁能拿到动手的权限。如果你是开发者，要准备好 Mac 用户授权变麻烦，只申请最少的权限。如果你是普通用户，只把 Agent 放进那些，就算它被一个坏网页带偏，你也能接受后果的应用。

### 次要新闻

再看 Anthropic。昨天我们报道了，Anthropic 的上市定价是每股 83 美元。周四，它在纳斯达克正式开始交易，股票代码 ANT。

据 CNBC，开盘价 111.5 美元，比发行价高了大约百分之三十四。但没守住，收盘 97.17 美元，仍然上涨百分之十七。全天成交超过 8.46 亿股，是当天纳斯达克成交最多的股票。按 CNBC 的计算，收盘时完全稀释后的市值，大约 1.8 万亿美元。场外也有反对声音，CNBC 报道，超过一百人在 Anthropic 旧金山总部外抗议。

我的判断是，开盘大涨，说明需求很强，收盘回落，说明买家也有底线。往后要看的，是收盘价，不是开盘价。真正的考验，是它作为上市公司的第一份季报。

接下来，是欧盟委员会遭到的网络攻击。

周四，欧盟委员会确认，已经针对一次被发现的网络攻击，采取了必要措施。据 The Record 报道，委员会把它称为 AI 生成的网络攻击。

目前能确认的是，最早的迹象在 10 月 22 日被发现。据 The Register 和 The Record，人事系统停了十多天，包括员工查工资条、报工时、请假的平台，还有医疗报销系统。工资照常发放。委员会发言人说，没有证据显示，大范围的员工数据被泄露，调查还在进行。

最早报道此事的 Politico，引述消息人士说，防守方发现，攻击者在用 ARTEX。欧盟的网络安全机构 CERT-EU，把它描述为一个开源的、自动化的渗透测试平台，接的是外部的大模型。作者在十月撤下了代码仓库，但副本还在流传。上周，美国联邦调查局警告，有的入侵借助 ARTEX，从开始瞄准到拿到高级权限，不到三个小时。

需要说明的是，目前没有人说，ARTEX 就是攻击者进入系统的手段，报道也没有说明，它具体起了什么作用。但安全研究工具和攻击工具之间的距离，已经越来越近了。

再看中美。这周在深圳举行的 APEC 会议期间，AI 是特朗普和习近平之间的话题之一，双方释放的信号不太一样。

据法新社，特朗普说，美国反对任何形式的“全球主义”监管，也反对任何控制和压制他所说的“超级智能”的做法，美国不会允许。据新华社，习近平表示，两国应加强沟通协调，确保人工智能始终处于人类控制之下。周四，特朗普在接受福克斯新闻采访时说，他和习近平都希望，出于安全目的制定 AI 规则，会一起制定合理的规则。这是路透社的转述。

这两种说法，并不完全矛盾，不要全球性的监管，但可能有双边的安全对话。今年九月，双方曾同意建立 AI 紧急事件沟通渠道。目前还没有看到细节落地的报道。在有书面文本之前，这只是信号，还不是协议。

然后是阿里巴巴。周四，阿里公布了截至九月的季度财报，AI 业务分成了两面。

一面是赚钱的云。阿里现在叫“AI 云与算力服务”的业务，收入增长百分之五十三，达到大约 610 亿元，其中外部客户收入增长百分之六十四。AI 相关产品收入，已经连续第十三个季度三位数增长。

另一面是花钱的千问。包括千问模型和千问 App 的业务，收入增长百分之五十一，但经调整亏损扩大到大约 105 亿元，一年前是大约 34 亿元。阿里说，原因是加大 AI 投入，还有千问 App 的推理成本上升。

我的判断是，这和美国的情况一样，把算力卖给企业，现在就能赚钱，给普通用户做 AI 应用，还是一笔没有回本的投入。

### 快讯

最后两条快讯。

第一条，OpenAI 说，自己的模型帮忙设计了芯片。据媒体对 OpenAI 这周一篇文章的报道，OpenAI 的模型在 AMD 已经反复优化过的旗舰 GPU 设计上，找到了最多百分之十四的功耗、性能和面积改进，用时不到五个月。要注意，这是功耗、性能和面积的综合设计指标，不等于 GPU 快了百分之十四，而且是公司自己的说法。

第二条，国际能源署周四发布了《世界能源展望》。据 CNBC，国际能源署预计，在它的既定政策情景下，到 2030 年，数据中心的用电量可能翻一番，达到每年大约 1 万亿千瓦时。

### 结语

今天这些新闻背后，有一个更大的趋势，AI Agent 正在走出聊天框。它们开始操作电脑，替人订房，落到坏人手里，还会去试探政府的网络。模型的能力，大体已经够用了。现在争的是权限，谁来给，谁能收回，Agent 用错了权限，又由谁负责。我接下来最关注的，是苹果在 macOS 27.3 正式版里，到底定下什么规则，因为那会决定，所有桌面 Agent 要在什么样的框架里工作。

### Hook

The AI agents that click and type on your computer for you just ran into the companies that own the computer. And this week showed why that fight is about safety as much as business.

### Today's top stories

Today: Google lets Gemini take over your Mac, while Apple reportedly builds its own agent and adds new warnings for everyone else's. Anthropic's first day of trading. And the European Commission confirms a cyber campaign that investigators reportedly tie to an open-source AI hacking tool.

This is Daily AI Signal for Friday, November twentieth.

### Main story: the fight over who drives your desktop

Here's what happened. On Thursday, Google started rolling out what it calls computer use in the Gemini app for Mac. You add "at Computer use" to a prompt, and Gemini does the clicking, typing, menus and forms across your local files, desktop apps and the web. It works in a separate background window with a live preview, so your own screen and cursor stay free. Google says you choose which apps it can touch, you can watch every step and pause it, and it asks before key actions. It's rolling out in English in the US, for paying Google AI Plus, Pro and Ultra subscribers, on Apple Silicon Macs.

The interesting part is Google's own help page. It says, quote, "Untrusted content on screen in one app can manipulate Gemini into jumping across application boundaries to take actions in other allowed Mac desktop apps." It also says Gemini can read multi-factor codes that are visible on screen, and that some actions can't be undone. That's an unusually frank warning, and it's correct. An agent that reads your screen can be steered by whatever is on your screen.

The same day, Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reported that Apple is hiring to build its own agent. One new Apple job listing reads: "Imagine an AI agent that can use a computer the way a capable person does." It goes on: the agent browses the web, works across apps, runs commands, and writes and debugs code. Bloomberg says Apple has eyed launching as soon as the end of next year, but the work is at an early stage. Apple didn't comment.

And here's the catch. Bloomberg also reports that the beta of macOS twenty-seven point three adds warning screens that push users to reconsider giving outside agents control of their Mac. According to Bloomberg, turning on third-party computer-use tools for ChatGPT now takes fourteen steps, up from four. Bloomberg says some AI companies see that as slowing down rivals.

Why would Apple tighten the rules? Look at Meta. On Wednesday, Reuters reported on more than two dozen internal Meta posts about Muse, Meta's agent app, which has passed ten million downloads since it launched in September. Employees described Muse dropping what looked like someone else's bank balances into an unrelated chat, reminding one person to answer a Signal message, though she said she never gave it access to other apps, and booking a non-refundable Airbnb without permission. Reuters says it could not establish whether Muse was showing real data from other users or making it up. Meta's spokesperson called them isolated incidents, said many were hallucinations that had already been fixed, and said employees test versions of Muse the public doesn't get. Apple told Reuters it wants users to understand exactly what they're allowing, and Reuters says an Apple update effectively closed the pathway it had identified.

So what changes? Desktop agents depend on operating-system permissions: accessibility, screen recording, control of other apps. Apple owns those on the Mac, and it is redesigning them while competitors ship agents and while it builds its own. There's a security argument here and a competition argument, and both can be true.

My read: the agent race is moving from which model is smartest to who gets permission to act. For developers, expect friction in onboarding Mac users, and design for it: ask for the narrowest app access you can, and make every action visible. For everyone else, Google's warning is the practical advice. Only let an agent into apps you'd be fine with it misusing after reading the wrong web page.

What to watch next: the final macOS twenty-seven point three release, whether regulators read Apple's warnings as security or as gatekeeping, and whether Google publishes any success-rate numbers for Gemini on the Mac.

### Anthropic's first day of trading

Yesterday we covered Anthropic pricing its IPO at eighty-three dollars a share. On Thursday it started trading on Nasdaq under the ticker A-N-T.

According to CNBC, it opened at one hundred eleven dollars and fifty cents, about thirty-four percent above the IPO price. It didn't hold. It closed at ninety-seven dollars and seventeen cents, still up seventeen percent. More than eight hundred forty-six million shares changed hands, the most of any Nasdaq stock that day, and more than SpaceX traded on its first day. CNBC puts the fully diluted value at the close at about one point eight trillion dollars.

Not everyone celebrated. CNBC reports more than a hundred protesters outside Anthropic's San Francisco headquarters, chanting "ban AI that builds AI," and protesters outside Nasdaq in New York.

My read: the pop at the open shows how much demand there was. The fade into the close shows buyers aren't paying any price. The closing price, not the opening one, is the number to watch from here. The next real test is its first quarterly report as a public company.

### The European Commission's AI-assisted cyberattack

On Thursday, the European Commission confirmed it had responded to what it called a detected cyber campaign. The Record reports the Commission described it as AI-generated.

Here are the facts we have. The first signs were detected on October twenty-second. HR systems went offline for more than ten days, including the platform staff use for payslips, timesheets and leave, and the one for medical reimbursements, according to The Register and The Record. Salaries were still paid. The Commission's spokesperson said there's no evidence of a data breach affecting staff broadly, and that the investigation is still open.

Politico, which broke the story, reported that defenders saw the attackers using ARTEX. We've covered ARTEX before. The EU's own cyber agency, CERT-EU, describes it as an open-source, autonomous penetration-testing platform that plugs into outside AI models. Its author pulled the repository in October after reports of misuse, but copies live on. Last week the FBI warned that some intrusions using it went from first targeting to privileged access in under three hours.

The catch: nobody has said ARTEX was how the attackers got in, and the reports don't say exactly what role it played.

This matters because the gap between a security research tool and an attack tool is now one config file. Defenders get the same speed, but the attacker only has to win once.

### Trump and Xi on AI rules

At the APEC meetings in Shenzhen this week, AI was on the table between Donald Trump and Xi Jinping, and the two sent different signals.

According to AFP, Trump said, quote, "we oppose any form of globalist regulation," or anything to control and stifle what he called super intelligence, adding, "we won't allow that to happen." Xi, according to China's state news agency Xinhua, said the two countries should coordinate and make sure AI always remains under human control.

Then, in a Fox News interview on Thursday, Trump said he and Xi both want AI rules for safety purposes and will work together on reasonable ones, according to Reuters.

Those positions don't fully clash. No global regime, but maybe some bilateral safety talk. Back in September the two sides agreed to set up a channel for urgent AI incidents. We haven't seen any report that the details were finalized. Until there's a written text, treat this as signals, not an agreement.

### Alibaba: AI cloud is making money, Qwen is spending it

Alibaba reported its September quarter on Thursday, and the AI numbers split in two.

The cloud side is growing fast. Revenue from what Alibaba now calls AI Cloud and Compute Services rose fifty-three percent, to about sixty-one billion yuan, roughly nine point one billion dollars. Revenue from outside customers rose sixty-four percent. AI-related product revenue grew at triple digits for the thirteenth quarter in a row.

The consumer side is costly. The unit that holds the Qwen models and the Qwen app grew revenue fifty-one percent, but its adjusted loss rose to about ten and a half billion yuan, from about three point four billion a year ago. Alibaba blamed more investment in AI and higher inference costs from the Qwen app. Capital spending rose sixty percent, and free cash flow was negative.

My read: it's the same pattern as in the US. Selling compute to companies pays now. Building AI apps for consumers is a bet that hasn't paid off yet.

### Quick hits

OpenAI says its models helped design chips. According to Analytics India Magazine and Gigazine, which reported on an OpenAI post this week, OpenAI's models found up to fourteen percent improvement in power, performance and area on AMD's flagship GPU designs, in under five months, on top of designs AMD had already heavily optimized. AMD has reportedly used some of the changes in a recent design. That's a combined design measure of power, performance and area, not a fourteen percent faster GPU, and these are company claims.

And the International Energy Agency published its World Energy Outlook on Thursday. CNBC reports the IEA projects data-center electricity use could double to about a thousand terawatt-hours a year by twenty thirty in its stated-policies scenario.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that AI agents are leaving the chat window. They're operating Macs, booking stays, and in the wrong hands, probing government networks. The models are mostly good enough. The fight now is over permissions: who grants them, who can take them away, and who is responsible when an agent uses them badly. The one thing I would watch next is what Apple ships in macOS twenty-seven point three, because that will set the rules every desktop agent has to live with.

That's Daily AI Signal for today.
