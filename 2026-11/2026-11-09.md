### 头条

先说头条。英伟达董事会，宣布对自己的首席执行官黄仁勋，展开独立调查。

昨天我们讲到，美国众议院三位委员会主席，要求黄仁勋闭门说明他和 Megaspeed 一名高管的接触。Megaspeed 是一家总部在新加坡的数据中心公司，也是英伟达的大客户。这个周日，事情有了新进展。

英伟达董事会在声明里说，鉴于最近有报道称，黄仁勋是一次外国情报活动的目标，董事会决定启动独立调查。调查由独立董事组成的委员会负责，并聘请外部独立律师协助。董事会说，目前没有理由认为国家安全或者英伟达的机密受到了损害，但会审查所有能拿到的信息。董事会还说，黄仁勋完全支持这次调查，董事会对他和管理团队有信心。

同一天，黄仁勋给员工发了一封邮件。他向家人和同事道歉，承认自己对妻子不忠，说这件事没有任何借口。他表示支持调查，会全力配合，也会继续全身心投入英伟达。邮件里没有提到任何人的名字，也没有直接回应情报方面的报道。

再看背后的报道。据美国全国广播公司援引，英国金融时报上周五报道，美国政府认为，黄仁勋和一位与 Megaspeed 有关的中国女商人 Alice Huang 有过亲密关系。两人没有亲属关系。纽约时报则报道，美国情报官员认为，她为中国国家安全部提供情报。这些说法都来自匿名官员，目前没有任何指控。据英国广播公司报道，中方驳斥这些说法是毫无根据的猜测。黄仁勋此前说，他和对方的往来是正常的商业接触。

各方的反应也很清楚。英伟达发言人说，黄仁勋会继续担任首席执行官。美国国务卿鲁比奥拒绝置评，称这是英伟达的内部事务。特朗普总统为黄仁勋说话，称他是一个正直的人。众议院对华特别委员会主席穆勒纳尔则说，国会必须弄清楚，美国国家安全到底受到了多大风险。

为什么这件事重要？英伟达几乎是所有大型 AI 项目的芯片来源，而黄仁勋，一直是在华盛顿主张向中国多卖芯片的最主要声音。

我的判断是，短期内对英伟达生意的影响有限，董事会说没有发现机密泄露，特朗普也在为他说话。更大的影响在政策上。CEO 和一家中国客户的接触，正在被自己的董事会调查，这时候再主张放宽对华出口限制，会更难。再加上上周末的新闻，有报道追问谁拿到了 H200 的许可证，英伟达开始追踪东南亚芯片的位置，整体方向是审查越来越严。

当然，调查的范围和时间表都还不清楚，情报方面的说法也都是匿名的，最后也可能平静收场。接下来看两个日子，11 月 17 日英伟达发布财报，第二天是众议院要求的闭门简报。

### 次要新闻

第二条，AI 背后的电力。据英国金融时报报道，Anthropic 同意花大约 34 亿美元，购买燃气发电机，给自己的数据中心供电。金融时报说，这是已知的 AI 公司单笔最大的电力设备采购。据一位知情人士说，总容量大约 1.7 吉瓦，也就是 170 万千瓦。

这笔交易的结构很特别。得州一家叫 OnePWR 的公司，大约两年前向卡特彼勒订了这批发电机，但一直没凑够钱完成购买。Anthropic 本来打算向这家公司买电，结果在 9 月底，直接接手了这批发电机订单，连安装工作也一起接了过来，但没有收购这家公司。发电机会用在哪里，目前不清楚。Anthropic 和 OnePWR 都拒绝置评，这笔交易也还没有得到独立证实。

为什么值得关注？现在新建 AI 数据中心，最卡脖子的往往不是芯片，而是电。接入电网要等好几年，发电机和燃气轮机也要排队。谁手里已经有订单，谁就抢到了时间。我的判断是，头部 AI 公司正在变成能源买家，设备排队的位置，就像 GPU 配额一样，成了竞争资源。不过，燃气发电意味着排放，也意味着地方上的审批和反对，这些发电机最后落在哪里，值得继续看。

第三条，是 DeepSeek 的智能体应用。11 月 1 日我们讲过，DeepSeek Harness 团队发了一篇关于超级智能体的设计文章。现在，他们公布了产品数据。

DeepSeek 说，它的开源智能体应用 Harness，截至 11 月 4 日，日活跃用户大约 154 万，这只统计了通过官方接口使用的用户。每天消耗大约 92 万亿个 token，第三方插件大约有 3.5 万个，大约百分之六十的活跃用户，自己做过或者装过至少一个插件。Harness 是 8 月中旬才上线的。这些都是 DeepSeek 自己公布的数字，没有经过第三方核实。

下一步，DeepSeek 计划推出官方插件市场，提高插件接口的稳定性，加强插件安全，还要给插件开发者提供支持，可能包括 token 奖励。

这里有意思的地方在于，用户只算官方接口的，也就是说，每一个日活用户，同时也在为 DeepSeek 的 token 付费。我的判断是，这是应用商店的思路，客户端开源免费，靠底层的 token 赚钱，再用插件生态建立护城河。问题在于，据 InfoQ 收集的用户反馈，有人升级后插件配置丢失，也有人抱怨 token 消耗太高。插件市场本身也会带来新的安全风险，DeepSeek 怎么审核插件，和插件有多少一样重要。

### 快讯

接下来是几条快讯。美国参议院定于当地时间周一晚上，就一项两党支持的审批改革法案进行程序性投票。根据两党政策中心的解读，法案要求联邦监管机构调整输电定价，让数据中心承担自己带来的新增电网成本，新数据中心的这部分成本，不能转嫁给其他用户。法案需要 60 票才能推进，众议院民主党领袖杰弗里斯的发言人表示，他不会照单全收。AI 用电谁来买单，已经成了联邦层面的议题。

用 Claude Code 的开发者，建议检查一个文件。据网络安全新闻网站 Cyber Security News 报道，安全公司 Apiiro 发现，在 Claude Code 里选择“同意，并且不再询问”，可能会把这条命令，连同里面的密码或者密钥，一起存进本地的设置文件。如果这个文件被提交到 GitHub，密钥就公开了。Apiiro 找到 1122 份含有真实密钥的公开文件，算上历史提交等，一共大约 2500 个不同的密钥。常用的密钥扫描工具，也漏掉了其中一部分。我们没有看到 Anthropic 的回应。建议的做法是，先更换泄露的密钥，再把这个文件移出版本管理。

苹果新任首席执行官特努斯，想让 AI 推进得更快。据彭博社记者古尔曼报道，特努斯在一次闭门会议上，要求软件负责人费德里吉加快软件路线图，不能只满足于修好 Siri。古尔曼说，Siri 最早可能在明年变得更像一个智能体。苹果没有回应。

最后，一个关于 AI 做数学的冷静数据。清华大学和月之暗面的研究者发布了一个新基准，叫 TCSExam，测试模型做理论计算机科学研究的能力。OpenAI 的 GPT-6 Astra 总分第一，在定理证明任务里，有百分之七十完成了机器可验证的证明，但 30 个开放猜想，一个也没有解决。月之暗面自己的 Kimi K3.1 Pro 排第二，需要说明的是，这个基准就是月之暗面参与做的。简单说，证明已知结论越来越常见，真正解决开放问题，还很少见。

### 结语

把今天的新闻放在一起看，AI 最大的限制，已经不只是技术。英伟达这周要面对的，是华盛顿的信任。Anthropic 要解决的，是电。DeepSeek 要建的，是一个大家能放心接入的生态。这些问题，都不是一个更强的模型能解决的。

接下来最值得关注的，是 11 月 17 日英伟达的财报电话会，看它怎么谈这次调查，怎么谈中国。以上就是今天的 Daily AI Signal。

### Main story: Nvidia's board investigates Jensen Huang

Yesterday we mentioned that three House committee chairs had asked Jensen Huang for a closed-door briefing about his contacts with an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data center company and a big Nvidia customer. On Sunday, the story moved inside Nvidia.

Here's what happened. Nvidia's board said it is starting an independent investigation, in its words, "in light of recent reporting" that Huang "has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort." A committee of independent directors, with outside counsel, will run it. The board says it has no reason to believe that national security or Nvidia's secrets were compromised, but that the inquiry will look at all available information. It says Huang fully supports the review, and that the board has confidence in him and his leadership team.

The same day, Huang sent an email to staff. He apologized to his family and his colleagues, and wrote that he had been unfaithful to his wife. Quote: "There is no excuse for what I did." He said he supports the investigation, will cooperate fully, and remains fully committed to Nvidia. The email doesn't name anyone, and it doesn't address the intelligence reports directly.

Now the reporting behind this. According to NBC, the Financial Times reported on Friday that the US government believed Huang had a romantic relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman linked to Megaspeed. She is not related to him. The New York Times has reported that US intelligence officials believe she was an asset of China's Ministry of State Security. Those are allegations from unnamed officials. There are no charges. China has rejected the allegations as groundless speculation, according to the BBC. Huang had earlier described his dealings with her as business, and "completely normal."

Megaspeed matters here. According to the Times, as cited by CBS, it bought more than four point eight billion dollars of Nvidia chips over two years.

The reactions split along predictable lines. An Nvidia spokesperson said Huang is committed to staying on as CEO without interruption, and that, quote, "if we were the target of a genuine threat, the U.S. government would have notified us." Secretary of State Marco Rubio declined to comment, calling it an internal corporate matter. President Trump defended Huang, calling him an honorable guy. And Representative John Moolenaar, who chairs the House China committee, said Congress needs to determine how far US national security was put at risk.

So why does this matter beyond the headlines?

Nvidia is the supply line for almost every serious AI project, and Jensen Huang has been the industry's loudest voice in Washington for selling more chips to China. His argument rests partly on trust: that Nvidia knows its customers and can keep its technology where it's supposed to be.

My read: the short-term business risk looks limited. The board says it has no evidence anything was compromised, and President Trump has defended him. The bigger effect is on policy. It gets harder for Nvidia to argue for looser China export rules while its CEO's contacts with a Chinese customer are under review by his own board. Combine that with what we covered over the weekend: reporting that questioned who got H-two-hundred licenses, and Nvidia starting to track where its chips sit in Southeast Asia. The direction is toward more scrutiny, not less.

The counterpoint: we don't know the scope or the timeline of the investigation, the intelligence claims are anonymous, and boards open independent reviews partly to get ahead of exactly this kind of pressure. It could end quietly.

What to watch: November seventeenth, Nvidia's earnings, where analysts will ask about it whether management wants to or not. Then November eighteenth, the closed-door briefing House chairs have requested. And any update from the board's committee.

### Secondary: Anthropic buys its own generators

Next, the power behind AI.

The Financial Times reports that Anthropic has agreed to pay about three point four billion dollars for gas-fired generators to run its data centers. The FT calls it the biggest known single purchase of power equipment by an AI company. According to one person familiar with the deal, it covers one point seven gigawatts of generators.

The structure is unusual. A San Antonio company called OnePWR Solutions ordered the generators from Caterpillar about two years ago, but needed money to complete the purchase. Anthropic had planned to buy power from OnePWR. Instead, in late September, it took over the generator orders and the installation work itself. It didn't buy the company. Where the generators will go is not known. Anthropic and OnePWR declined to comment, and the deal hasn't been independently confirmed.

For context, Anthropic's amended IPO filing earlier this month put its spending commitments at about eight hundred ninety-five billion dollars, almost all for compute and infrastructure.

Why it matters: power, not chips, is increasingly the bottleneck for new AI campuses. Grid connections take years, and generators and turbines have long waiting lists. So whoever already holds an order holds time. My read: frontier labs are becoming energy buyers, and a place in the equipment queue is now a competitive asset, a bit like a GPU allocation.

The catch: gas generation means emissions and local permitting fights, and we've seen how those go. Voters rejected data center projects in most local ballot measures last week, and xAI is in court over turbines in Memphis. Watch where these generators end up, and whether the local community gets a say.

### Secondary: DeepSeek's agent app hits 1.5 million daily users

On November first we covered the DeepSeek Harness team's essay on what they called a super-agent. Now they've put numbers on the product.

DeepSeek says Harness, its open-source agent app, had about one point five four million daily active users as of November fourth. That counts only people using DeepSeek's official API. It says users consume about ninety-two trillion tokens a day, that about thirty-five thousand third-party plugins have been built, and that about sixty percent of active users have created or installed at least one. Harness launched in mid-August. These are DeepSeek's own figures, not audited.

What's next: DeepSeek plans an official plugin marketplace, more stable plugin interfaces, stronger plugin security, and a support program for plugin authors that may include token grants.

Here's what's interesting. Because the count includes only official-API users, every one of those daily users is also a paying customer for DeepSeek's tokens. My read: this is the app-store playbook. Give away the client, sell the tokens underneath, and let plugins become the moat.

The catch: early user reports, collected by InfoQ, include a buggy upgrade that wiped plugin settings and complaints about heavy token use. And a plugin marketplace is a new supply-chain risk. We've seen what tampered extensions can do in code editors. How DeepSeek reviews plugins will matter as much as how many there are.

### Quick hits

The US Senate has scheduled a procedural vote for Monday evening, Washington time, on a bipartisan permitting bill that would make data centers pay for the grid costs they add. Under the bill, as explained by the Bipartisan Policy Center, federal regulators would revise transmission pricing so data centers pay their share plus the added costs they cause, and for new data centers, other customers couldn't be charged for those costs. It needs sixty votes, and a spokesman for House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries says he won't rubber-stamp it without more involvement from House Democrats. Who pays for AI's electricity is now a federal question.

If you use Claude Code, check one file. Security firm Apiiro, as reported by Cyber Security News, found that choosing "Yes, and don't ask again" can save the approved command, including any password or key in it, into a local settings file. When that file gets committed, the secret goes public. Apiiro found one thousand one hundred twenty-two public copies of the file containing real secrets, and about two and a half thousand distinct secrets once it counted git history and related files. Common scanners missed a share of them. We haven't seen a response from Anthropic. The fix: rotate anything exposed first, then untrack the file and add it to your git ignore.

Apple's new CEO wants more speed on AI. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports that John Ternus told software chief Craig Federighi, in a closed-door meeting, that Apple needs to accelerate its software roadmap and move beyond fixing Siri. Gurman says Siri could work more like an agent as early as next year. Apple hasn't commented. A week ago we covered Ternus saying Apple won't ship "AI for AI's sake." Both can be true. The pressure is on timing, not on principle.

And a reality check on AI math. A new benchmark called TCSExam, from researchers at Tsinghua and Moonshot AI, tests models across theoretical computer science research. OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra led the full suite. It completed machine-checked proofs on seventy percent of the theorem tasks, but solved none of the thirty open conjectures. Moonshot's own Kimi K-three point one Pro came second, and it's worth noting Moonshot helped build the test. The takeaway: proving known results is getting routine. Settling open problems is still rare.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that AI's biggest constraints are no longer just technical. Nvidia's challenge this week is trust in Washington. Anthropic's is electricity. DeepSeek's is building an ecosystem people can safely plug into. None of those are solved by a better model.

The one thing I'd watch next is November seventeenth, Nvidia's earnings call, for how the company talks about the investigation and about China.
