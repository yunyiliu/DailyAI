### 头条

先说头条。黄仁勋回来了。周一，英伟达董事会宣布，全票通过，恢复黄仁勋的总裁、首席执行官和董事职务，立即生效。

这件事我们跟了两周。黄仁勋是 11 月 12 日辞职的，起因是有报道说，他成了一次外国情报行动的目标。之后由首席财务官 Colette Kress 临时代理 CEO，她会继续当首席财务官，董事长还是 Harvey Jones。就在上周二，这位董事长还说，董事会不打算让黄仁勋回来。

董事会的声明很短，所以每个字都值得看。声明说，董事会对黄仁勋的坦诚和领导能力感到满意。还说，董事会收到了美国政府高层的意见，据此确信，他回归不会带来国家安全风险。至于是哪些官员，查到了什么，声明没有说。

声明没提的，是特朗普。据美国全国广播公司和 CNBC 报道，特朗普说，他直接和间接地跟董事会沟通过，还说黄仁勋没做错什么。

市场反应很直接。据 CNBC 报道，英伟达周一收盘大涨大约百分之三十一，股价从上周五的 125 美元左右，回到 165 美元左右。

也有人不买账。参议员沃伦说，这是一个鲁莽的决定。参议员科宁反问，没有国家安全风险，依据是什么？

还有不少事没有结束。董事会说，独立董事委员会对那次外国情报行动的调查，会继续进行到底。据华尔街日报和路透社报道，美国证监会正在调查英伟达，华尔街日报说，重点是会计做法，证监会拒绝置评。CNBC 还报道，美国对新加坡公司 Megaspeed，也就是 Alice Huang 以前任职的公司，调查仍在进行。

为什么重要？英伟达的芯片，是大部分 AI 公司的算力底座。过去几周，谁来掌舵这个问题，一直压着整个 AI 板块。现在这个问题有了答案。

我的判断是，当初让黄仁勋离开的那个问题，并没有公开回答，而是由不具名官员的意见回答的，而且总统说他游说过董事会。所以真正的看点，是董事会还有多独立。英伟达之后在中国市场、出口合规、卖给谁这些事上的决定，都会被放在这个背景下看。

当然，董事会可能掌握着不能公开的信息。而且一天涨百分之三十一，主要是收回前几周的跌幅，不代表芯片需求变了。接下来要看三件事，董事会委员会的调查结论，证监会的调查会不会有下文，还有国会会不会叫黄仁勋去作证。

### 次要新闻

第二条，亚马逊。周一亚马逊宣布，已经签协议，买下 AI 初创公司 Decart 一套优化软件背后的技术，还把 Decart 的三位联合创始人和研究团队，一起招进亚马逊。

亚马逊没有公布价格，据彭博社报道，大约 75 亿美元。交易还需要监管批准。

亚马逊买的，不是模型，也不是一个产品，而是让 AI 芯片跑得更快的软件，和写这套软件的人。亚马逊说，Decart 团队之前就和它合作，在亚马逊自研的 AI 芯片 Trainium 上，做出了很好的性能。亚马逊负责自研芯片的高管 Peter DeSantis 说，AI 的每一次进步，都从榨出每一块芯片的更多性能开始。

这件事真正重要的地方在于，芯片好不好用，很大程度看软件。我的判断是，亚马逊这笔钱，是在补软件这块短板。另外，交易的结构也值得注意，买技术、招团队，但不买公司本身。亚马逊没说 Decart 自己的视频模型会怎么样。下周一，亚马逊的 re:Invent 大会开幕，Trainium 应该会有更多消息。

第三条，Anthropic 发布了 Claude Ship，是一组处理 GitHub 代码合并请求的 Agent。合并请求，就是程序员提交代码改动，等别人审核通过，再并进主代码。

一共三个 Agent。第一个叫 Code Review，负责审代码，之前已经正式上线。第二个叫 PR Steward，目前是测试版，负责修复没通过的检查、代码冲突和审核意见。Anthropic 说，它设计上不会批准，也不会合并代码，每次提交后，还会说明改了什么，怎么撤回。

第三个最值得看，叫 Claude Approvals，也是测试版。团队把审批规则写成一个文件，放在代码仓库里。按 Anthropic 的说法，如果规则允许，Claude 可以自己批准常规改动，也可以要求人来签字。能不能真正合并，还是看仓库的分支规则和开发者的权限。

目前只给 Claude 团队版和企业版，只支持 GitHub，按用量收费。Anthropic 说，它自己的 Claude Code 团队，从 6 月到 9 月，合并的代码请求增加了一倍多，被撤回的比例下降了百分之四十以上。这是它自己团队的数字，不是第三方测的。

我的判断是，写代码已经变便宜了，审代码成了瓶颈。这套产品真正的核心，是那个规则文件，把谁可以批准什么，写清楚，也能被检查。问题在于，用模型去批准模型写的代码，和让另一位工程师审核，不是一回事。Anthropic 自己也说，它可能漏掉漏洞和安全问题。

第四条，谷歌 DeepMind 和谷歌量子 AI 团队发布了一项研究，用 Gemini 4 的 Agent 去找更好的量子算法，而且每个结果都附带机器验证的证明。

为什么要证明？因为大型量子电路，现在的电脑和量子芯片都跑不了，没法测试。所以 Agent 在一个叫 Lean 的证明工具里写程序，由 Lean 来检查程序对不对，需要多少资源。

结果是，一个叫 arcsin 的常用程序，很多量子算法用它把数字装进量子态，速度提升了 290 倍。一个和核聚变有关的模拟，成本降低了大约 12 倍。谷歌把相关的代码库、问题和验证过的答案，都开源了。

谷歌自己也说，这些模拟需要的资源，仍然超过第一批纠错量子计算机大概能提供的水平。而且这些是纸面上证明的成本，不是在硬件上跑出来的。我的判断是，用证明来检查 Agent 的工作，你就不用只听 Agent 自己怎么说。

### Hook

### Today's top stories

### Main story: Jensen Huang is back

We've been following this story for two weeks. Here's what changed.

On Monday, Nvidia announced that its board had reinstated Jensen Huang as president, chief executive, and board member, effective immediately. The vote was unanimous. Huang had resigned on November twelfth, after reports that he was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. Chief financial officer Colette Kress ran the company in the meantime, and she stays on as CFO. Harvey Jones stays on as board chair.

The board's statement is short, so the exact words matter. It says the board was, quote, "satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang's candor and ability to lead." And it says it "received input from senior levels of the United States government," and that based on that input, it is confident his return "poses no national security risk." It doesn't say who those officials were, or what they found.

Huang's only line in the release: "I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment."

Now the part the release leaves out. President Trump told CNBC that he spoke to the board, in his words, "directly and indirectly," and that Huang "did nothing wrong." On Friday he had said he'd love to see Huang get a second chance. Last Tuesday, the board chair said the board did not intend to reinstate him.

The market's answer was immediate. Nvidia closed up about thirty-one percent on Monday, according to CNBC and NBC. That takes it to about a hundred and sixty-five dollars, from about a hundred and twenty-five on Friday. Yahoo Finance says it added nearly a trillion dollars in market value in one day.

Not everyone was cheering. Senator Elizabeth Warren called it a "reckless decision." Senator John Cornyn asked, "No national security risk? Based on what?"

And a lot is still open. The board says its independent committee investigating the foreign intelligence effort will, quote, "proceed to conclusion." The Wall Street Journal and Reuters have reported that the SEC is looking into Nvidia, and the Journal says the focus is its accounting practices; the SEC declined to comment. CNBC reports that a U.S. investigation into Megaspeed, the Singapore company where Alice Huang used to work, is ongoing. Nvidia says it will keep cooperating with government investigations.

So why does this matter beyond one company? Nvidia makes the chips most of the AI industry runs on. For weeks, the question of who runs it had been dragging on the whole AI trade. That question now has an answer.

My read is this. The question that forced Huang out was never answered in public. It was answered by input from officials nobody named, after the president said he lobbied the board. That makes the board's independence the real story. Nvidia's next decisions on China, on export compliance, on who it sells to, will be read in that light.

The counterpoint is fair. The board may know things it can't publish, and founder-led companies often do better with the founder. And a thirty-one percent day mostly wins back what the stock lost in the last few weeks. It isn't new demand for chips.

What to watch: the board committee's findings, whether the SEC inquiry goes anywhere, and whether Congress calls Huang to testify.

### Amazon buys Decart's optimization technology

Second story. On Monday, Amazon said it signed an agreement to buy the technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack, and to bring Decart's three co-founders, Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev, and Orian Leitersdorf, and their team of researchers and engineers to Amazon.

Amazon didn't give a price. Bloomberg reported about seven and a half billion dollars. The deal still needs regulatory clearance.

Here's what Amazon is actually buying. Not a model, and not a product people use. It's buying software that gets more work out of AI chips, and the people who wrote it. Amazon says Decart's team had already worked closely with it to get strong results on Trainium, Amazon's own AI chip. Peter DeSantis, who runs Amazon's custom silicon and AI models work, put it this way: "Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip."

This matters because a chip is only as good as the software that keeps it busy. That software layer is a big part of why companies stick with Nvidia. Amazon is paying a reported multi-billion sum to close that gap.

The interesting part is the structure. Amazon buys the technology and hires the team, but doesn't buy the company. Amazon's post doesn't say what happens to Decart's own video models. Big tech has used this kind of deal before for AI startups. This one still has to clear regulators. Amazon's re:Invent conference starts next Monday, so expect to hear more about Trainium there.

### Anthropic's Claude Ship

Third. Anthropic launched Claude Ship on Monday. It's three agents for GitHub pull requests.

The first, Code Review, was already generally available. It reviews changes and posts what it finds. The second, PR Steward, is new and in beta. It fixes failing checks, merge conflicts, and review comments. Anthropic says it's designed not to approve or merge anything, and after every push it comments with what it changed and how to undo it.

The third is the one to look at. Claude Approvals, also in beta, applies an approval policy that the team writes as a file in the repository. In Anthropic's words, "Claude can approve routine changes on its own if the policy allows it, or can require deeper human sign-off." What can actually merge still depends on the repository's branch rules and the developer's permissions. Anthropic recommends starting in observe-only mode.

It's in beta for Claude Team and Enterprise plans, GitHub only, billed on usage. Anthropic says that on its own Claude Code team, merged pull requests more than doubled from June to September, while the share that got reverted fell by more than forty percent. That's Anthropic's own team, not an independent measurement.

Why it matters: coding agents made writing code cheap, and review became the bottleneck. My read is that the policy file is the real product. It turns "who is allowed to approve what" into something written down and reviewable. The catch is independence. A model approving code that models wrote is a different kind of check than a second engineer. Anthropic itself says the tool may miss bugs and security issues.

### Quantum algorithms with proofs attached

Fourth. Google DeepMind and Google Quantum AI published work on Monday where Gemini 4 agents searched for better quantum algorithms, and every result came with a machine-checked proof.

The setup: you can't test big quantum circuits on today's computers or today's quantum chips. So the agents wrote programs in the Lean proof assistant, and Lean checked both that each program is correct and how many resources it needs.

The results, against prior baselines: a routine called arcsin, which many quantum algorithms use to load numbers into quantum states, got a two hundred and ninety times speedup. A specific version of Grover's search algorithm got two point five seven times faster. For a fusion-related simulation, cost fell by about twelve times, and the memory needed dropped from five thousand six hundred and fifty error-corrected qubits to four thousand three hundred and thirty-eight. Google open-sourced the Lean library, the problems, and the verified solutions.

The catch, in Google's own words: running these simulations would still need more resources than the first error-corrected quantum computers are likely to have. And these are proofs about cost on paper, not runs on hardware.

My read: this follows AlphaProof Code, which we covered on November twelfth, where Google's agents wrote Lean proofs about the Signal protocol. The pattern is clear. When a proof checks the agent's work, you don't have to take the agent's word for it.
