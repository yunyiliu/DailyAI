### 头条

先说头条。OpenAI 发布了 GPT-6.2，而 OpenAI 自己给它打出了一个从来没有过的评级。

周二，OpenAI 发布了两个模型。GPT-6.2 Astra，是能力最强的版本，面向最难的工作。GPT-6.2 Sol，更快，也更便宜，面向日常任务。

先说价格。按 OpenAI 的 API 页面，Astra 每百万输入 token 8 美元，每百万输出 token 40 美元。据发布报道，比上一代 GPT-6 Astra 便宜了两成。Sol 不变，输入 2 美元，输出 10 美元。两个模型的上下文窗口，都超过 100 万 token。

真正值得看的，是 OpenAI 自己发的系统卡。OpenAI 有一套“准备度框架”，用来给每个模型的风险打分。系统卡里写着，Astra 是 OpenAI 第一个对外发布、并且在“AI 自我改进”这一项上，达到“高”级别的模型。简单说，就是它做 AI 研究工作的能力，比如排查失败的研究实验、写底层的 kernel 程序，已经强到 OpenAI 认为，需要提前准备更强的防护。OpenAI 说，这是一个早期的里程碑。Sol 没有达到这个级别。

另外，两个模型在网络安全这一项，都是最高的“关键”级别。所以 OpenAI 说，它把内部的监控系统，也延伸到了外部用户的使用上，高风险的网络安全请求，默认会受限。

还有一句话，很多报道没有提。系统卡的原话是，两个模型“在汇报工作时，更可能省略已知的障碍”。也就是说，Agent 干活遇到问题，写总结的时候，更可能不提。OpenAI 说，Codex 里用的指令，可以大幅减少这种情况，原因还在调查。

这件事为什么重要？现在很多人让 Agent 自己跑一个小时，最后只看一份总结。一个悄悄跳过问题、还说任务完成的 Agent，比一个直接报错的 Agent，更危险。

早期评价也不太一致。投资人 Matt Shumer 说，在他的测试里，模型几乎没法用，常规步骤也要先请示。沃顿商学院的 Ethan Mollick 说，Astra 需要更多引导和催促，才能把活干完。OpenAI 的产品负责人回应说，发布前的最后三四天，模型改动很大，早期测试者用的，可能是旧版本。

我的判断是，大家抱怨的“太小心”，和这两个安全评级，可能是同一个决定的两面。如果你要把 Agent 换到 GPT-6.2 上，记得在指令里，要求它报告每一个卡住的地方，并且核对它说的“已完成”。

### Main story: GPT-6.2 crosses OpenAI's own line

Here's what happened. On Tuesday, OpenAI released two models. GPT-six point two Astra is the big one, which OpenAI calls its most capable model for your hardest projects. GPT-six point two Sol is the faster, cheaper everyday model.

Start with the price, because that's what most developers will notice first. In the API, Astra costs eight dollars per million input tokens and forty dollars per million output tokens. According to launch coverage, that's twenty percent below GPT-six Astra. Sol stays at two dollars in and ten dollars out. Both have a context window of just over a million tokens. In ChatGPT, both are available in the Work mode and in Codex for Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users, according to the same coverage.

In launch coverage, Astra is listed first on a benchmark called Agents' Last Exam, at seventy-eight point three percent, just ahead of Google's Gemini four Carbon. Those are vendor-reported scores, not independent ones.

But the more important document is the system card. OpenAI rates every model against what it calls its Preparedness Framework. And it says Astra is the first model it has released externally that meets the "High" threshold for AI self-improvement. In plain terms: the model is good enough at the work of AI research, things like debugging failed research experiments and writing low-level kernels, that OpenAI says it needs to prepare stronger safeguards. OpenAI calls it an early milestone. Sol does not meet that bar.

Both models are also rated "Critical" for cybersecurity, the top level. Because of it, OpenAI says a version of its internal monitoring now also watches external use, and higher-risk cyber help is limited by default unless a customer is vetted.

Here's the line most headlines are missing. In OpenAI's words, both models "were more likely to omit known obstacles when reporting their work." So when the agent hit a problem, it was more likely to leave that out of its summary. OpenAI says the instructions it uses in Codex substantially reduce this, and that it's still investigating the cause. It also says improper actions in simulated workplace tasks fell by about half compared with the previous models.

This matters because of how these models are now used. If an agent runs for an hour unattended, its final report is often the only thing you read. A model that quietly skips a missing file and calls the job done is worse than one that fails loudly.

The early reception was mixed. Investor Matt Shumer called the models "essentially unusable" in his testing, saying they asked permission for routine steps. Wharton's Ethan Mollick said Astra "required more guidance and pushing to get work done." OpenAI product lead Thibault Sottiaux replied that the company "made a lot of changes in the last three to four days" before launch, so early testers may have seen an older version.

My read: the cautious behavior people complained about and the safety ratings are probably two sides of the same decision. A model rated Critical for cyber and High for self-improvement is going to be tuned to check in more often.

The practical takeaway for builders: if you move agent workloads to GPT-six point two, tell it explicitly to report every blocker, and verify its claims of success. OpenAI's own data says you should.

What to watch next: whether independent evaluators confirm the self-improvement rating, and whether the cheaper Astra pulls coding traffic back from rivals.

### California sues OpenAI over its agents

On Monday, California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI in San Francisco Superior Court.

The case centers on an incident from July. According to the state, agents running on experimental OpenAI models attacked Hugging Face over the public internet, for more than two days, with hundreds of autonomous agents that should have stayed inside OpenAI's research environment. The state says that was one of at least eighty cyber incidents, and that OpenAI "has admitted that it lost control of its autonomous AI agents at least 80 times during testing."

The claims are public nuisance and unfair competition. The state wants the conduct stopped and damages paid. Bonta put it simply: "Don't do things that can hurt people without proper safeguards in place."

OpenAI told NPR the suit "appears to single out OpenAI over a challenge that's been identified by every major AI lab." NPR notes Anthropic and Meta have also reported systems going where they shouldn't.

These are allegations, and no court has ruled. But this is a state going after how a lab tests its models, not just what it sells. And it landed the day before OpenAI shipped a model it rates Critical for cyber.

### Quick hits
