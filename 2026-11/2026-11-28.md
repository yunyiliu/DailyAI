### 头条：OpenAI 反诉苹果

先说头条。OpenAI 在法庭上反击苹果了。

10 月我们讲过，苹果起诉 OpenAI，说两名前员工，Tang Tan 和 Chang Liu，把苹果的硬件机密带到了 OpenAI。Tang Tan 是 io 的联合创始人，io 是 OpenAI 去年收购的硬件公司。当时，OpenAI 同意在案子审理期间，不使用苹果所说的这些机密。

周五，OpenAI 提交了反诉。我们看了反诉文件，一共 81 页，递交到加州圣何塞的联邦法院，OpenAI、io，还有这两名员工，都是反诉方。文件主要讲了三件事。

第一，反过来指控苹果窃取商业秘密。文件里说，有一名员工，文件里叫他 X 员工，从苹果来到 OpenAI，做了一段时间运营工作，又回到了苹果。OpenAI 说，他在六个月里，把 OpenAI 的文件发到自己的苹果账号和手机号上，离开时带走了几十份文件，回去以后，还应苹果的要求，向苹果高层介绍了 OpenAI 的硬件工作，和未发布产品的路线图。

第二，供应商。苹果和 OpenAI 用的精密制造商，有一部分是同样的几家。文件说，苹果向这些供应商施压，让他们别给 OpenAI 的项目投入资源。文件举了两个例子。一家原本给 OpenAI 做得好好的制造商，不再接 OpenAI 的活了。另一家供应商，在苹果反对两家的零件同时加工之后，把 OpenAI 的阳极氧化工序，挪到了夜班。文件没有点名是哪家供应商。

第三，员工。OpenAI 说，苹果鼓励员工把工作文件存在个人 iCloud 里，用个人 iMessage 谈工作。员工一跳槽，手里自然还有苹果的资料，苹果就可以说他偷了东西。文件把这叫作“暗门”。文件还说，已经有 400 名苹果员工加入了 OpenAI，这是 OpenAI 自己给的数字。

苹果的回应是，它的案子讲的是 OpenAI 员工故意窃取苹果的机密，OpenAI 的反诉，是在转移注意力。

为什么重要？在这之前，这个案子看起来只是两名工程师和一些文件的纠纷。现在，它变成了一场关于老牌硬件巨头，怎么对待新对手的官司，涉及供应链、雇佣规则，还有人才流动。

我的判断是，供应商这部分最关键，也最难证明。文件里有些说法，是基于 OpenAI 掌握的信息和推断，也没有点名任何一家供应商。但如果有供应商出来作证，说苹果让他们拖慢 OpenAI，那对苹果来说，问题就比几份文件大得多了。

要提醒的是，这些都还只是一方的指控，没有经过法庭检验。在商业秘密官司里，反诉也是常见的策略。接下来，要看苹果会不会申请驳回反诉，以及有没有供应商被点名。

### 次要新闻：英伟达最新机柜出现在上海

第二条，是我们这个月一直在跟的英伟达。

彭博社通过中国的贷款登记文件发现，英伟达最新的 Blackwell 设备，出现在上海一个大型数据中心里。我们读的是雅虎财经和阿纳多卢通讯社转载的彭博社报道。

文件显示，上海一家名叫 Shuoyao 的公司，把一整台 GB300 NVL72 机柜，抵押给了上海的一家银行，贷款 9600 万元人民币，约合 1400 万美元。这家公司给 Megaspeed 提供服务，它说双方只是普通的商业合作。同一个园区里，另一家名叫 Kezhan 的公司，有一台 GB200 服务器。登记文件显示，它的大股东是一家东京公司，而据财新报道，这家东京公司由 Alice Huang 控制。她就是英伟达这场高层风波里，那位前 Megaspeed 高管。

彭博社还通过公开的保修数据库，把这台机柜的序列号，追到了台湾服务器厂商技嘉。技嘉不愿透露最初的买家，说自己的业务合规。Blackwell 芯片运往中国，一直需要美国的出口许可。

有两点要说清楚。彭博社说，没有发现 Megaspeed 本身转运了这些设备，Megaspeed 也否认有任何违规。设备怎么到的上海，目前还不清楚。我们也没有看到英伟达的回应。

时间点很关键。上周一刚复职的英伟达 CEO 黄仁勋，同意 12 月 1 日私下会见国会的几个委员会，12 月 3 日，他会在参议院银行委员会公开作证，听证会的主题是出口管制。

我的判断是，参议员们现在有了一台具体的机柜，和一个具体的地址可以追问。最关键的问题是路径，从技嘉最初的买家，到上海的一份贷款文件，中间发生了什么。

### 次要新闻：比利时叫停谷歌数据中心

第三条，说明决定 AI 算力建在哪里的，不只是芯片，还有水。

周四，比利时南部的瓦隆大区政府，下令谷歌停止建设沙勒罗瓦附近的数据中心，当时工地上大约有 300 名工人。

事情是这样的。谷歌最早拿到的许可，是三座风冷数据中心。去年，它改了方案，把建筑合并，改用附近河流的河水冷却。大区部长说，这需要每小时大约 600 立方米的河水，比利时通讯社报道，回到河里的不到四分之一。这个修改后的许可，今年 4 月获批。居民提出申诉后，大区的部长在 8 月撤销了许可。谷歌向比利时最高行政法院上诉，但在等待裁决期间，一直没有停工。法院的听证定在明年 1 月 7 日。

谷歌表示，愿意和大区政府开放沟通。推动叫停的绿色和平比利时分部说，科技巨头不能凌驾于法律之上。

我的判断是，这是政府因为冷却用水，直接让一家云计算巨头的工地停下来。在许可被撤销后继续施工，等于押注上诉能赢。以后在欧洲建数据中心的公司，都得把这种风险算进去。

### 快讯

接下来是快讯。

俄罗斯的 Sber，在 Hugging Face 上发布了 GigaChat 4 的开放权重。大模型总参数 7080 亿，激活参数大约 410 亿，上下文大约 100 万 token，用的是 MIT 许可。但小一号的 370 亿参数模型，许可禁止商用。Sber 自己公布的成绩有高有低：在一项长时程的编程测试 DeepSWE 上，它大约 35 分，DeepSeek 近期的一个模型，大约 74 分，不过在 SWE-bench Verified 上，两者很接近。

### 结语

今天这几条新闻，背后有一个共同的趋势，AI 最难的约束，越来越多来自物理世界和法律。零件找谁做，机柜最后落在哪里，一条河能不能冷却你的机房，这些都不是模型跑分决定的。

接下来，我最关注的，是 12 月 3 日黄仁勋的听证会。如果参议员们拿着那台上海机柜的序列号来提问，出口管制的讨论，会一下子变得非常具体。

### Hook

OpenAI has hit back at Apple in court. In an eighty-one-page filing, it says Apple took its secrets, leaned on suppliers, and set traps for anyone trying to leave. Apple calls it a distraction. And it's not the only fight over AI hardware this week.

### Today's top stories

### Main story: OpenAI countersues Apple

Back in October we covered OpenAI agreeing not to use what Apple says are its trade secrets while Apple's lawsuit runs. Apple sued this summer. It says two former Apple employees, Tang Tan and Chang Liu, took confidential hardware information to OpenAI. Tan co-founded io Products, the hardware company OpenAI bought last year.

On Friday, OpenAI answered with counterclaims of its own. We read the filing. It was filed in federal court in San Jose, and OpenAI, io Products, Tan and Liu are all on it. It makes three arguments.

First, trade secrets, in the other direction. OpenAI describes a person it calls Employee X, who came from Apple, worked in OpenAI's operations for a short time, then went back to Apple. The filing says that over six months he sent OpenAI documents to personal Apple accounts and phone numbers, and left with dozens of them. It says he then briefed senior Apple people on OpenAI's hardware work and its roadmap for unreleased products, at Apple's request. That claim is brought under the federal Defend Trade Secrets Act.

Second, suppliers. This is the part I'd pay most attention to. OpenAI and Apple use some of the same specialist manufacturers. The filing says Apple pushed those vendors not to put resources on OpenAI work. It gives two examples. A manufacturer that had done OpenAI work without problems stopped doing it. And another supplier moved OpenAI's work in a shared facility onto a night shift of its anodizing process, after Apple objected to OpenAI parts being processed at the same time as Apple's. No supplier is named.

Third, employees. OpenAI says Apple encourages staff to keep work files in their personal iCloud accounts and do business over personal iMessage. Then, when someone leaves for a rival, Apple can say they walked out with company information. The filing calls these "trapdoors." It also says four hundred Apple employees have joined OpenAI so far. That's OpenAI's number, and it claims Apple wants to scare off the next four hundred.

Apple's response, to CBS News: its case is about OpenAI employees "deliberately stealing" its confidential information, and the counterclaims are "a transparent attempt to distract from the substance of Apple's lawsuit."

So what's new here? Until Friday, this looked like a narrow dispute about two engineers and some files. Now it's a fight about how a device incumbent treats a new rival. Its suppliers, its employment rules, its people.

My read: the supplier claims are the most important part, and the hardest to prove. Some are pleaded on information and belief, and no vendor is named. But if a supplier ends up testifying that Apple told it to slow-walk OpenAI, that's a much bigger problem for Apple than a dispute over files.

The catch: these are allegations in a pleading. None has been tested. And counterclaims are a standard move in trade-secret fights. They give you leverage, and they open the other side's conduct to discovery.

What to watch: whether Apple moves to throw the counterclaims out, and whether any supplier gets named.

### Second story: Blackwell racks at a Shanghai site

Next, an update on the Nvidia story we've been following all month.

Bloomberg reports that Nvidia's newest hardware has turned up at a large data center in Shanghai, and it found this through Chinese loan filings. We read Bloomberg's story through syndicated copies, on Yahoo Finance and Anadolu Agency.

Here's what the filings show. A Shanghai company called Shuoyao pledged a GB300 NVL72 rack, that's a full rack of Nvidia's top Blackwell systems, as collateral for a loan of ninety-six million yuan, about fourteen million dollars. Shuoyao provides services to Megaspeed, and it describes that as ordinary commercial cooperation. A second company at the same site, Shanghai Kezhan, has a GB200 server there. The filings show Kezhan is mostly owned by a Tokyo firm, and the Chinese outlet Caixin has reported that Alice Huang controls that firm. She's the former Megaspeed executive at the center of the Nvidia leadership saga.

Bloomberg also traced the rack's serial number, through a public warranty database, to the Taiwanese server maker Gigabyte. Gigabyte wouldn't name the original buyer and says it follows the law. Blackwell chips have always needed a US export license to go to China.

Two important limits. Bloomberg says it found no evidence that Megaspeed itself diverted this hardware. And Megaspeed denies wrongdoing. How the equipment got to Shanghai is still unclear. We didn't see a response from Nvidia.

The timing matters. Jensen Huang, reinstated as Nvidia's CEO last Monday, has agreed to meet congressional committees privately on December first. On December third, he testifies publicly before the Senate Banking Committee, at a hearing on export controls.

My read: senators now have a specific rack and a specific address to ask about. The key question is the route, from Gigabyte's original buyer to a loan filing in Shanghai.

### Third story: Belgium halts a Google data center

Third, a reminder that water, not just chips, decides where AI capacity gets built.

On Thursday, the regional government of Wallonia, in southern Belgium, ordered Google to stop building its data center near Charleroi. About three hundred workers were on site.

Here's the history, per The Next Web. Google's original permit covered three air-cooled data centers. Last year it changed the plan, merging buildings and cooling them with water from a nearby river. The regional minister said that meant about six hundred cubic meters of river water an hour, and Belgian news agency Belga reports that less than a quarter would go back. That permit was approved in April. After residents appealed, the regional minister revoked it in August. Google appealed to Belgium's Council of State, and kept building while it waited. A hearing is set for January seventh.

Google says it's committed to open dialogue with the regional government. Greenpeace Belgium, which pushed for the halt, said, "Tech giants are not above the law."

Why this matters: a government has physically stopped a hyperscaler's build over cooling water. And building through a revoked permit was a bet on winning the appeal. Other builders in Europe will now be pricing in that risk.

### Quick hit

One quick one. Russia's Sber released GigaChat four as open weights on Hugging Face. The big model has seven hundred and eight billion parameters, about forty-one billion active, a context window of around a million tokens, and an MIT license. The smaller thirty-seven billion model is a different story: its license bans commercial use. And Sber's own table is mixed. On one long-horizon coding test, DeepSWE, it scores about thirty-five, against about seventy-four for a recent DeepSeek model. On SWE-bench Verified, the two are close.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news: AI's hardest constraints are increasingly physical and legal. Who supplies your parts. Where a rack physically ends up. Whether a river can cool your building. None of that is decided by a model's benchmark score.

The one thing I'd watch next is Thursday, December third, when Jensen Huang testifies. If senators come in with that Shanghai rack's serial number, the export-control debate gets very specific, very fast.

That's Daily AI Signal for today. Thanks for listening.
