### 开场

这里是 Daily AI Signal，今天是 11 月 7 日，星期六。

### 头条：AI 公司的身价，在重新定价

先说头条。这一周，全球最大的两家 AI 公司，几乎同时被市场压了价。

据彭博社报道，OpenAI 已经完成新一轮融资的第一笔，金额 110 亿美元，估值 1.25 万亿美元。路透社转发了这条消息。按彭博的说法，这一轮总共可能到 200 亿美元左右，剩下的钱预计在未来几周到位。

对比一下就知道变化在哪。OpenAI 原本的目标，是按 1.4 万亿美元估值，融至少 300 亿美元。现在到手的，钱更少，价格也更低。

再看 Anthropic。雅虎财经援引彭博和华尔街日报的报道说，准备参与 Anthropic 上市的投资人，现在讨论的估值在 1 万亿到 1.3 万亿美元之间。上个月股市大跌之前，还有投资人谈到过 2 万亿美元。上市时间原本普遍预期在 11 月底之前，现在可能推迟。我们没有看到 Anthropic 对此的回应，发行价区间也还没有定。

需要说明的是，这些数字都来自知情人士，最终条款还可能变化。

这件事为什么重要？

第一，这两家公司，是整个 AI 行业估值的参照物。最上面的价格一松动，下面所有正在融资的 AI 创业公司，都会跟着受影响。

第二，钱并没有停。就在纳斯达克大跌的这一周，OpenAI 还是拿到了 110 亿美元。这不是资金断流，而是买方开始有了讲价的底气。

第三，估值会传导到算力。大模型公司签下的长期算力合同，很大程度上靠下一轮融资来支撑。融资变小，上市估值变低，提前锁定算力的空间也就小了一些。

我的判断是，这是一次重新定价，不是泡沫破裂。1 万亿美元，对一家还没上市的公司来说，依然是非常高的数字。变化在于，过去一年是 AI 公司自己开价，这一周，轮到买方说了算。

接下来最值得看的，是 Anthropic 最终给出的发行价区间，会不会落在 1 万亿到 1.3 万亿美元之间，以及能不能在这个月上市。

### 次要新闻

第二条，来自数学和理论计算机领域。

一家叫 Physical Superintelligence 的创业公司，简称 PSI，宣布证明了 Aaronson-Ambainis 猜想。这个猜想是 2008 年提出的，是量子计算复杂性理论里一个核心的未解问题。

简单说，量子计算机在有结构的问题上，比如分解大数，可以比普通计算机快得多。但如果输入是随机的、没有结构的，它还能不能快出指数级？这个猜想认为不能，最多快出多项式级。PSI 的证明支持了这个结论，范围是所谓的查询模型，也就是只看算法读取输入的次数。

这条新闻和 AI 的关系在于，PSI 说，证明来自它内部的研究系统，用的是 Anthropic 一个没有公开的模型。系统运行到第三天，就给出了完整证明，之后又把结果改进了两次。整个证明通过了 Lean 的检查。Lean 是一种证明助手，由计算机逐步核对每一个推理。

真正值得关注的，是这套流程。模型负责生成，机器负责验证，人负责选题和把关。在有形式化验证工具的领域，我们不必只相信模型自己说的话。

当然，局限也要讲清楚。这个结果限制的是查询次数，不是总计算时间，所以不能说明量子计算机没用。它覆盖的是大多数输入，不是全部。论文还没有经过同行评审，我们也没有看到 Aaronson、Ambainis 或者 Anthropic 的回应。关于 AI 起了什么作用，目前都是 PSI 自己的说法。

第三条，同样是 AI 做研究，这次是密码学。

据多家科技媒体报道，Google DeepMind 宣布，AI 智能体在人的监督下，和密码学家 Nadia Heninger、Emmanuel Thomé 合作，找到了针对 1024 位 RSA 的两项新攻击方法。1024 位 RSA，是已经被淘汰、但还有不少旧系统在用的加密方式。

第一项和数字签名有关。报道说，智能体找到的新算法，在理论上把已知最好的伪造签名攻击，效率提高了大约 1600 倍。这只是理论估算。在沙箱测试里，它复现了学术界已经完成的一个结果，所用的计算时间，只有原来的三点五分之一左右。

第二项是批量分解。如果同时攻击很多把密钥，新方法能把每多破解一把的成本，降低大约 30 倍。

DeepMind 方面表示，这些结果只针对 1024 位 RSA，2048 位这样的现代加密不受影响。

我的判断是，1600 倍这个数字是理论值，更实际的信号是，攻击旧加密的成本一直在下降，现在 AI 正在加快这个过程。如果你的系统里，还有 1024 位的 RSA 密钥，这是一个尽快升级的理由。

需要说明，我们没有打开 DeepMind 的原文或者论文，这些细节来自媒体报道，几家报道的说法一致。具体用了哪些模型，目前还不清楚。

第四条，是 AI 智能体被用来干坏事。

安全公司 Check Point 发布报告，研究了一个叫 ARTEX 的开源 AI 渗透测试平台。它用大模型协调多个智能体，自己规划攻击，自己调用工具，自己记录证据。现在，它被一批以赚钱为目的的网络犯罪分子拿去用了。

几个数字很说明问题。在一次大约两周的行动里，智能体攻击了 10 家灰色市场的苹果应用签名店，拿到了 300 多个代码签名证书，其中 3 个仍然有效，可以转卖。Check Point 估计，整场行动的成本只有 700 到 1500 美元。另一次针对游戏账号服务的行动，找到了 19 万多组账号密码，其中大约 6000 个账号还能登录，成本只有几百美元。

操作的人，基本只是偶尔看看进度。大部分人用的是中国的大模型，最常见的是 DeepSeek 的 Flash 系列。

真正值得警惕的，是这些智能体利用的漏洞都很基础，比如写死在代码里的密码，没做身份验证的接口，权限控制有缺陷。我的判断是，防守方能规模化做的，就是把这些最基础的漏洞先补上，因为便宜的智能体，最先找到的就是它们。

### 快讯

接下来是几条快讯。

英国央行给 AI 泼了点冷水。据英国科技媒体 The Register 报道，英国央行在 11 月的货币政策报告里估计，过去三年，AI 只让英国的生产率提高了大约百分之零点五。企业反映，到目前为止，AI 对就业几乎没有整体影响。不过企业预期，未来三年，AI 每年能给生产率增长贡献将近 1 个百分点。

谷歌宣布，在美国怀俄明州投资 170 亿美元建设数据中心园区。主要电力来自一个 702 兆瓦的燃料电池项目，燃料是天然气，并配套碳捕集和地下封存。园区采用风冷，谷歌说会自己承担全部电费。就在谷歌宣布的两天前，美国多地选民刚刚投票反对数据中心，谷歌这次的思路很清楚，自带电源，不碰当地的水。

路透社报道，大约 60 万块英伟达 H200 芯片，正堆在香港，等待中国批准大规模进口。此前有媒体报道，最终获批的总量可能不到 20 万块，北京也希望企业在推理上优先使用国产芯片。英伟达没有回应。

最后是一条后续。Meta 无限期暂停了原定 11 月 20 日生效的新商业条款。这些条款会允许 Meta 抓取企业用户的网站内容。此前，挪威和巴西的法院都发出了禁令。Meta 表示，要花时间把这件事做对。

### 结语

把今天的新闻放在一起看，有一个共同点，AI 正在被各方重新检验。投资人在检验价格，Lean 在检验证明，密码学家在检验智能体的算法，央行在检验生产率的故事。

接下来最值得关注的，是 Anthropic 的发行价区间。这个数字，会决定今年剩下的时间里，AI 公司值多少钱。

### Main story: the AI price tag gets reset

Let's start with the money, because it moved this week.

Yesterday, Bloomberg reported that OpenAI has closed on eleven billion dollars. That's the first part of a round at a valuation of one point two five trillion dollars. Reuters carried the report. According to Bloomberg, the full round could reach about twenty billion, with the rest expected over the coming weeks. The first checks came from Coatue and T. Rowe Price. SoftBank, Sequoia, Founders Fund, Andreessen Horowitz and investors from the UAE are also in the wider round.

Here's what changed. OpenAI had been aiming for at least thirty billion dollars at a one point four trillion dollar valuation. We covered those talks over the last two weeks. What actually closed is smaller, and priced lower.

Now Anthropic. Yahoo Finance, citing Bloomberg and the Wall Street Journal, reports that investors looking at Anthropic's IPO are now talking about a valuation between one trillion and one point three trillion dollars. Last month, before the selloff, some buyers were talking about as much as two trillion. The listing was widely expected before the end of November. It could slip. On prediction markets, the odds of a listing before December first fell from roughly eighty percent to about fifty. We haven't seen a comment from Anthropic, and no price range has been set.

All of this is reporting based on people familiar with the talks. Final terms can still change.

So why does this matter?

First, it's the same buyers, the same week, the same direction. These two companies are the reference points for every AI valuation below them. When the top of the market reprices by a few hundred billion dollars, every founder raising money next quarter feels it.

Second, the money did not stop. OpenAI wired eleven billion dollars in during a week when the Nasdaq had one of its worst days in a long time. That's not a funding freeze. That's buyers getting more say on price.

Third, valuations feed compute. These labs sign multi-year, multi-billion dollar compute deals on the expectation of future raises. A smaller round, or a cheaper IPO, means a little less room to pre-commit. That shows up later in cloud backlogs and chip orders.

What most headlines are missing is that this is a reset, not a collapse. One trillion dollars is still an extraordinary number for a company that isn't public yet. The question is just who sets the price. For most of this year it was the labs. This week, it's the buyers.

The catch is that we're reading the market through anonymous sources. There are figures floating around, like a specific listing date, or a one point one trillion dollar mark, that I couldn't confirm. So I'm leaving them out.

The one thing to watch: whether Anthropic sets a price range inside that one to one point three trillion band, and whether it lists this month. If it slips into December, expect private rounds across AI to reprice too.

### Secondary: an AI system and an eighteen-year-old conjecture

Now a different kind of AI news.

A startup called Physical Superintelligence, or PSI, says it has proved the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture. Scott Aaronson and Andris Ambainis posed it in two thousand eight. It's a central open question in quantum complexity theory.

In plain terms, it asks this: quantum computers get huge speedups on problems with structure. Factoring is the famous example. But on random inputs with no structure, can they still be exponentially faster? The conjecture says no, at most polynomially faster. PSI's proof says the conjecture is right, in what's called the query model, which counts how many times an algorithm looks at its input.

Here's the part that makes it news for us. PSI says the proof came from its internal research system, running instances of a non-public Anthropic model. It had a complete proof on the third day of the run, then improved the result twice. The proof was checked by Lean, a proof assistant where a computer verifies every step. A PSI researcher, David Turturean, scoped the problem, ran the system, reviewed the paper, and says he's responsible for its correctness. PSI says Stanford researchers who proved a narrower case in August are reading it and writing a shorter explanation.

This matters because of the workflow. A model generates, a machine checker verifies, and humans scope the problem and audit the result. In fields with a formal checker, you don't have to take the model's word for it.

The limits are real. The result limits queries, not total running time, so it doesn't say quantum computers are useless. It covers most inputs, not all. It hasn't been peer reviewed. We haven't seen comments from Aaronson or Ambainis, or from Anthropic. And the claims about the AI's role are PSI's own.

### Secondary: DeepMind's agents and legacy RSA

Same day, a second lab, a different field.

Google DeepMind says AI agents, working under human supervision alongside cryptographers Nadia Heninger of UC San Diego and Emmanuel Thomé of Inria, found two new attacks on legacy one-thousand-twenty-four-bit RSA. The work was led by DeepMind's Dj Dvijotham and Abhradeep Thakurta.

The first result is about signatures. According to coverage of the announcement, the agents found an algorithm that, in theory, improves the best known forgery attack on these keys by about sixteen hundred times. That's a theoretical estimate. In a sandbox test, their implementation reproduced an earlier academic result on an already-solved benchmark key using three and a half times less CPU time.

The second result is batch factoring. If you're attacking many keys at once, the new method cuts the cost of each additional key by about thirty times compared with previous batch techniques.

According to the coverage, DeepMind says the results apply only to legacy thousand-twenty-four-bit RSA, so modern keys of two thousand forty-eight bits or more aren't affected.

Here's my read. The headline number is theoretical. The practical signal is that attacks on old cryptography keep getting cheaper, and AI agents are now speeding that up. If your systems still use thousand-twenty-four-bit RSA, in old hardware, old certificates, or embedded devices, this is a good reason to finish that migration.

One caveat: I couldn't open DeepMind's own post or paper. These details come from news coverage of the announcement, and the outlets agree with each other. We don't know which models the agents used.

### Secondary: hacking on autopilot

Now the less pleasant side of agents.

Check Point Research published a report on ARTEX, an open-source AI penetration-testing platform from China. It uses language models to coordinate agents that plan attacks, run tools and log evidence. We covered Microsoft's report on a state-aligned group using it. Check Point's report is about ordinary criminals.

The numbers are the story. In one campaign, over about two weeks in October, agents went after ten gray-market iPhone app-signing shops and pulled more than three hundred code-signing certificates with passwords. Three were still valid and sellable. Check Point estimates the whole campaign cost seven hundred to fifteen hundred dollars. Another campaign against game account services recovered over a hundred and ninety thousand credential sets; about six thousand accounts were still valid, for an estimated few hundred dollars in model costs.

The operators barely touched it. Mostly they checked progress. Most used Chinese models, especially DeepSeek's Flash family. In one case, Check Point says, an operator's attempt with Anthropic's Opus 5.5 ran into guardrails, and the operator switched to Chinese models.

This matters because the bugs the agents exploited were basic. Hardcoded passwords. Missing authentication. Broken access controls. My interpretation: guardrails on one model shift attackers to another. The defense that actually scales is fixing the boring bugs, because that's exactly what cheap agents find first.

### Quick hits

The Bank of England has a reality check. According to The Register's reading of the Bank's November Monetary Policy Report, AI has raised UK productivity by only about half a percent over three years. Firms report almost no effect on jobs so far. But they expect AI to add almost a full percentage point a year to productivity growth over the next three years.

Google announced a seventeen billion dollar data center campus in Laramie County, Wyoming. It's powered largely by a seven-hundred-two megawatt fuel cell project running on natural gas, with carbon capture and underground storage. It's air-cooled, and Google says it will pay for all of its own power. Coming two days after voters pushed back on data centers, the pitch is clear: bring your own power, don't touch the water.

Reuters reports that about six hundred thousand Nvidia H-two-hundred chips are sitting in Hong Kong, waiting for Beijing to approve large-scale imports. An earlier report from The Information said total approvals could come to fewer than two hundred thousand, and that Beijing wanted companies to favor Chinese chips for inference. Nvidia didn't comment.

And a follow-up. Meta has indefinitely paused new business terms that would have let it scrape the websites of its business account users. Courts in Norway and Brazil had blocked them. Meta says it's taking its time to get this right.

### Final take

The bigger trend behind today's news is that AI is being checked from every side. Investors are checking the price. Lean is checking a proof. Cryptographers are checking an agent's math. Central bankers are checking the productivity story. The labs still lead, but this week the checkers set the terms.

The one thing I'd watch next is Anthropic's price range. That number will tell us where AI is priced for the rest of the year.
